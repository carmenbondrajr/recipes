# 💍 Marry Me Chicken

*One skillet, ~30 minutes, serves 4. Blistered grape tomatoes and bloomed tomato paste instead of sun-dried — same depth, no chew.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Grape tomatoes (1 pint / ~10 oz) — *grape, not cherry: meatier walls, less water in the sauce*
- Fresh basil (small bunch)
- Lemon
- Garlic (1 head, or use the jar)

**Meat**
- Chicken cutlets (4 pieces / ~1.5 lb) — *or 2 large breasts halved horizontally and pounded even*

**Dairy**
- Heavy cream (1 cup / 8 oz) — **heavy cream specifically, not half-and-half**
- Parmesan, block (~2 oz / ½ cup grated)
- Butter

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Tomato paste (small can or tube — *tube is better, you only need 2 tbsp*)
- Chicken broth (1 cup / 8 oz — smallest carton or a bouillon cube)

*You should have at home: olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder, Italian seasoning, red pepper flakes*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**
- 4 chicken cutlets (~1.5 lb), patted very dry
- 10 oz grape tomatoes, left whole
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 tsp minced garlic
- ½ cup chicken broth
- 1 cup heavy cream
- ½ cup grated parmesan
- 2 tbsp olive oil + 1 tbsp butter
- ½ tsp red pepper flakes
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- Salt, pepper, garlic powder
- Handful fresh basil, torn
- Lemon, for finishing

**Steps**

1. **Season and sear the chicken** (~8 min). Pat the cutlets bone dry — moisture is the only thing standing between you and a good crust. Season both sides with salt, pepper, garlic powder, and Italian seasoning. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high until it shimmers, then lay the cutlets in without crowding (two batches if needed). **Leave them alone** — they'll stick at first and release on their own when they've got color, ~4 min. Flip once, another 3–4 min. Pull them to a plate at 160°F; they'll finish in the sauce. Don't wipe the pan.

2. **Blister the tomatoes** (~5 min). Tomatoes go in whole, straight into the chicken fat, still over medium-high. Let them sit mostly undisturbed until the skins blacken in spots and split, pressing a few with the back of a spoon to encourage the rest. They'll spit — keep a lid handy as a shield. This step is the whole point: the dry heat drives off water and concentrates their sugar. Add them later and they just stew into something watery and sad.

3. **Bloom the paste** (~2 min). Drop heat to medium. Add the butter, garlic, red pepper flakes, and tomato paste. Stir constantly for 1–2 minutes until the paste darkens from bright red to brick and smells roasty rather than tinny. Raw tomato paste tastes metallic — this is the step that fixes it, and it's where the sun-dried tomato depth comes from.

4. **Deglaze** (~2 min). Pour in the broth and scrape every brown bit off the bottom with a wooden spoon. Let it bubble down by about half.

5. **Cream and cheese** (~3 min). Turn the heat to low. Stir in the cream, then take the pan off the burner and add the parmesan, stirring until smooth. **Off-heat is not optional** — parm added to a boiling sauce breaks and goes grainy. Taste and salt.

6. **Finish the chicken** (~5 min). Return the cutlets and any juices to the pan, spoon sauce over them, and simmer gently until they hit 165°F. Keep it at a bare simmer — a hard boil now risks breaking the sauce.

7. **Rest 5 minutes, then finish bright.** Tear basil over the top and add a small squeeze of lemon. The sauce looks thin coming off the heat and tightens noticeably as it sits, so don't chase thickness on the stove — you'll end up with paste.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: a broken sauce.** Acid plus dairy is the risk, and the guardrails are heavy cream only (36% fat is stable enough to take the tomato acid — half-and-half or milk will curdle) and no hard boil once the cream is in. If it does break, pull it off the heat and whisk in a splash of cold cream.
- **Water math.** The bursting tomatoes release liquid, which is why the broth is only ½ cup. If it still ends up loose, simmer the sauce *before* the chicken goes back in — it's much easier to reduce then.
- **Leave the tomatoes whole.** They collapse more dramatically than halved ones and you get a burst of hot sweet juice against the cream. Halved ones give up their water early and go limp.
- **Want it thicker?** Dredge the seasoned cutlets in flour before searing. The fond carries into the sauce and thickens it on its own. Skipped in the main method to keep it a one-bowl-fewer job.
- **Grape vs. cherry:** grape are sweeter and hold up better. Cherry work fine, just cut the broth to ⅓ cup — they're juicier.
- **The name is marketing.** Delish coined it in 2016 on the premise that it's good enough to prompt a proposal. Underneath it's a Tuscan-style cream chicken, which is itself an American invention — you won't find it in Tuscany.

## What to Serve With It

The sauce is the point, so the job of a side is to **catch it** and to **cut it**. Pick one from each.

**To catch it**
- **Crusty bread** — the lowest-effort, highest-payoff option. Nothing to time.
- **Angel hair or linguine** — thin pasta suits a cream sauce better than anything ridged or chunky
- **Mashed potatoes** — the most decadent direction, if you're going all in
- *Skip rice and orzo here — they soak the sauce up and disappear into it instead of carrying it*

**To cut it**
- **Arugula salad, lemon vinaigrette** — the best pairing by a wide margin. Peppery and sharp against all that cream, and it takes 3 minutes.
- **Roasted asparagus or broccolini** — 400°F, 12–15 min, goes in while the chicken sears
- **Garlicky green beans**, blistered in a hot pan

**The easy default:** bread + arugula salad. No timing to coordinate, and the acid does exactly what the dish needs.
