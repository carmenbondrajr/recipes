# ⚾ World Series Nachos
### Grilled Chili-Lime Chicken Sheet Pan Nachos

*Serves 2 as dinner, or 4 as a game-time snack. ~1 hour of hands-on time plus a 30-minute-to-4-hour marinade. Chili-lime chicken thighs charred on the grill and chopped small, layered twice over sturdy chips with Mexican-blend cheese and black beans, baked until bubbly, then piled with fresh pico and a lime crema drizzle. One half sheet pan, eaten straight off it.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Roma tomatoes (4). Romas are less watery than round tomatoes, which matters a lot here: watery pico means soggy chips
- White onion (1 medium). Half goes in the pico
- Jalapeño (1), for the pico. Skip it if you want the heat to come only from the pickled ones
- Cilantro (1 bunch)
- Limes (3). 1 for the marinade, 1 for the pico, 1 for the crema and wedges
- Garlic (1 head; you need 2 cloves)

**Meat**
- **Boneless skinless chicken thighs, ~1¼ lb** (usually 4–5 thighs)
  - **Thighs, not breasts.** Thighs stay juicy at the higher temp it takes to get a good char, and they forgive a couple of extra minutes on the grill. Breasts dry out fast and taste like nothing once they're chopped small

**Dairy**
- **Shredded Mexican-blend cheese, two 8 oz bags** (you'll use about 3 cups / 12 oz of the 4 cups). Any blend works, whether it's "Mexican," "Fiesta," or "Taco." Grab one with Monterey Jack in it if you have a choice, since Jack is the smoothest melter
- Sour cream (one 8 oz tub; you'll use ½ cup)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Tortilla chips, one big bag (~12–13 oz; you'll use about 10 oz).** Thick and sturdy is the whole game. Thin, restaurant-style chips go limp under the cheese and break when you lift them. If the bag says "thick cut" or "cantina," great. If a chip snaps when you press it gently with your thumb, it's too thin
- Black beans (one 15 oz can)
- Pickled jalapeño slices (one jar). Check if you already have some
- Ground cumin, smoked paprika, chili powder, dried oregano. Check you have all four (you bought them for the Grill Night Tacos)

*You should have at home: neutral oil, honey, kosher salt, black pepper, foil or parchment*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The marinade*
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 2 tbsp lime juice (1 lime)
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp chili powder
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp dried oregano
- 1 tsp honey
- 1¼ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper

*The pico*
- 4 Roma tomatoes, diced
- ½ white onion, finely diced (about ⅔ cup)
- 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced
- ½ cup chopped cilantro
- Juice of 1 lime
- ½ tsp kosher salt

*The lime crema*
- ½ cup sour cream
- 1 tbsp lime juice
- Pinch of kosher salt
- 1–2 tsp water

*Everything else*
- ~1¼ lb boneless skinless chicken thighs
- ~10 oz sturdy tortilla chips
- 3 cups (12 oz) shredded Mexican-blend cheese
- 1 can (15 oz) black beans
- ¼ cup pickled jalapeño slices
- Extra cilantro and lime wedges for serving

---

### 1️⃣ Marinate the chicken · ⏱ 5 min, plus 30 min–4 hours
- Whisk the marinade together in a bowl:
  - **2 tbsp neutral oil**
  - **2 tbsp lime juice**
  - **2 minced garlic cloves**
  - **1 tsp chili powder**
  - **1 tsp cumin**
  - **1 tsp smoked paprika**
  - **½ tsp dried oregano**
  - **1 tsp honey**
  - **1¼ tsp kosher salt**
  - **½ tsp black pepper**
- Put the **chicken thighs** in a zip-top bag, pour in the marinade, squeeze out the air, and massage it in. Refrigerate for **30 minutes to 4 hours**. The salt seasons the meat as it sits, and the honey is there to help the outside caramelize into charred, crispy edges on the grill.
- Even 30 minutes is plenty for tonight. Thighs are sturdier than steak, so the lime won't turn them mushy within that window.

### 2️⃣ Prep everything else while it marinates · ⏱ 20 min
- **Make the pico.** Stir together in a bowl:
  - **4 diced Roma tomatoes**
  - **½ white onion, finely diced**
  - **1 seeded, minced jalapeño**
  - **½ cup chopped cilantro**
  - **Juice of 1 lime**
  - **½ tsp kosher salt**
- Let it sit at least 15 minutes. The salt pulls juice out of the tomatoes and mellows the raw onion. That juice is good for flavor in the bowl, but it's exactly what you don't want on the chips later, so you'll drain it before serving.
- **Measure the cheese.** Dump **3 cups of the shredded cheese** into a bowl and let it sit out on the counter until it's time to build. Room-temperature cheese melts faster and smoother, so it's fully gooey before the chips have a chance to burn.
- **Rinse the beans.** Drain the **black beans**, rinse them in a strainer, then spread them on a kitchen towel and pat them dry. Wet beans steam the chips from underneath.
- **Make the lime crema.** Stir together **½ cup sour cream**, **1 tbsp lime juice**, and a pinch of salt, then add water **1 tsp at a time** until it falls off the spoon in a ribbon. Spoon it into a zip-top bag and keep it in the fridge. Later you'll snip a tiny corner off and drizzle it like a pro.

### 3️⃣ Heat the grill and the oven · ⏱ 10–15 min
- Take the chicken out of the fridge.
- Get the grill to **medium-high, about 425°F** with the lid closed. Leave one side cooler (one burner on low, or no coals under one side) as a safe zone for flare-ups. Thighs drip a lot of fat, and the honey in the marinade burns if it sits over flames.
- Scrape the grates clean and oil them with a paper towel dipped in oil and held in tongs.
- **Preheat the oven to 425°F** now too, so it's ready when the chicken comes off.

### 4️⃣ Grill the chicken · ⏱ 10–14 min
- Lift the thighs out of the bag and let the extra marinade drip off. Scrape off any loose bits of garlic, which burn and turn bitter on the grill.
- Lay them flat on the hot side, smooth side down, and close the lid. Leave them for **5–7 minutes**, until they have dark grill marks and release from the grates on their own. If they stick, they're not ready.
- Flip and cook another **5–7 minutes**.
- ⚠️ **Flare-ups are the main hazard.** If a thigh catches fire, slide it to the cool side until the flames die down, then move it back. A little blackened char on the edges is great. A sooty, bitter crust isn't.
- 🌡️ **Pull at 175°F** in the thickest part. Thighs are safe at 165°F, but they get juicier and more tender closer to 175–185°F as the connective tissue melts, so don't pull them early.

### 5️⃣ Rest and chop the chicken · ⏱ 10 min
- Rest the thighs on a cutting board for **5 minutes**, so the juices settle in instead of running out the second you cut.
- Chop them into **small, ½-inch pieces**. Chopped beats shredded on nachos: every piece has a charred edge, and small pieces spread evenly so every chip gets some instead of one chip carrying a whole thigh.
- Scrape the chicken and any juices on the board into a bowl, squeeze over a little lime, and toss. The juices keep the chicken moist in the oven.

### 6️⃣ Build in two layers · ⏱ 5 min
- Line a **half sheet pan** with foil or parchment. Cleanup is the last thing anyone wants to do after the 9th inning.
- **Layer 1:** Spread **half the chips** in a layer, overlapping a little like shingles, but not piled up. Scatter over:
  - **⅓ of the cheese**
  - **half the chicken**
  - **half the black beans**
  - **half the pickled jalapeños**
  - another **⅙ of the cheese** on top, to glue it all down
- **Layer 2:** Lay the **rest of the chips** over that, then repeat with the rest of the chicken, beans, and jalapeños, and finish with **all the remaining cheese**.
- ⚠️ **This is the step that makes or breaks it.** Dump everything on top of one tall pile of chips and you end up with a cheesy lid over a pile of naked chips. Two thin layers mean every chip gets something. The cheese on top of each layer melts over the chicken and beans and holds them on the chips, so they don't fall off when you pick one up.

### 7️⃣ Bake · ⏱ 6–10 min
- Bake at **425°F for 6–10 minutes**, until the cheese is completely melted and bubbling and the chip edges are just turning golden.
- ⚠️ **Watch the edges, not the clock.** Chips go from toasty to scorched quickly, and the corners of the pan brown first. Pull them as soon as all the cheese has melted, even if it's only been 6 minutes.

### 8️⃣ Top it and eat it · ⏱ 2 min
- Spoon the **pico** over the top with a **slotted spoon**, shaking off the juice as you go.
- ⚠️ **Don't pour it on from the bowl.** That tomato juice settles at the bottom and soaks into the chips in about 3 minutes. Save the juice. It's good spooned over individual bites if you want it.
- Snip a tiny corner off the crema bag and drizzle it across everything, then scatter on extra **cilantro**.
- Serve right on the sheet pan with **lime wedges**. Nachos are at their best in the first 15 minutes, so get them in front of the TV fast.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: soggy chips.** Every source of moisture is the enemy: pico juice, wet beans, thin chips, and toppings that get baked when they should go on cold. That's why the cold toppings (pico and crema) only go on after the oven, and why the pico goes on with a slotted spoon.
- **What else ruins it: dry, flavorless chicken.** Use thighs, use the marinade, and pull at 175°F. Plain grilled breast chopped into cubes just disappears under the cheese.
- **Bake it on the grill instead?** Once the chicken comes off, set the sheet pan on the **cool side** of the grill, close the lid, and give it **8–12 minutes** at around 400–450°F. It picks up a little smoke. Check the bottoms halfway, because direct heat under the pan burns chips fast.
- **Want more heat?** Leave the seeds in the jalapeño, or mince 1 chipotle in adobo into the marinade for smoky heat. A few dashes of hot sauce in the crema works too.
- **Want queso instead of shredded cheese?** That's ballpark-style (see below), and it's a different recipe. The shredded double layer here is the oven-baked kind.
- **Leftover chicken** is great in tacos or a quesadilla the next day. The chips won't keep, so build only as many nachos as you'll eat.
- **The original nacho, 1943:** in Piedras Negras, Mexico, just across the border from Eagle Pass, Texas, a group of U.S. Army wives from nearby Fort Duncan came into the Victory Club after the kitchen had closed. Maître d' **Ignacio "Nacho" Anaya** threw together what he had: fried tortilla triangles, melted cheese, and pickled jalapeño slices. He called them *Nacho's especiales*. Piedras Negras still holds a Nacho Festival every October 21st.
- **Ballpark nachos, 1976:** the stadium version came from **Frank Liberto**, a concessions businessman who sold them at the Texas Rangers' Arlington Stadium. His big idea was a pumpable cheese sauce that didn't need refrigeration, so nachos could be made in seconds instead of baked to order. They really took off in 1977 when Howard Cosell kept talking about them on *Monday Night Football*, so the snack went baseball first, then football.
