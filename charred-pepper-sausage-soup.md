# 🔥 Stovetop Charred Pepper & Hot Sausage Soup

*Serves 6 — Dutch oven, ~55 min. The layered-flavor version of stuffed pepper soup.*

Same idea as the Instant Pot version, built the slow way: every component browns on its own
before anything wet goes in. The peppers get blistered and added at the very end so they still
taste like peppers instead of dissolving into sweet mush.

---

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- 3 bell peppers — grab a mix of colors (red/orange are sweeter, green is grassier; a mix is best)
- 1 yellow onion
- 3 celery stalks
- 1 head garlic
- Flat-leaf parsley, small bunch

**Meat**
- 1 lb **hot** Italian sausage — bulk if the case has it, otherwise links and you squeeze the
  casings off
  - *Sweet Italian works if you don't want heat; you'll want a pinch of red pepper flakes to
    compensate*

**Dairy**
- Parmesan — **ask the cheese counter for a rind**, they're cheap and often free. A wedge works
  too, you just cut the rind off.

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Tomato paste, small can (you'll use 2 tbsp)
- 1 can (15 oz) **fire-roasted** diced tomatoes — worth seeking out here, it doubles down on the char
- 1 can (15 oz) tomato sauce
- Chicken broth (32 oz carton)
- Jasmine rice (you'll use 1 cup dry)
- Red wine vinegar (if you don't have it)

*You should have at home: olive oil, butter, Italian seasoning, smoked paprika, bay leaf, salt,
black pepper*

## Recipe

### Ingredients
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 3 bell peppers, cut into 1" squares
- 1 lb hot Italian sausage, casings removed
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 3 celery stalks, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 can (15 oz) fire-roasted diced tomatoes
- 1 can (15 oz) tomato sauce
- 4 cups (32 oz) chicken broth
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 parmesan rind
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 cup jasmine rice, cooked separately
- Parsley + grated parmesan, to serve

---

### 1. Char the Peppers — 8 min
Heat **2 tbsp olive oil** in the Dutch oven over medium-high until it shimmers. Add the
**3 peppers, cut in 1" squares**, in a single layer — **work in two batches if they don't all
fit flat**. Let them sit **undisturbed 3-4 min** until the undersides have black-brown blisters,
then toss once and give them 2 more. Scrape out to a bowl and set aside.

**The failure mode is crowding.** Piled-up peppers trap their own steam and you get soft, pale,
sweaty peppers instead of char. If you hear sizzling, you're searing; if you see the pan go
cloudy and quiet, they're steaming — pull half out and do it in two rounds.

They should look a little *too* dark and a little *too* firm. They finish cooking in the soup.

### 2. Brown the Sausage — 8 min
Same pot, no need to wipe. Add the **1 lb hot sausage** in chunks and press flat. **Leave it
alone 4-5 min** before touching it, then break it into big craggy pieces — chunks, not fine
crumbles. More surface area means more crust, and you want to bite into actual pieces of sausage.

Scoop the sausage out with a slotted spoon. Leave about **2 tbsp of the rendered fat** behind and
pour off the rest if it's swimming. That fat is seasoned with fennel and chili — it's the cooking
medium for everything that follows.

The brown stuck to the bottom is **fond**. Don't scrub it, don't panic. It comes up in the next step.

### 3. Sweat the Aromatics & Deglaze — 8 min
Drop to medium. Add the **diced onion** and **3 diced celery stalks**, season with a pinch of
salt, cook ~6 min until softened. As they release water, scrape the bottom with a wooden spoon —
the fond dissolves right into the vegetables. That's deglazing, and it's where most of the
soup's depth comes from.

Add the **4 cloves garlic**, cook 1 min.

### 4. Caramelize the Paste & Bloom the Spices — 3 min
Push the vegetables to the sides. Add **2 tbsp tomato paste** to the bare center and cook it
2 minutes, stirring, until it darkens from bright red to brick. Raw paste tastes sour and tinny;
cooked paste turns sweet and deep.

Stir in the **1 tsp Italian seasoning** and **1 tsp smoked paprika** and cook **30 seconds** in
the fat before any liquid goes in. Those compounds are fat-soluble — bloomed in oil they carry
through the whole pot, dumped into broth they mostly just float.

### 5. Build & Simmer — 25 min
Return the sausage and any juices. Add the **fire-roasted diced tomatoes**, **tomato sauce**,
**4 cups chicken broth**, **1 bay leaf**, and the **parmesan rind**.

Bring to a boil, then drop to a gentle simmer, lid slightly ajar. **Simmer 25 min.**

The rind is doing quiet work down there — it slowly releases glutamates and makes a 25-minute
soup taste like a 2-hour one. Fish it out at the end; it'll be soft and a little gluey, which is
normal.

### 6. Cook the Rice — meanwhile
Cook **1 cup jasmine rice** separately per the package while the soup simmers.

Separate is deliberate: rice simmered in the soup keeps drinking broth, and by tomorrow you have
porridge. In the pot it's a soup; on the side it's a soup forever.

### 7. Add the Peppers Back — 5 min
Stir the **charred peppers** in for the last 5 minutes of the simmer. Just long enough to warm
through and trade some flavor with the broth, not long enough to undo the char.

### 8. Finish — 2 min
Pull out the bay leaf and parmesan rind. Off the heat, stir in **1 tbsp red wine vinegar** and
**1 tbsp butter**. Taste for salt.

Don't skip this. Tomato soups go flat and heavy after a long simmer — the vinegar reads as
*brighter*, not sour, and the butter rounds the edges. If it tastes vaguely like something is
missing, it's almost always acid.

### 9. Serve
Scoop rice into each bowl, ladle soup over. Chopped parsley and grated parmesan on top.

## Tips & Notes

- **Too rich?** Hot sausage renders a lot of fat. Next time do **½ lb sausage + ½ lb ground
  turkey** — you keep the fennel-and-chili backbone with a lighter body. Add 1 tbsp olive oil
  when browning, since turkey brings none of its own.
- **Deglaze with wine** for another layer: after the tomato paste, add **½ cup dry white wine**
  and scrape hard until it's nearly cooked away, then continue. Optional, but it's a good skill
  to have in your hands.
- **No parmesan rind?** A tablespoon of grated parmesan stirred in at the end gets you partway.
  Buy rinds when you see them — they freeze indefinitely and improve every tomato soup and
  minestrone you'll ever make.
- **Fire-roasted tomatoes over regular** — they're the same price and already carry char.
- **Pepper colors:** red and orange are ripe and sweet, green is younger and grassier. All green
  makes the soup taste sharper; all red makes it candy-sweet. A mix splits the difference.
- **Leftovers:** soup keeps 4 days, rice separately. It thickens as it sits — loosen with a
  splash of broth, and re-brighten with a few drops of vinegar since acid fades in the fridge.
- **Freezes well** without the rice. 3 months.
- **In a hurry?** The dump-and-go Instant Pot version is
  [here](instant-pot-stuffed-pepper-soup.md) — different tool, different night.
