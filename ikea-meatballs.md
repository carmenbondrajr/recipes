# 🇸🇪 IKEA Meatballs
### Swedish Meatballs with Mashed Potatoes
*The IKEA plate at home, built on IKEA's own 2020 recipe: tender beef-and-pork meatballs in a silky soy-and-Dijon cream gravy over mashed potatoes. 4 servings (~40 small meatballs), ~1 hour*

---

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Yellow onion (1 small)
- Garlic (1 clove)

**Meat**
- Ground beef, 85/15 (1 lb)
- Ground pork (½ lb). *IKEA's recipe and every copycat use this 2:1 beef-to-pork blend, because the pork makes them softer and sweeter. **All-beef pivot:** buy 1½ lb beef and skip the pork. They'll be a little firmer but still great.*

**Dairy**
- Premade mashed potatoes (about 2 lb / two 24 oz trays, enough for 4). *Microwaveable ones in the refrigerated case near the deli or packaged sides. Look for a short ingredient list (potatoes, butter, milk, cream, salt).*
- Unsalted butter (5 tbsp)
- Heavy cream (⅔ cup, one half-pint). *Have half-and-half at home? You can skip this and use half-and-half in the gravy too. The flour in the roux keeps it from curdling. It'll be a little less rich but still creamy.*
- Eggs (1)

**Frozen**
- Frozen peas (2 cups, about a 12 oz bag). *Check your freezer first!*

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Low-sodium beef broth (⅔ cup)
- Low-sodium vegetable broth (⅔ cup). *IKEA splits the broth half beef, half vegetable, which gives a softer, creamier gravy that isn't as heavily beefy. **Easy pivot:** skip the veg broth and use 1⅓ cups beef broth. Get low-sodium either way because the soy sauce adds plenty of salt.*
- Panko breadcrumbs (½ cup)
- Soy sauce
- Dijon mustard
- *Optional:* lingonberry jam, the classic IKEA side. It's near the jams or in the international aisle. Whole-berry cranberry sauce is an easy swap.

*You should have at home: half-and-half (¼ cup, or milk), kosher salt, black pepper, all-purpose flour, neutral oil*

---

## Recipe (4 servings)

**Ingredients**

*Meatballs*
- 1 lb ground beef + ½ lb ground pork (or 1½ lb beef)
- ½ cup panko
- ¼ cup half-and-half (or whole milk)
- 1 egg
- ½ small yellow onion, in chunks
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp butter + 1 tbsp neutral oil, for searing

*Gravy*
- 3 tbsp butter
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- ⅔ cup low-sodium beef broth + ⅔ cup vegetable broth (or 1⅓ cups beef)
- 2 tsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- ⅔ cup heavy cream (or half-and-half)
- Black pepper to taste

*To serve*
- Premade mashed potatoes (~2 lb)
- 2 cups frozen peas

**Steps**

1. **Make the panade (5 min).** In a big bowl, stir the **½ cup panko** into the **¼ cup half-and-half** and let it sit while you knead the meat. The soaked crumbs keep the meatballs tender. Keep it at ¼ cup, because the pulsed onion adds a lot of moisture too. More liquid than this makes a sticky mix and meatballs that fall apart.

2. **Knead the meat until tacky (3 min).** In a second bowl, add the **1 lb beef + ½ lb pork** and the **1 tsp salt** and knead with your hands for 1-2 minutes, until it turns sticky and stringy and pulls away in strands. **This is the step that makes them "tight."** Salt plus kneading draws out the meat proteins, which bind everything together into the springy, sausage-like bite IKEA meatballs have. This is the opposite of Italian meatballs, where you mix gently to keep them loose and crumbly.

3. **Mix it all together (3 min).** Pulse **½ small onion** (in chunks) and the **1 garlic clove** on **Chop** in the mini food processor, scraping down once, until it's a wet pulp, then scrape it into the panade. The pulp melts into the meat, so you don't get crunchy bits of onion. Add the **1 egg** and **½ tsp pepper**, stir, then add the kneaded meat and mix until evenly combined. That's all the seasoning, which matches IKEA's 2020 recipe. **Check the texture:** it should be soft and a bit tacky but should hold a shape when you roll a test ball. If the test ball slumps, see Tips.

4. **Roll small and chill (15-20 min).** Wet your hands and roll **1-inch balls** (measure out **2 level teaspoons** for the first few so you calibrate your eye, ~40 total) onto a plate or sheet pan. **Keep them small.** IKEA's meatballs are bite-sized, and it's a big part of the vibe: more browned surface, more gravy in every bite, and they cook through fast. If they start looking golf-ball-sized, you've drifted, so compare against the first few. Chill them at least 15 min. The fat firms up and they hold together much better in the pan, so don't skip this.

5. **Sear the meatballs (10 min).** Heat **2 tbsp butter + 1 tbsp oil** in a large skillet over medium-high. Sear the meatballs in 2-3 batches without crowding, rolling them around the pan until browned on most sides, 3-4 min per batch. Shaking the pan works better than flipping 40 meatballs one at a time. They don't need to be cooked through yet because they finish in the gravy. Set them aside on a plate and **don't clean the pan.** The brown bits stuck to it are the flavor base of the gravy.

6. **Make the roux (2-3 min).** Lower the heat to medium. Melt the **3 tbsp butter** in the same skillet, then whisk in the **3 tbsp flour**. Keep whisking for 2 minutes until it smells toasty and turns peanut-butter tan. Cooking the flour gets rid of the raw pasty taste and adds the nutty depth that makes this gravy taste like gravy, not white sauce.

7. **Build the gravy (5 min).** Slowly pour in the **1⅓ cups broth** while whisking constantly, scraping up the brown bits as you go. Adding it gradually is how you avoid lumps. If some show up anyway, keep whisking and they'll smooth out as it simmers. Whisk in the **2 tsp soy sauce** and **1 tsp Dijon** and simmer until it coats a spoon, 2-3 min. Soy and Dijon are IKEA's official secret. They're an odd pair for Swedish food, but the soy adds savory depth and a tan color, and the mustard adds a little tang that keeps the cream from tasting flat.

8. **Add the cream and finish the meatballs (6-8 min).** Stir in the **⅔ cup heavy cream**, return the meatballs and any juices from the plate, and simmer gently, turning them now and then, until cooked through, about 6-8 min since they're small (160°F in the center, or no pink when you cut one open). Keep it at a lazy bubble, not a hard boil. Taste and add pepper. Hold off on salt until you've tasted, because it's usually already there.

9. **Heat the mash and peas (5 min).** While the meatballs finish in the gravy, microwave the **premade mash** per the package directions, stirring halfway so there are no cold spots in the middle. Then microwave the **2 cups frozen peas** in a covered bowl with a splash of water for 2-3 min and add a pat of butter and a pinch of salt. Stop when they're bright green. Past that they turn drab and mushy.

10. **Plate it IKEA-style.** Put a big scoop of mash on the plate, add meatballs, pour gravy over everything, and add a pile of peas on the side. The peas look like a throwaway, but that sweet green pop against all the beige is a big part of why the IKEA plate works. The mash is really there to soak up gravy, so be generous with it.

## Tips & Notes
- **Gravy too thick?** Splash in more broth or cream. **Too thin?** Simmer a few more minutes, since it thickens as it reduces and again as it cools.
- **Mix too wet?** Roll a test ball first. If it slumps, chill the whole bowl 15 min and try again. If it's still too loose, fold in **1 tbsp panko at a time** and wait 2 min between additions so the crumbs can absorb. Slightly soft is better than stiff, since a mix that's easy to roll with dry hands makes bready meatballs.
- **Want warm spice? (the Swedish-grandma version)** IKEA's recipe is just salt and pepper, but about half the copycats add **½ tsp allspice + ¼ tsp nutmeg** to the meat mix. Many traditional home cooks in Sweden do too. It makes the meatballs a little sweet and cozy, a bit like holiday food. Try it plain first, then decide next time.
- **IKEA's oven finish:** the official method browns the meatballs, then bakes them covered at 350°F for 30 min in a dish before saucing. Simmering them in the gravy gets you to the same place with one less dish.
- **Pickled cucumbers are the classic third side.** *Pressgurka* is just thinly sliced cucumber in 2 parts water, 1 part white vinegar, sugar, and salt for 20 min. The bright crunch cuts through all the richness. If you want it, add a cucumber and white vinegar to your shopping list.
- **Leftovers reheat beautifully.** Store the meatballs in their gravy, then reheat gently with a splash of broth or milk because the gravy tightens in the fridge.
- **Homemade mash, for a high-energy night:** start 3 lb peeled Yukon Golds (2-inch chunks) in cold salted water, since starting cold cooks them evenly. Simmer until fork-tender, 15-20 min, and get them going before you sear the meatballs. Drain, let them steam dry for a minute, then mash with 4 tbsp butter *first* and ¾ cup warm milk + 1½ tsp salt after. Coating the starch in fat first makes the mash creamier. Never use a blender or food processor, because it turns potatoes into glue.
- **Lingonberry jam is the IKEA finishing touch.** A spoonful on the side adds a tart pop against the rich gravy. Whole-berry cranberry sauce does the same job.
- **Freezer-friendly:** Roll a double batch and freeze the extra meatballs raw on a sheet pan, then move them to a bag. Sear them straight from frozen, adding a couple extra minutes.
- **Fun history:** Sweden's meatballs, *köttbullar*, may not be Swedish at all. In 2018, Sweden's official Twitter account said King Charles XII brought the recipe back from the Ottoman Empire in the early 1700s, inspired by Turkish *köfte*. Food historians still debate that. IKEA started selling them in 1985 and reportedly goes through about a billion a year.
