# 🥓 Totaro's Tortellini Tripea

*Cheese tortellini in a Parmesan cream sauce with crispy pancetta and peas. One skillet plus a pasta pot, ~25 minutes, serves 2.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Garlic (1 clove)

**Meat**
- Diced pancetta (~4 oz / 100g)
  - Bacon works if there's no pancetta. It's smokier and renders more fat, so you'll pour some off

**Dairy / Refrigerated**
- Refrigerated cheese tortellini, **one 9–10 oz package**
  - Buitoni is 9 oz and Rana is 10 oz. Either works
  - Cheese is the classic. Prosciutto-filled doubles down on the pork if that's what they have
  - Frozen tortellini works too, with no thawing. Add 2–3 minutes to the cook time
- Heavy cream, **one half-pint (1 cup) carton**. You'll use ¾ cup
- Parmigiano-Reggiano, **a small wedge (~4 oz)**
  - Not the pre-grated tub. It won't melt smooth (see step 1)
  - Look for the dotted rind stamp. A domestic "Parmesan" wedge is fine if the real stuff is pricey
- Unsalted butter
  - Unsalted on purpose. The pancetta and parm bring all the salt this dish needs

**Frozen**
- Frozen peas

*You should have at home: salt, black pepper, a microplane or fine grater*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**
- 1 package (9–10 oz) refrigerated cheese tortellini
- 4 oz / 100g diced pancetta
- ¾ cup heavy cream
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 2 oz / 60g Parmigiano-Reggiano, freshly grated (about 1½ cups fluffy microplaned), plus more for serving
- 1 garlic clove, smashed
- Salt and **lots of** freshly cracked black pepper

---

### 1️⃣ Boil the water and grate the cheese · ⏱ 5 min
- Bring a big pot of salted water to a boil.
- While it heats, microplane the **2 oz / 60g Parmigiano-Reggiano**. Do it now, because there's no time once the sauce is going.
- Freshly grated parm is fluffy and fine, so it melts into the cream almost instantly. Pre-grated tubs are coated in cellulose to stop clumping, which also stops them from melting and makes the sauce gritty.

### 2️⃣ Crisp the pancetta · ⏱ 6–8 min
- Put the **4 oz / 100g pancetta** in a cold, dry skillet with no oil, then turn the heat to medium. Starting cold renders the fat out slowly before the meat browns, so it gets crispy all the way through.
- ⚠️ **Don't start in a hot pan.** It seizes the outside before the fat renders, and the pancetta comes out chewy.
- Add the **smashed garlic clove** for the last minute, then fish it out.
- Scoop the pancetta onto a paper towel and leave the fat in the pan.
- **Set a small handful of pancetta aside for the top.** Whatever goes back into the cream softens, so this handful is what keeps the dish crispy.

### 3️⃣ Build the cream sauce · ⏱ 4 min
- Turn the heat to **medium-low** and add the **1 tbsp unsalted butter** to the pancetta fat.
- Pour in the **¾ cup heavy cream** and bring it to a gentle simmer, with small lazy bubbles instead of a rolling boil.
- Let it reduce for **3–4 minutes**, until it coats the back of a spoon. Reducing concentrates the cream so it clings to the pasta instead of pooling in the bottom of the bowl.
- Stir in the **1 cup frozen peas** for the last 2 minutes.
- If it goes past "coats a spoon" and turns pasty, loosen it with a splash of pasta water. You can grab a ladle from the pot anytime.

### 4️⃣ Cook the tortellini · ⏱ 2–7 min, per package
- While the cream reduces, drop the **tortellini** into the boiling water and cook them **1 minute less than the package says**. They finish cooking in the sauce.
- ⚠️ **Keep it at a steady boil, not a violent one.** Hard rolling water bursts the seams.
- **Scoop out a mug of pasta water** before you drain.

### 5️⃣ Bring it together · ⏱ 1–2 min
- Add the drained tortellini and the pancetta (minus the reserved handful) to the cream.
- Toss gently over **low** heat for about a minute with a splash of pasta water. The starch in the water helps the cream and cheese bind into one glossy sauce.

### 6️⃣ Finish with the parm · ⏱ 1 min
- **Take the pan off the heat** before the cheese goes in.
- Add the **2 oz / 60g grated parm** in two handfuls, tossing after each one.
- ⚠️ **This is the moment that can go wrong.** Over direct heat, the parm proteins tighten into stringy clumps and the sauce turns greasy. Off the heat, it melts smooth.
- Crack in **a lot** of black pepper. It's a star here, cutting through the cream the way it does in cacio e pepe.
- Taste it. **It'll probably taste like it needs salt, so reach for more parm before the salt shaker.** An extra handful seasons the sauce and thickens it at the same time.
- Too thick instead? Loosen it with pasta water a tablespoon at a time.

### 7️⃣ Serve right away
- Top with the reserved crispy pancetta, more grated parm, and another generous crack of pepper.
- Get it to the table fast. The tortellini keep soaking up sauce as they sit, so what looks saucy in the pan will be much thicker 5 minutes later.

## Tips & Notes
- **Sauce broke or went greasy?** The heat was too high when the cheese went in. Off the heat, whisk in a splash of pasta water or cream and it usually comes back together.
- **Sauce too thin?** Add more parm at the end, or simmer the cream 1 more minute before the tortellini go in. **Too thick?** More pasta water.
- **Unsalted butter is tested and confirmed.** Since the finish is "add parm to taste," salted butter would over-season it before you get there.
- **Using bacon?** Pour off some of the fat before the butter goes in, since bacon renders a lot more than pancetta.
- **Want some acid to cut the richness?** Once the parm is in and the pan is off the heat, zest in about ½ lemon and squeeze in 1–2 tsp juice, then toss. The zest brings the bright lemon smell without much sourness, and the juice gives the cut. Keep it last and small, since juice in simmering cream can split it, and too much turns the dish sour. Easiest test: squeeze a wedge over just your own bowl first.
- **More green:** wilt a handful of baby spinach in with the peas.
- **A pinch of nutmeg** in the cream is a classic Emilia-Romagna touch. Cream and nutmeg are the base of their besciamella.
- **The history:** tortellini come from Emilia-Romagna, and Bologna and Modena still argue over who invented them. Legend says an innkeeper in Castelfranco Emilia shaped them after peeking at Venus's navel through a keyhole. Traditionally they're served *in brodo* (in broth). *Tortellini panna, prosciutto e piselli* was the rich, cream-sauced version that swept Italian trattorias in the 1980s, and this is basically that with pancetta swapped in.
