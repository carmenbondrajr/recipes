# 🍜 Bò Kho (Vietnamese Beef Stew)

*Serves 6 generously — Dutch oven, ~3 hours mostly hands-off. No fish sauce.*

Bò kho is what happens when French pot-au-feu moves to Saigon and picks up lemongrass, star
anise and five-spice. The name just means "braised beef." It's traditionally eaten for
**breakfast** with a torn baguette — a leftover of French colonial bread culture — or over rice
noodles for dinner.

## Recipe

### Ingredients

**Marinade**
- 3 lbs beef chuck, in 2" cubes
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp Maggi seasoning
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tsp Chinese five-spice
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp minced ginger
- 2 stalks lemongrass, minced fine (bottom 4" only)
- 1 tsp black pepper

**Braise**
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 large onion, sliced thick
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 3 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tsp annatto powder (or sweet paprika)
- 2 cans (22 oz total) unsweetened coconut water
- 3 cups beef broth
- 1 stalk lemongrass, bruised and tied in a knot
- 3 star anise, 2 bay leaves, 1 cinnamon stick (optional)
- 1 tbsp Maggi + 1 tbsp soy sauce, for adjusting at the end
- 5 carrots, cut into big 1.5" diagonal chunks
- Daikon, cut the same size (optional)

**To serve**
- Rice noodles or baguette
- Cilantro, Thai basil, lime wedges, sliced chilies

---

### 1. Marinate the Beef — 30 min, or overnight
Toss the **3 lbs beef cubes** with **3 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp Maggi, 1 tbsp brown sugar,
2 tsp five-spice, 4 minced garlic cloves, 2 tbsp minced ginger, 2 stalks minced lemongrass,
and 1 tsp black pepper**. Cover and rest at least 30 minutes; overnight in the fridge is
noticeably better.

Five-spice is the ingredient with the smallest margin for error in this whole dish — the star
anise in it compounds with the whole pods later. Two teaspoons across three pounds is the
ceiling, not a suggestion.

### 2. Sear the Beef — 15 min
Heat **2 tbsp neutral oil** in a Dutch oven over medium-high. Shake the excess marinade off each
piece (keep the marinade!) and sear in batches, ~2 min per side, until browned. Don't crowd —
crowding steams.

**The failure mode here:** the sugar and aromatics in the marinade scorch fast. You want
*browned*, not *black*. If the pot bottom is going dark and bitter-smelling between batches,
pull it off the heat, splash in a little broth, scrape it clean, and keep going. Bitter fond
will haunt the entire pot.

### 3. Build the Base — 8 min
Lower to medium. Add the **sliced onion** and **2 smashed garlic cloves**, cook ~4 min until
softened. Add **3 tbsp tomato paste** and **1 tsp annatto powder**, cook 2 min until the paste
darkens and the oil turns red — this is where the color comes from, so don't rush it. Scrape in
every bit of the reserved marinade and cook 1 more minute.

### 4. Braise — 2 to 2.5 hours
Return the beef and any juices. Add **22 oz coconut water**, **3 cups beef broth**, the
**knotted lemongrass stalk**, **3 star anise**, **2 bay leaves**, and the **cinnamon stick**.
Liquid should just about cover the beef; top with water if not.

Bring to a boil, then drop to the laziest simmer your burner can hold — barely a bubble. Lid
slightly ajar. Simmer **2 to 2.5 hours**, stirring every 30 min, until a fork twists a cube
apart with no resistance.

The coconut water is doing real work: it's mildly sweet and slightly acidic, which tenderizes
and gives bò kho its characteristic round sweetness without tasting like dessert. Coconut
*milk* would make it a curry — wrong dish.

### 5. Add the Vegetables — 30 min
Add the **5 carrots** and **daikon** if using. Simmer uncovered 25-30 min until fork-tender.
Uncovered is intentional — the broth reduces and concentrates while the carrots cook.

Cut them big. Small carrots dissolve into mush over 30 minutes of simmering, and fat chunks
that hold their shape are half the pleasure of this stew.

### 6. Season & Finish — 5 min
Fish out the star anise, bay leaves, cinnamon stick, and lemongrass knot. Taste, then adjust
with **1 tbsp Maggi + 1 tbsp soy sauce**, going a little at a time. A pinch of brown sugar if
it's sharp; a squeeze of lime if it's flat and heavy.

Broth should coat a spoon lightly. Too thin — simmer uncovered 10 more minutes. Too thick —
splash in broth.

### 7. Serve
**Over noodles:** soften rice noodles per the package, divide into bowls, ladle stew over.
**With bread:** toasted baguette for dipping, stew in a bowl alongside.

Top with cilantro, Thai basil, chilies, and a lime wedge on every bowl. The lime isn't a
garnish — the squeeze at the table is what keeps a three-hour braise from tasting heavy.

## Tips & Notes

- **Why Maggi instead of fish sauce:** fish sauce contributes salt + glutamate funk. Maggi is
  hydrolyzed wheat protein — same savory-glutamate hit, zero anchovy. It's already a staple in
  Vietnamese kitchens (it rode in with the French), so this isn't a compromise substitution.
- **No Maggi anywhere?** 2 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp mushroom bouillon powder per 1 tbsp Maggi
  called for. Mushroom powder covers the glutamate gap.
- **Skip the daikon** if you're unsure — it's traditional and adds a clean, slightly peppery
  bite, but it's also the ingredient most likely to sit in your crisper drawer. Carrots alone
  are completely legitimate.
- **Lemongrass prep:** peel the dry outer layers, use only the pale bottom 4 inches. The green
  tops are woody — save one to knot and toss in the braise for perfume.
- **It's better on day two.** The spices keep integrating overnight. Make it a day ahead if you
  can, and skim the solidified fat off the top cold.
- **Freezes beautifully** for 3 months. Freeze the stew only; cook fresh noodles.
- **Instant Pot version:** sear on Sauté, then 35 min high pressure + natural release. Add
  carrots and pressure 4 more minutes. Loses a little depth, saves two hours.
- **Too sweet?** More Maggi and a lime squeeze. **Too salty?** A splash of coconut water and
  another carrot.
