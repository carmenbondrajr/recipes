# 🫘 Crispy Chickpeas & Buttery Limas

*One 10-inch Dutch oven, ~30 minutes, serves 2 as a meal. Rosemary chickpeas fried crispy in butter and dusted with paprika and bee balm, piled over garlicky lemon-thyme lima beans steamed in the same pot. It's the chickpeas from Chickpea Chops without the pork. Heat is given as numbers on the stove dial.*

## Shopping List

**From the yard**
- Fresh thyme (~6 sprigs)
- Fresh rosemary (1 sprig)
- Dried bee balm / wild bergamot (½ tsp crumbled, about 3–5 dried leaves)

**Produce**
- Lemon (1)
- Garlic (1 head)

**Dairy**
- Butter (4 tbsp)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Chickpeas (one 15 oz can)

**Freezer**
- Frozen lima beans, ~2 cups (10–12 oz). Baby limas or the bigger Fordhooks both work. If your bag is 16 oz, use about three-quarters of it

*You should have at home: neutral oil (vegetable/canola), kosher salt, black pepper, sweet paprika, garlic powder*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The finishing dust*
- ½ tsp sweet paprika
- ½ tsp crumbled dried bee balm
- ¼ tsp garlic powder
- Pinch of black pepper

*Everything else*
- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas
- ~2 cups (10–12 oz) frozen lima beans
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 tbsp butter
- 2 garlic cloves, smashed and peeled
- ~6 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 sprig fresh rosemary
- ¼ cup water
- ½ lemon, plus wedges for serving
- Kosher salt

---

### 1️⃣ Prep · ⏱ 5 min
- Set the empty Dutch oven on **5** while you prep. A heavy pot needs a few minutes to heat evenly, or the chickpeas stick in the hot spots.
- Drain the **chickpeas** and rinse them right in the can: fill it with water, swirl, and drain again.
- Tip them onto a kitchen towel, fold the towel over the top, and pat them dry. Leave them under the towel until they go in, because the extra minutes let them air-dry even more. Any water left on them turns to steam, and wet chickpeas never crisp.
- Crumble the **½ tsp dried bee balm** between your palms into a small bowl, then mix in the rest of the finishing dust:
  - **½ tsp sweet paprika**
  - **¼ tsp garlic powder**
  - **Pinch of black pepper**
- Smash and peel the **2 garlic cloves**. Leave the **limas** in the freezer until step 4. They go straight into the pot frozen.

### 2️⃣ Fry the chickpeas · ⏱ 8–10 min
- Turn the dial up to **6** and add **1 tbsp oil** and **1 tbsp butter**. The oil keeps the butter from burning during the long fry.
- Add the **chickpeas** with a pinch of salt and tuck the **rosemary sprig** in among them. Spread them into one layer and let them sit for a minute before the first stir, so they get a head start on browning.
- Stir now and then. Once the rosemary is crisp and dark green, after about **2–3 minutes**, pull it out onto a plate. The butter already has its flavor by then, and the needles turn bitter if they stay in.
- **Halfway through (~4–5 minutes),** add **2 tbsp butter**. The chickpeas soak up fat as they fry, and once the pot goes dry they scorch instead of crisping.
- They're done when they're deep golden, crisp at the edges, and rattle when you stir them.
- ⚠️ **Keep the lid off.** The tall walls already trap some steam, and steam undoes crispy.

### 3️⃣ Dust them off the heat · ⏱ 1 min
- Lift the pot off the burner, sprinkle the **finishing dust** over the chickpeas, and toss for **30 seconds**. The pot is still hot enough to toast the spices in the butter.
- ⚠️ **Never add the paprika while the pot is on the burner.** Paprika has natural sugars and turns bitter in under a minute in a hot, dry pot. The pork version gets away with it because the paprika is on the meat, not straight on the metal.
- Scoop the chickpeas onto a plate with a slotted spoon and **leave the red butter in the pot**. That butter is what seasons the limas.

### 4️⃣ Steam the limas · ⏱ 6–8 min
- Set the pot back on **4** and add the **frozen limas** straight from the bag, along with:
  - **2 smashed garlic cloves**
  - **~4 thyme sprigs** (save the rest for the plate)
  - **1 tbsp butter**
  - **¼ cup water**
- Cover and let them steam for **5–6 minutes**, stirring once halfway. The water does the cooking, and the spiced butter coats them as it goes.
- Bite one to check. It should be creamy all the way through with no chalky center. Fordhooks may need **2 more minutes**.

### 5️⃣ Finish the limas · ⏱ 1–2 min
- Take the lid off and let any leftover water cook away until the beans look glossy instead of wet.
- Fish out the thyme stems, then season with salt and a squeeze from the **½ lemon**. Taste one and add more lemon or salt until it tastes bright.

### 6️⃣ Plate it
- Spoon the limas into bowls with their butter.
- ⚠️ **Add the chickpeas last, right at the table.** If they sit on the steamy limas for more than a few minutes, they go soft.
- Pile the **chickpeas** on top, crumble the crispy rosemary needles over them (toss the stem), strip a few fresh thyme leaves over everything, and serve with lemon wedges.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: soggy chickpeas.** They're either still wet going in, crowded, or sitting on the limas too long. Dry them well, give them the full fry, and add them at the last second.
- **What else ruins it: burnt paprika.** It only goes in off the heat. If the dust smells bitter, it scorched, so start a fresh pinch.
- **Want it creamier?** Mash about a quarter of the limas against the side of the pot in step 5. They thicken the butter into a light sauce.
- **Make it heartier:** A fried egg on top is great, with the runny yolk acting as a sauce. Blistered green beans from Chickpea Chops also work as a side.
- **Leftovers:** Store the chickpeas and limas separately. Re-crisp the chickpeas in a dry pan on **5** for a few minutes. The limas reheat fine in the microwave with a splash of water.
- **Want a little smoke?** Swap the sweet paprika for smoked, or do half and half.
- **Why "lima"?** They're named after Lima, Peru. Spanish ships carried them out of that port, and crates got labeled by where they came from. Lima beans were domesticated in the Andes thousands of years ago. In the American South they're "butter beans," which suits how they taste here.
