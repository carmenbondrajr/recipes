# 🌮 Grilled Steak Tacos

*Serves 2 (about 8 tacos), ~40 minutes of hands-on time plus a 2–4 hour marinade. Lime-and-chili marinated skirt steak, hard-seared on the grill and sliced thin, with a quick pico, avocado, and cotija on grill-charred corn tortillas. Adapted from [BBQ Revolt](https://bbqrevolt.com/recipes/grilled-steak-tacos/).*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Limes (3). 1 for the marinade, 1 for the pico, 1 cut into wedges for serving
- Garlic (1 head; you need 2 cloves)
- White onion (1 small)
- Cilantro (1 bunch)
- Roma tomatoes (2). Romas are less watery than round tomatoes, so the pico doesn't puddle in the tortilla
- Avocado (1). Buy one that gives slightly when squeezed, or a firm one two days ahead
- Jalapeño (1, optional). Grab it if you want some heat in the pico

**Meat**
- **Skirt steak or flank steak, ~1 lb**
  - **Skirt is the pick.** It's the classic carne asada and fajita cut: beefier, looser-grained, and it cooks in about 5 minutes
  - **Flank is the backup.** It's thicker and leaner, so it takes a few minutes longer and is a little less forgiving
  - If the case has both "outside" and "inside" skirt, outside is more tender. Either works

**Dairy**
- Cotija (one small wedge or tub, ~4 oz; you'll use about ⅓ cup). Feta is a fine stand-in if the store is out

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Small corn tortillas (6-inch, one pack). Flour works too if you prefer it
- Ground cumin, smoked paprika, chili powder, dried oregano. Check you have all four. Mexican oregano is ideal if you see it, it's more citrusy than the Mediterranean kind

*You should have at home: olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The marinade*
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp lime juice (1 lime)
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- ¾ tsp ground cumin
- ¾ tsp smoked paprika
- ¾ tsp chili powder
- ½ tsp dried oregano
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper

*The quick pico*
- 2 Roma tomatoes, diced
- ⅓ cup finely diced white onion
- ¼ cup chopped cilantro
- 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced (optional)
- Juice of ½ lime
- Pinch of kosher salt

*Everything else*
- ~1 lb skirt or flank steak
- 8 small corn tortillas
- 1 avocado, sliced
- ⅓ cup crumbled cotija
- Extra diced onion and cilantro for topping
- Lime wedges for serving

---

### 1️⃣ Marinate the steak · ⏱ 5 min, plus 2–4 hours
- Whisk the marinade together in a bowl:
  - **2 tbsp olive oil**
  - **2 tbsp lime juice**
  - **2 minced garlic cloves**
  - **¾ tsp cumin**
  - **¾ tsp smoked paprika**
  - **¾ tsp chili powder**
  - **½ tsp dried oregano**
  - **1 tsp kosher salt**
  - **½ tsp black pepper**
- If the **skirt steak** is one long strip, cut it into 2 or 3 pieces so it fits in the bag and on the grill.
- Put the steak in a zip-top bag, pour in the marinade, squeeze out the air, and massage it in. Refrigerate for **2–4 hours**. The salt does most of the work, seasoning the meat deeper the longer it sits.
- ⚠️ **Don't go past about 8 hours.** Lime juice is acidic, and given too long it turns the surface of the steak mushy and grainy instead of tender.

### 2️⃣ Make the quick pico · ⏱ 10 min
- Do this while the steak marinates, or at least 15 minutes before eating, so the flavors have time to meld.
- Stir together in a bowl:
  - **2 diced Roma tomatoes**
  - **⅓ cup finely diced white onion**
  - **¼ cup chopped cilantro**
  - **1 minced jalapeño**, if using
  - **Juice of ½ lime**
  - **A pinch of kosher salt**
- The salt pulls juice out of the tomatoes and mellows the raw onion bite as it sits. Give it a stir and a taste before serving, and add salt or lime if it's flat.
- Dice a little extra onion and chop a little extra cilantro for topping.

### 3️⃣ Heat the grill · ⏱ 10–15 min
- Take the steak out of the fridge when you start the grill so it loses some of its chill.
- Get the grill **hot, 450–500°F** with the lid closed. On charcoal, that's a full chimney spread under one side. Skirt steak is thin, so you want high heat that crusts the outside before the inside overcooks.
- Scrape the grates clean, then oil them with a paper towel dipped in oil and held in tongs. Marinated steak loves to stick.

### 4️⃣ Grill the steak · ⏱ 5–10 min
- Lift the steak out of the bag and let the excess drip off, then pat it lightly with a paper towel. A wet surface steams instead of searing, and loose bits of garlic burn on the grill.
- Lay it on the hot grates and leave it alone. **Skirt: 2–3 minutes per side. Flank: 4–5 minutes per side.** You want dark char marks before you flip.
- 🌡️ **Pull at 125–130°F** in the thickest part for medium-rare. It'll coast up about 5 degrees while it rests. Skirt is best at medium-rare to medium. Past that, it gets chewy fast.
- ⚠️ **Thin ends cook first.** Skirt is uneven, so check the thickest piece and pull the thin pieces early if they're done.

### 5️⃣ Rest the steak and char the tortillas · ⏱ 10 min
- Move the steak to a cutting board and let it rest for **10 minutes**, untented. Resting lets the juices settle back in, so they stay in the meat instead of running all over the board.
- While it rests, lay the **8 tortillas** on the grill for **30–45 seconds per side**, until they puff a little and get a few charred spots.
- Stack them inside a folded kitchen towel as they come off. The trapped steam keeps them soft and bendable. ⚠️ Corn tortillas left out in the open go stiff and crack when folded.

### 6️⃣ Slice against the grain · ⏱ 3 min
- Look at the steak and find the grain, the long muscle lines running across it. On skirt steak, they run across the **short** width of the strip, not the length.
- **For skirt:** cut the steak into 4–5 inch sections first, then turn each section 90° and slice it thin, across those lines.
- **For flank:** the grain runs the long way, so slice thin across the short side, angling the knife slightly.
- Chop the slices into bite-size pieces if you like, which makes the tacos easier to eat.
- ⚠️ **This is the step that makes or breaks it.** Sliced with the grain, skirt steak is like chewing a rubber band. Sliced across it, the same steak is tender.

### 7️⃣ Build the tacos
- Fill each tortilla with **steak**, a spoonful of **pico**, a couple of **avocado slices**, a sprinkle of **cotija**, and a pinch of the extra **onion and cilantro**.
- Serve with **lime wedges** and squeeze one over each taco right before eating.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: overcooked steak sliced the wrong way.** Skirt and flank are lean, hard-working muscles. Kept at medium-rare and sliced thin across the grain, they're tender and beefy. Cooked to well-done or sliced along the grain, they're tough.
- **No grill?** Use a cast iron skillet over high heat with a slick of oil, 3–4 minutes per side for skirt. Open a window, it'll smoke. Char the tortillas directly over a gas burner flame with tongs, about 15 seconds a side.
- **Tortillas tearing?** Use two per taco, stacked, like the taquerías do. Or switch to flour tortillas, which bend without cracking.
- **Flavor swaps for the marinade:** add 1 tsp Worcestershire or soy sauce for more savory depth, or 1 minced chipotle in adobo for smoky heat.
- **Want a sauce?** Stir lime juice and a pinch of salt into sour cream for a quick crema, thin enough to drizzle.
- **The names:** *carne asada* just means "grilled meat." *Fajita* means "little belt" or "little sash," the vaqueros' nickname for skirt steak, the cheap belt-shaped cut that ranch hands along the Texas–Mexico border were given as part of their pay starting around the 1930s. When fajitas got popular in the '80s, skirt steak went from a throwaway cut to a pricey one.
- **Tacos al carbón** is the name for tacos made with charcoal-grilled meat, which is exactly what this is.
