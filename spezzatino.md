# 🍅 Spezzatino
### Italian Beef Spezzatino

*Serves 5-6. Tomato-braised beef with sweet peppers and chickpeas: thicker than a stew, chunkier than a ragu, and made for dunking garlic bread. Stovetop, Dutch oven, ~3½-4 hrs (mostly hands-off). Start early, because the beef sets the schedule, not the clock.*

*"Spezzatino" comes from **spezzare**, "to break into pieces." So it's literally "little broken-up bits," which is what Italians call beef stew. Every region does its own version. Peppers and chickpeas give this one a southern Italian lean.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- 1 yellow onion
- 3 carrots (from a pack)
- 2 bell peppers, any color (red/yellow/orange are sweetest; green is more bitter and grassy)
- 1 bunch flat-leaf parsley
- 1 head garlic (you need 5 cloves)

**Meat**
- 2.4 lbs cubed beef chuck (pre-cut "stew meat" works, but **chuck** is the one you want: it has the most collagen)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- 28 oz can crushed tomatoes
- 1 small can tomato paste (6 oz; you'll use about half)
- Beef broth (one 32 oz carton; you'll use 2 cups and keep the rest in reserve)
- 1 can chickpeas (15.5 oz)

**Bakery / Frozen**
- Bake-and-eat garlic bread 🍞 (the whole point)

*You should have at home: olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper, dried oregano, bay leaves, red pepper flakes, sugar. Optional: ½ cup dry red wine, a Parmesan rind, balsamic vinegar*

## Recipe

### Ingredients
- 2.4 lbs beef chuck, in ~1.5" cubes
- 2 tsp kosher salt (for the beef), plus more to taste
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 3 tbsp olive oil, divided
- 1 yellow onion, diced
- 3 carrots, finely diced (about ¼", so they melt into the sauce)
- 5 cloves garlic, sliced thin
- 3 tbsp tomato paste (about half the small can)
- ½ tsp red pepper flakes
- 1½ tsp dried oregano
- ½ cup dry red wine (optional)
- 28 oz crushed tomatoes
- 2 cups beef broth (plus reserve)
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 Parmesan rind (optional)
- 2 bell peppers, cut into ½"-wide strips
- 1 can chickpeas (15.5 oz), drained and rinsed
- ½ cup chopped parsley
- Pinch of sugar / splash of balsamic, if needed at the end

### 1. Dry & Season the Beef (5 min)
Pat the **2.4 lbs beef** very dry with paper towels. Season all over with **2 tsp kosher salt** and **1 tsp black pepper**. Wet meat steams instead of searing, and you won't get the brown crust that makes the sauce taste deep and beefy.

### 2. Sear in Batches (20 min)
Heat **1½ tbsp olive oil** in the Dutch oven over medium-high until it shimmers. Sear the beef in **2-3 batches** with space between the cubes. Leave each side alone for ~3 min until it's deep brown and releases easily. Move the seared beef to a **rimmed plate, uncovered**. Foil traps steam and softens the crust, and the juices that pool are flavor you'll pour back in later. Add a little more oil between batches if the pot looks dry.

**Failure mode:** If you crowd the pot, the beef dumps its juice and turns grey and boiled-looking. If you see liquid pooling, pull some pieces out and do another batch. The dark crusty bits stuck to the pot bottom (the *fond*) are flavor, not burn. Just keep the heat from going past dark brown into black.

### 3. Soften the Vegetables (8 min)
Drop the heat to medium. Add the remaining **1½ tbsp olive oil**, the **diced onion**, and the **3 finely diced carrots** with a pinch of salt. Cook ~6 min, scraping up the fond as the onion releases its moisture. Add the **5 cloves sliced garlic** and cook 1 min until fragrant. Don't let the garlic go past golden, because burnt garlic makes the whole pot bitter.

### 4. Bloom the Tomato Paste (3 min)
Push the veg to the edges and add the **3 tbsp tomato paste**, **½ tsp red pepper flakes**, and **1½ tsp dried oregano** straight onto the hot pot bottom. Stir and cook ~2-3 min until the paste goes from bright red to **brick red** and starts sticking. Frying the paste caramelizes its sugars, which is where the "simmered all day" depth comes from.

### 5. Deglaze & Build the Braise (5 min)
Pour in the **½ cup red wine** (or ½ cup of the broth if skipping wine) and scrape the bottom hard. Everything stuck down there should dissolve into the liquid. Let it bubble 2 min. Add the **28 oz crushed tomatoes**, **2 cups beef broth**, **2 bay leaves**, and the **Parmesan rind** if using. Return the beef and all its resting juices to the pot.

### 6. Low & Slow Simmer (2½-3 hrs)
Bring to a simmer, then turn it down until you see just a **lazy bubble or two** breaking the surface. Cover with the lid **cracked about an inch** so some steam escapes and the sauce slowly thickens. Stir every 20-30 min, scraping the bottom so nothing catches.

**🔥 Dial it to 3 and wait.** On Carmen's burner, **3** is the lazy-bubble spot for this pot. The cast iron holds so much heat that it takes several minutes to show a dial change. So set it to **3**, give it ~5 min, and *then* judge the bubbles. If you keep nudging the dial before the pot catches up, it swings between boiling and barely moving.

**Failure mode #1: boiling.** A rolling boil squeezes the moisture out of the meat, leaving it stringy and dry even while it's sitting in liquid. Keep it lazy.

**Failure mode #2: too low = stalled.** If the surface is totally still with no bubbles, the pot is too cool to melt the collagen, and the beef just sits there tough. An hour of that is basically an hour lost. You want *some* movement: a lazy bubble every few seconds.

**Failure mode #3: panicking at 90 minutes.** Chuck gets *tougher* before it gets tender, and the collagen needs time to melt into gelatin. If a cube is chewy at the 90-min mark, it needs **more** time, not less. The sauce will also look thin for a while. That's normal.

### 7. Add Peppers & Chickpeas (30-40 min)
Once a beef cube gives easily to a fork, stir in the **2 bell peppers (strips)** and the **drained chickpeas**. Take the lid **off** and simmer another 30-40 min. The peppers should turn silky but still hold their shape. The chickpeas soak up the sauce, and the open pot lets the sauce reduce.

**What "done" looks like:** The sauce coats the back of a spoon and clings to the beef instead of pooling like soup. If it's too thin, keep reducing uncovered. If it's too thick, add a splash of the reserved broth.

**🍞 Garlic bread timing:** Preheat the oven now so the bread comes out when the pot does.

### 8. Finish & Taste (5 min)
Fish out the **bay leaves** and **Parmesan rind**. Taste and adjust:
- **Flat?** Add more salt. It's almost always salt.
- **Sharp or tinny?** Tomato acidity; add a **pinch of sugar**.
- **Heavy or one-note?** Add a **small splash of balsamic** to brighten it.

Stir in most of the **½ cup chopped parsley**, then scatter the rest on top. Serve in shallow bowls with the garlic bread for dunking.

## Tips & Notes
- **Better on day two.** Like most braises, the flavors meld overnight. It reheats beautifully on the stove with a splash of broth. If the beef came out a little firm, simmer the leftovers gently, covered, for 20-30 min. That finishes the tenderizing.
- **Freezes great** for ~3 months. The peppers will soften a bit more, and that's fine.
- **Want it as a true ragu?** Once it's done, shred the beef right in the pot with two forks. Then it's pasta sauce (pappardelle or rigatoni) or a polenta topper. Save this for leftovers night.
- **No wine?** Use broth to deglaze and add the balsamic splash at the end. You still get the acidity and depth.
- **Parmesan rind hoarding:** Keep your rinds in a freezer bag. They add savory umami to any tomato braise or soup.
- **Leftover pantry bits:** The other half of the tomato paste freezes in tablespoon dollops on parchment. The diced tomatoes and second chickpea can are perfect for [Pasta Fazool](pasta-fazool.md).
- **Want more stew-ness?** Add 1 lb red potatoes (1.5" chunks) with the peppers at step 7 and give it the full 40 min. They release starch and thicken the sauce, so you may need a splash of the reserved broth.
