# 🧀 Party Mac
### Party Baked Mac & Cheese

*One 9×13 dish, serves 6 alongside other food. A sharp cheddar and fontina sauce with a crispy Parmesan-panko top. It's made to sit on the table for a while, and it's great piled onto a pulled pork slider. Easiest party plan: assemble it in the morning and bake it when people get hungry.*

## Shopping List

**Dairy**
- **Sharp cheddar, 16 oz:** two 8 oz blocks
  - ⚠️ **Buy blocks, not pre-shredded.** Bagged shreds are coated in starch so they don't clump in the bag, and that same coating makes the sauce grainy
  - White or orange both work. Orange is more festive for Halloween
- **Fontina, ~8 oz:** one wedge, anything from 6 to 8 oz is fine
  - Fontina is the stretchy, melty one. If they don't have it, Gruyère or Monterey Jack work too
- Cream cheese (half of an 8 oz brick)
- Whole milk (4 cups / 1 quart)
- Butter (8 tbsp / 1 stick)
- Parmesan, grated (½ cup)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Pasta, 1 lb:** cavatappi (corkscrews) is best, since the spirals trap sauce. Elbows or shells work too
- Panko breadcrumbs (1 cup)
- Dry mustard powder (1 tsp)
  - If you don't have it, use 2 tsp Dijon

*You should have at home: flour, kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, smoked or sweet paprika, a 9×13 baking dish, a box grater*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The pasta*
- 1 lb cavatappi
- Kosher salt for the pasta water

*The cheese sauce*
- 6 tbsp butter
- 6 tbsp flour
- 4 cups whole milk
- 4 oz cream cheese, cut into cubes
- 1 tsp dry mustard
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 16 oz sharp cheddar, grated (set aside 1 cup for the top)
- ~8 oz fontina, grated

*The crunchy top*
- 1 cup panko
- 2 tbsp butter, melted
- ½ cup grated Parmesan
- 1 cup of the reserved cheddar
- A pinch of paprika

---

### 1️⃣ Grate the cheese · ⏱ 10 min
- Grate the **16 oz cheddar** and the **~8 oz fontina** on the large holes of a box grater. Toss them together in a bowl, then set **1 cup** aside for the topping.
- Do this first. The sauce moves quickly once it starts, and you won't have time to grate in the middle of it.
- Butter the **9×13 dish** and heat the oven to **375°F**.

### 2️⃣ Undercook the pasta · ⏱ 10 min
- Bring a big pot of water to a boil and salt it well, until it tastes like the ocean. This is your only chance to season the pasta itself.
- Cook the **1 lb cavatappi** for **2 minutes less than the package says**. Drain it and don't rinse.
- ⚠️ **Underdone on purpose.** The pasta keeps soaking up sauce in the oven and while it sits out, so pasta that's fully cooked now turns soft and mushy by the time people eat it.

### 3️⃣ Make the roux · ⏱ 2 min
- In the same pot (or a big saucepan), melt **6 tbsp butter** over **medium** heat.
- Whisk in **6 tbsp flour** and keep whisking for **1–2 minutes**, until it smells a little toasty. That cooks off the raw flour taste. It should stay pale blond, not brown.

### 4️⃣ Build the sauce · ⏱ 8–10 min
- Pour in the **4 cups whole milk** a splash at a time, whisking the whole time. Adding it slowly is what keeps lumps from forming.
- Whisk in the seasonings:
  - **1 tsp dry mustard**
  - **½ tsp garlic powder**
  - **1½ tsp kosher salt**
  - **½ tsp black pepper**
- Keep it at a gentle simmer, whisking often and scraping the corners, until it's thick enough to coat a spoon. That takes about **6–8 minutes**.
- This is more sauce than most recipes call for, on purpose. The pasta keeps absorbing it as it sits, and the extra keeps the mac creamy instead of turning into a firm block.

### 5️⃣ Melt in the cheese · ⏱ 3 min
- **Turn the heat off** before you add any cheese.
- Stir in the **4 oz cubed cream cheese** first, until it's smooth. It's the stabilizer that keeps the sauce creamy for hours instead of turning greasy.
- Add the **cheddar and fontina** (except the cup you set aside) a handful at a time, and let each handful melt before adding the next.
- ⚠️ **Cheese over high heat breaks.** The proteins tighten up and squeeze out the fat, and you end up with a greasy, gritty sauce. The hot sauce has plenty of heat left to melt everything.
- Taste it. It should taste a little *too* salty and cheesy, because the pasta will dull it down.

### 6️⃣ Combine and top · ⏱ 5 min
- Fold the **drained pasta** into the sauce. It'll look loose and saucy, and that's right.
- Pour it into the **9×13 dish** and spread it out evenly.
- Mix the topping in a small bowl:
  - **1 cup panko**
  - **2 tbsp melted butter**
  - **½ cup grated Parmesan**
- Scatter the **1 cup reserved cheddar** over the mac, then the panko mixture on top of that, and finish with a light dusting of **paprika** for color.
- **Making it ahead?** Stop here. Let it cool, cover it with foil, and refrigerate it for up to a day. Put the panko topping on right before baking so it stays crunchy.

### 7️⃣ Bake · ⏱ 20–25 min (35–40 if it's been in the fridge)
- Bake uncovered at **375°F** until the edges are bubbling and the top is golden.
- If it's cold from the fridge, bake it covered with foil for the first **20 minutes**, then uncover it for the last **15–20** so the top gets crisp.
- Let it rest **10 minutes** before serving. The sauce settles and thickens a little, so it scoops instead of running.

### 8️⃣ Keep it warm
- Turn the oven down to its lowest setting, **170–200°F**, and leave the dish in with a loose sheet of foil over it. Bring it out when people want it, or set it out on a trivet and put it back in between rounds.
- ⚠️ **It's best within about 2–3 hours of baking.** After that it slowly goes from creamy to firm. That's why the best plan is assembling in the morning and baking when the crew gets hungry, rather than baking at 10 am.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: a grainy or greasy sauce.** That comes from pre-shredded cheese or adding cheese over the heat. Grate your own and melt it off the burner.
- **What else ruins it: mushy pasta.** Undercook it by 2 minutes. It finishes cooking in the oven.
- **With the pulled pork:** a scoop of mac on a slider with pork on top is the move. A drizzle of BBQ sauce or a few pickled jalapeños takes it over the top.
- **Want it smoky?** Swap 4 oz of the cheddar for smoked Gouda. It's great next to slow-cooked pork.
- **Want a little heat?** Add ¼ tsp cayenne with the mustard. You won't really taste it as heat, it just makes the cheese taste cheesier.
- **Why dry mustard?** It's an old trick. You can't taste mustard in the finished dish, but its sharpness makes the cheddar flavor pop. Cayenne does the same thing.
- **The history:** pasta baked with cheese goes back at least to the 1300s. The *Liber de Coquina*, a medieval cookbook from southern Italy, has a recipe for pasta sheets layered with grated cheese. In America, it's often linked to **Thomas Jefferson**, who fell for pasta in Europe and served "macaroni pie" at the White House. A lot of the credit really belongs to **James Hemings**, an enslaved chef Jefferson brought to Paris to train in French kitchens. Hemings brought that European cooking back to Monticello, and historians increasingly credit him with introducing dishes like this to American cooking.
