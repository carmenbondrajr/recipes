# 🍎 Pork Chop with Apple Cider Pan Sauce

*One skillet, ~30 minutes (plus salting time), serves 1. A thick seared chop with a glossy cider, Dijon, and butter sauce. Very October.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Apple (1 small), **firm-tart** like Honeycrisp, Pink Lady, or Granny Smith — *soft apples like Red Delicious or McIntosh turn to mush in the pan*
- Shallot (1 small)
- Fresh thyme (small pack) — *optional, but it's the classic pairing. Dried works in a pinch*

**Meat**
- **One bone-in pork chop, 1¼–1½ inches thick** (~12–14 oz). This is the most important buy. Ask the butcher counter to cut one if the packs are all thin. *Thin chops (under 1 inch) overcook before they get a crust, and that's how you get dry pork. If thick isn't an option, see Tips.*

**Dairy**
- Butter

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Apple cider**, the cloudy unfiltered kind (smallest jug, usually 1 quart or half gallon, you'll use ½ cup) — *not apple juice, which is thinner and sweeter. Leftover cider is just a fall drink*
- Chicken broth, **low-sodium** (smallest carton, you'll use ¼ cup)
- Dijon mustard — *whole-grain is even better here if you have it*
- Apple cider vinegar

*You should have at home: neutral oil (vegetable/canola), salt, pepper*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**
- 1 bone-in pork chop, 1¼–1½ inches thick (~12–14 oz)
- ¾ tsp kosher salt + black pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- ½ small apple, cored and cut into thin wedges
- 1 small shallot, minced
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme (or ¼ tsp dried)
- ½ cup apple cider
- ¼ cup low-sodium chicken broth
- 1 tsp Dijon
- ½ tsp apple cider vinegar
- 2 tbsp **cold** butter, split into 1 tbsp + 1 tbsp

**Steps**

1. **Salt early** (ideally 45 min to overnight). Pat the **chop** dry and salt both sides with the **¾ tsp kosher salt**. Leave it uncovered on a plate in the fridge. Salt draws moisture out and then gets pulled back in, seasoning the meat all the way through and drying the surface so it browns. **No time? Salt it right before it hits the pan.** Avoid the in-between (5–30 min), when the surface is wet and won't sear. Take it out of the fridge while you prep the other stuff.

2. **Prep everything before the chop goes in** (~5 min). Slice the **½ apple**, mince the **shallot**, and measure the **½ cup cider**, **¼ cup broth**, **1 tsp Dijon**, and **½ tsp vinegar** into one cup. The sauce moves fast, so you want it all lined up.

3. **Render the fat cap** (~2 min). Pat the chop dry again and pepper it. Heat **1 tbsp oil** in a 10-inch skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Use tongs to stand the chop **on its fatty edge** and hold it there for 1–2 minutes until the fat is golden and has dripped into the pan. Rubbery white fat is sad. Crispy rendered fat is a treat.

4. **Sear and flip** (~8–12 min). Lay the chop flat. Sear 3 minutes without touching it, until it's deep golden and releases on its own. Flip, then **drop to medium** and keep flipping every 1–2 minutes. Frequent flipping cooks it more evenly and keeps a gray band from forming under the crust. **Pull it at 140°F** in the thickest part, away from the bone. It will coast up to 145°F while it rests.
   - **Slightly pink in the middle is correct.** USDA lowered pork to 145°F back in 2011. Old-school "cook it till it's white" is how everyone's grandma ended up with shoe leather.
   - Rest it on a plate, loosely tented with foil, for **at least 5 minutes**. That's exactly how long the sauce takes.

5. **Brown the apples** (~3 min). Pour off all but about 1 tbsp of fat. Back on medium, add the **apple wedges** in one layer and let them get golden on one side, ~2 minutes. Add the **shallot** and **thyme** and stir for 30 seconds until fragrant.

6. **Deglaze and reduce** (~4 min). Pour in the **cider/broth cup** and scrape up every brown bit. That fond is the flavor of the sauce. Simmer hard until it's reduced to about ⅓ cup and looks a little syrupy, so a spoon dragged through it leaves a trail. Pour any juices from the resting plate back in.

7. **Mount the butter, off heat** (~1 min). Take the pan **off the burner**, wait 10 seconds, then swirl in the **2 tbsp cold butter** a tablespoon at a time until glossy. **Off-heat and cold is the trick.** The butter emulsifies into a silky sauce instead of melting into a greasy puddle. Taste. Too sweet? A few more drops of vinegar. Flat? A pinch of salt.

8. **Plate it.** Slice the chop off the bone (or don't, gnawing the bone is chef's privilege), spoon the apples and sauce over it, and eat right away.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: a dry chop.** It's almost always one of two things, a thin chop or no thermometer. Thick and bone-in gives you a buffer. Pull it at 140°F and trust the rest.
- **What else ruins it: a cloying sauce.** Cider reduces into something very sweet. The Dijon and vinegar aren't optional, they're the balance. If it tastes like apple pie filling, add more vinegar a few drops at a time.
- **Broken butter sauce?** If it looks greasy and separated, the pan was too hot. Add a teaspoon of water or broth off the heat and swirl hard. It usually comes back.
- **Only thin chops at the store?** Buy two, skip the fat-cap step, and sear them hard on high, 2 minutes per side. Pull at 140°F. The crust won't be as deep, but they'll stay juicy.
- **Hard cider swap:** a dry hard cider makes a less sweet, more grown-up sauce. Same amount.
- **Normandy-style pivot:** stir in **2 tbsp heavy cream** after the reduction and skip the butter. That's basically *porc à la normande*. Normandy is apple and cream country, and pork with cider and cream is their signature move.
- **Why pork + apples is a classic:** pork season and apple season used to be the same season. Pigs were traditionally fattened in the fall (sometimes on windfall apples in orchards) and slaughtered as it got cold, right when the apples came in.
- **Leftover half apple:** slice it for the side, or snack on it while the chop rests.

## What to Serve With It

One thing to **soak up the sauce** and something green.

- **Mashed potatoes or buttered egg noodles** — the sauce is begging for them
- **Crusty bread** — zero effort, does the job
- **Roasted Brussels sprouts** — 425°F, cut side down, ~20 min. Go in while you salt the chop
- **Green beans** in a hot pan with a little garlic, while the chop rests

**The easy solo default:** bread + a handful of green beans thrown in the pan *before* the apples. Nothing extra to wash.
