# 🍋 Chicken Piccata Florentine
*Inspired by a local restaurant — home-friendly version*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Fresh spinach
- 1 lemon

**Meat**
- 2 chicken breasts

**Dairy**
- Heavy cream
- Butter

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Orzo (bag — you'll use about 1 cup dry)
- Capers (small jar)
- Chicken broth

*You should have at home: olive oil, flour for dredging, salt, pepper*

---

## Recipe (2 servings)

**Ingredients**

*Chicken*
- 2 chicken breasts, sliced horizontally into medallions
- 2–3 tbsp flour (for dredging)
- Salt and pepper
- 1 tbsp olive oil per batch (don't eyeball it — too much and they'll swim)

*Lemon Caper Sauce*
- ½ cup chicken broth
- Juice of 1 lemon
- 1½ tbsp capers
- 2 tbsp butter

*Orzo*
- 1 cup dry orzo
- 2 handfuls fresh spinach
- 4–5 tbsp heavy cream
- 1 tbsp butter
- Salt

**Steps**

1. **Prep.** Slice chicken breasts into medallions: cut each breast in half lengthwise first (separating the thick end from the thin end). The thin end is already the right thickness — leave it. Fillet the thick end horizontally into two thinner pieces to match. You'll get ~3 pieces of roughly equal thickness per breast, which means they all cook evenly. Season with salt and pepper. For the dredge: spread a couple tablespoons of flour on a plate, lay each piece in it, flip once, then shake off anything that doesn't stick. You want a thin dusting — still able to see the chicken through it, not a thick coating.

2. **Start orzo.** Cook orzo like pasta in salted boiling water according to package directions. Drain and set aside. *(Time it to finish around when the chicken is done.) Optional: toast the dry orzo in a little butter for 2–3 min before boiling for a nuttier flavor.*

3. **Sear chicken.** Heat 1 tbsp olive oil per batch in a skillet over medium-high heat — let the pan preheat for 3–4 minutes before the chicken goes in (longer if you have electric). The oil is ready when it shimmers. If your "medallions" are large thick chunks (1+ lb breasts), pound them to even thickness first. Pat each piece dry with a paper towel before it goes in — moisture kills browning. Don't crowd the pan; do two batches if needed, adding another tbsp of oil for the second batch. Once the chicken is down, **leave it alone** — it'll stick at first and release on its own when it has color (~3–4 min). If it resists when you try to flip, it's not ready. Flip once, cook the other side. Remove and set aside — don't wipe the pan.

4. **Make sauce.** In the same pan over medium heat, add ½ cup broth to deglaze — scrape up all the brown bits from the bottom. Add the lemon juice, 1½ tbsp capers, and 2 tbsp butter. Let it simmer and reduce for ~5 minutes until slightly thickened.

5. **Finish orzo.** Stir spinach into the warm drained orzo — it wilts in about 30 seconds. Add 4–5 tbsp cream, 1 tbsp butter, and a pinch of salt, stir to combine.

6. **Serve.** Divide orzo into bowls. Lay chicken medallions on top, spoon sauce generously over everything.
