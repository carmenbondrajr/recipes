# 🐷 Porchetta-ish
### Fennel-Rosemary Roast Pork Sandwiches with Salsa Verde

*One roast makes 4–6 sandwiches. Rub it the night before, roast it on Sunday, and you have lunch for the week. About 25 minutes of hands-on work, an hour in the oven, and 5 minutes to build each sandwich. It's "-ish" because real porchetta is a whole pork belly rolled around the loin. This keeps the flavor and skips the butchery.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Fresh rosemary (1 small pack, ~3 sprigs)
- Flat-leaf parsley (1 bunch)
- Fresh tarragon (1 small pack). If they're out, fresh basil works too. Both bring the anise note
- Lemons (2)
- Garlic (1 head)
- Shallot (1 small)
- Baby arugula (one 5 oz clamshell)

**Meat**
- **Boneless pork loin roast, 2½–3 lb**
  - Look for one with a fat cap on top (~¼ inch). The fat bastes the lean loin while it roasts
  - Get *loin*, not *tenderloin*. Tenderloin is the skinny 1 lb one and cooks in 20 minutes

**Deli / Dairy**
- Sliced sharp provolone (~8 oz, 10–12 slices)

**Bakery**
- Ciabatta rolls (6), or one ciabatta loaf cut into sandwich lengths

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Whole fennel seed.** Whole, not ground. Toasting and crushing it yourself is what makes this taste like porchetta
- Capers (small jar)
- Red wine vinegar

*You should have at home: olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper, red pepper flakes*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The rub*
- 2 tbsp whole fennel seed
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary (~3 sprigs, stripped)
- 6 garlic cloves, grated or finely chopped
- Zest of 1 lemon
- 1 tbsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp red pepper flakes
- 3 tbsp olive oil

*The roast*
- Boneless pork loin roast, 2½–3 lb

*Salsa verde*
- 1 packed cup parsley leaves and tender stems
- 2 tbsp fresh tarragon leaves (or basil)
- 1 small shallot, minced
- 1 garlic clove, grated
- 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
- Zest of 1 lemon, plus 1 tbsp juice
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- ½ cup olive oil
- Pinch of red pepper flakes and kosher salt

*Each sandwich*
- 1 ciabatta roll
- ~4 oz thin-sliced pork (about a big handful)
- 2 slices provolone
- 1–2 tbsp salsa verde
- A handful of arugula

---

### 1️⃣ Rub the pork · ⏱ 15 min, then overnight
- Toast the **2 tbsp fennel seed** in a dry skillet over medium heat for **2–3 minutes**, shaking the pan, until they smell sweet and turn a shade darker. Heat wakes up the oils in the seeds, which turns their flavor from dusty to licorice-y and warm.
- Crush them roughly in a mortar and pestle, or in a zip bag under a rolling pin or the bottom of a heavy pan. You want a mix of cracked seeds and powder, not dust. The cracked bits give you little bursts of fennel in each bite.
- Mix them into a paste with the rest of the rub:
  - **2 tbsp chopped rosemary**
  - **6 garlic cloves**, grated
  - **Zest of 1 lemon**
  - **1 tbsp kosher salt**
  - **1 tsp black pepper**
  - **1 tsp red pepper flakes**
  - **3 tbsp olive oil**
- Pat the **pork** dry. With a sharp knife, score the fat cap in a crosshatch about ¼ inch deep, through the fat but not into the meat. The cuts let the rub sink in and help the fat render and crisp.
- Rub the paste all over the pork, pushing it into the scores.
- Set it fat-side up on a plate and refrigerate it **uncovered overnight** (or at least 4 hours). The salt has time to season the meat all the way to the center, and the dry surface browns much better in the oven.

### 2️⃣ Roast hot, then low · ⏱ ~1 hr 15 min
- Take the pork out of the fridge and heat the oven to **450°F**. The 30 minutes it sits out while the oven heats takes the chill off, so the outside isn't overcooked by the time the center is done.
- Put it **fat-side up** in a 9×13, roasting pan, or oven-safe skillet.
- Roast at **450°F for 15 minutes** to brown the outside and start the fat rendering.
- Without opening the door, turn the oven down to **325°F** and keep roasting. The lower heat cooks it gently and evenly to the center. Start checking the temperature after **35 minutes** at 325°F. Most 3 lb loins take **40–55 minutes**.
- 🌡️ **Pull it at 140°F** in the thickest part. It will rise to about 145°F while it rests. A blush of pink in the middle is correct and safe.
- ⚠️ **Overcooking is what ruins this.** Loin has almost no fat inside, so every degree past 145°F squeezes out juice you can't get back. It goes from juicy to cardboard in about 10 minutes. Trust the thermometer, not the clock.

### 3️⃣ Rest it and make the salsa verde · ⏱ 15 min
- Move the pork to a cutting board and let it rest, uncovered, for **15 minutes**. Resting lets the juices settle back into the meat so they don't all run out onto the board when you cut it.
- **Save the pan drippings.** Scrape them into a small jar. They're salty, fennel-y gold for warming the slices later.
- Meanwhile, chop the salsa verde ingredients **by hand**, not in a food processor. A processor bruises the herbs into a dark green paste, while a knife keeps the texture bright and chunky:
  - **1 packed cup parsley**
  - **2 tbsp tarragon**
  - **1 tbsp capers**, rinsed
- Scrape it into a jar or bowl and stir in the rest:
  - **1 small shallot**, minced
  - **1 garlic clove**, grated
  - **Zest of 1 lemon** + **1 tbsp lemon juice**
  - **1 tbsp red wine vinegar**
  - **½ cup olive oil**
  - **Pinch of red pepper flakes** and salt
- Taste it. It should be bright and a little sharp, since its job is to cut through the rich pork. Add another splash of vinegar if it tastes flat.

### 4️⃣ Chill, then slice thin · ⏱ 3 hrs to overnight, then 10 min
- Once the pork stops steaming, wrap it tightly and refrigerate for **at least 3 hours**, overnight if you can.
- Slice it **as thin as you can**, aiming for about ⅛ inch, with your longest, sharpest knife. Cold meat is firm, so it slices thinner and neater than warm meat. Thin slices are what make this feel like a deli sandwich instead of a pork chop on bread.
- Store the slices in a container with a spoonful of the **drippings** drizzled over them, so they stay moist all week.

### 5️⃣ Build a sandwich · ⏱ 5 min
- Split a **ciabatta roll** and toast it cut-side down in a skillet over medium heat for **1–2 minutes**, until golden. The toasted surface keeps the salsa verde from soaking into the bread.
- Pull the roll out. Add **~4 oz pork slices** to the same skillet with a small spoonful of **drippings** and toss them for **30 seconds**, just to warm them through.
- Pile the slices into a mound, lay **2 slices provolone** over the top, and cover the skillet for **about 1 minute** until the cheese melts.
- Spread **1–2 tbsp salsa verde** on both cut sides of the roll.
- Slide in the cheesy pork, top with a **handful of arugula**, and close it up.
- ⚠️ **Don't overheat the slices.** They're already cooked. Thirty seconds warms them, but two minutes turns them tough and gray.

## Tips & Notes
- **Don't build sandwiches ahead.** Keep the parts separate and assemble each one fresh. Pork, salsa verde, and bread all keep well alone, but together they turn soggy by noon.
- **How long things keep:** sliced pork lasts **4–5 days** in the fridge. Salsa verde lasts about **5 days**. Pour a thin layer of oil over the top and the herbs stay greener.
- **Hot Sunday dinner option:** have the first night's pork straight from the oven, sliced thick, with salsa verde spooned over it. Then chill the rest for sandwiches.
- **Packing it for work?** Pack the toasted roll, the pork + provolone, and the salsa verde + arugula in separate containers. Assemble it at your desk and eat it cold. Cold porchetta on bread is a perfectly respectable Roman lunch.
- **Where's the anchovy?** Classic Italian salsa verde often has one. It's left out here, and the capers bring a similar briny punch.
- **Want more fennel?** Swap the arugula for thinly shaved fresh fennel bulb tossed with lemon and salt. It doubles down on the anise.
- **Broccoli rabe upgrade:** sauté chopped broccoli rabe with garlic, chili flakes, and olive oil, then pile it on instead of arugula. Its bitterness is a classic match for rich pork. Philly's famous roast pork sandwich uses it with sharp provolone.
- **No tarragon or basil?** All-parsley salsa verde is still great, just a little less sweet and herbal.
- **The history:** *porchetta* means "little pig." It comes from central Italy, where a whole pig is deboned, stuffed with herbs, rolled, and roasted for hours. The town of Ariccia, outside Rome, is famous for it and holds an official EU protected status for its version. In Rome it's sold sliced to order from trucks and stalls, stuffed into a plain roll, and eaten standing up. Wild fennel is the signature seasoning, which is why fennel seed does the heavy lifting here.
