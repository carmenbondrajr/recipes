# 🐷 Porchetta-ish
### Instant Pot Fennel-Rosemary Pulled Pork Sandwiches with Blended Salsa Verde

*One Instant Pot, one 3½ lb shoulder, about 8 sandwiches. About 40 minutes of hands-on work, plus 1 hour to overnight for the salt and ~1 hr 35 min under pressure. It's "-ish" because real porchetta is a whole pork belly rolled around the loin. This keeps the fennel, rosemary, garlic and lemon, and lets the pressure cooker do all the tenderizing.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Fresh rosemary (1 small pack, ~3 sprigs)
- Flat-leaf parsley (1 bunch)
- Fresh tarragon (1 small pack). If they're out, fresh basil works too. Both bring the anise note
- Lemons (2)
- Garlic (1 head)
- Yellow onion (1)
- Shallot (1 small)
- Baby arugula (one 5 oz clamshell)

**Meat**
- **Boneless pork shoulder (also labeled Boston butt), 3½ lb**
  - Do not substitute loin. Shoulder's collagen is what melts into silky shreds, and loin doesn't have any
  - Only bone-in at the store? Buy **~4½ lb**, cut the meat off the bone, and throw the bone in the pot for extra body

**Deli / Dairy**
- Sliced sharp provolone (~12 oz, about 16 slices)

**Bakery**
- Ciabatta rolls (8), plus 1 extra roll to tear up for the salsa. Extras freeze well, and so does the pork

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Whole fennel seed.** Whole, not ground. Toasting and cracking it yourself is what makes this taste like porchetta
- Capers (small jar)
- Red wine vinegar
- Dry white wine (½ cup). No wine? Use another ½ cup broth and add a squeeze of lemon at the end
- Low-sodium chicken broth (½ cup)

*You should have at home: olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper, red pepper flakes*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The seasoning*
- 2 tbsp whole fennel seed
- 4 tsp kosher salt (1 tbsp + 1 tsp)
- 1 tsp black pepper
- ¾ tsp red pepper flakes

*The braise*
- Boneless pork shoulder, 3½ lb
- 1 tbsp olive oil, for searing
- 1 yellow onion
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary (~3 sprigs, stripped)
- 6 garlic cloves
- 1 tbsp olive oil, for the paste
- ½ cup dry white wine
- ½ cup low-sodium chicken broth

*The finish*
- Zest of 1 lemon, plus 1 tbsp juice

*Blended salsa verde (about 1 cup)*
- ~1 oz crustless ciabatta (about ½ packed cup, torn)
- 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 1 packed cup parsley leaves and tender stems
- 2 tbsp fresh tarragon leaves (or basil)
- 1 small shallot, roughly chopped
- 1 garlic clove, roughly chopped
- 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
- Zest of 1 lemon, plus 1 tbsp juice
- ⅓ cup olive oil
- Pinch of red pepper flakes and kosher salt

*Each sandwich*
- 1 ciabatta roll
- ~4 oz pulled pork
- 2 slices provolone
- 1–2 tbsp salsa verde
- A handful of arugula

---

### 1️⃣ Season the pork · ⏱ 15 min, then 1 hour to overnight
- Toast the **2 tbsp fennel seed** in a dry skillet over medium heat for **2–3 minutes**, shaking the pan, until they smell sweet and turn a shade darker. Heat wakes up the oils in the seeds, which turns their flavor from dusty to warm and licorice-y.
- Crush them roughly in a mortar and pestle, or in a zip bag under a rolling pin or heavy pan. You want a mix of cracked seeds and powder. **Set aside 1 tsp** in a small bowl for the end, since a long pressure cook mutes fennel's top notes.
- Cut the **3½ lb pork** into **6 chunks, about 3 inches each**, following the natural seams where you can. Slice off the thick outer fat cap and any hard white slabs you find, but leave the thin streaks running through the meat. Chunks cook faster than a whole roast, and trimming now is the best defense against greasy pork later.
- Pat the chunks dry and rub them all over with the remaining fennel and:
  - **4 tsp kosher salt**
  - **1 tsp black pepper**
  - **¾ tsp red pepper flakes**
- Set them on a plate and refrigerate **uncovered for at least 1 hour, overnight if you can**. The salt seasons the meat all the way through, and the dry surface browns better.

### 2️⃣ Prep · ⏱ 5 min
- Do this before the pot turns on, because the fond can scorch in the time it takes to prep an onion.
- **Bowl 1, the onion:** quarter the **1 yellow onion** and pulse it on **Chop** in the mini food processor until diced. Stop while you can still see separate pieces, because past that it goes watery.
- **Bowl 2, the paste:** pulse these on **Chop**, scraping down once, into a rough paste. The oil keeps everything moving, and this paste goes in after the sear so the garlic doesn't burn:
  - **2 tbsp rosemary leaves**
  - **6 garlic cloves**
  - **1 tbsp olive oil**
- Measure the **½ cup wine** and **½ cup broth** into one cup and keep it next to the pot.

### 3️⃣ Sear half the pork · ⏱ 10 min
- Set the Instant Pot to **Sauté on high** with **1 tbsp olive oil** and wait for it to shimmer.
- Sear **3 of the chunks** for **3–4 minutes per side** on two sides, until deep golden brown, then move them back to the plate. Half the meat makes plenty of fond to flavor the whole pot, and once it's shredded, nobody can tell which pieces were browned.
- ⚠️ **Watch the fennel.** Toasted seeds go bitter if they turn black. If the bottom of the pot looks close to burnt, add a splash of the wine and scrape.

### 4️⃣ Build the braise · ⏱ 5 min
- Add **Bowl 1 (the onion)** with a pinch of salt and cook for **3 minutes**, scraping as it releases its water.
- Add **Bowl 2 (the paste)** and stir for **30 seconds**, just until it smells great.
- Pour in the **wine and broth**, and scrape the entire bottom of the pot until it's smooth.
- ⚠️ **Scrape like you mean it.** Anything stuck to the bottom is what triggers the burn notice.
- Turn off Sauté and nestle in **all 6 chunks**, plus any juices from the plate. It will look like too little liquid, but the pork releases plenty as it cooks.

### 5️⃣ Pressure cook · ⏱ ~1 hr 35 min, hands-off
- Lock the lid, set the valve to Sealing, and cook on **High Pressure for 60 minutes**.
- Let it **natural release for 20 minutes**, then quick release the rest. Venting right away would make the meat seize up and lose its juices.
- ⚠️ **If a chunk resists two forks, it's undercooked, not overcooked.** Seal it back up for **10 more minutes** with a 10-minute natural release. Tough shoulder needs more time, and it won't dry out the way loin does.

### 6️⃣ Blend the salsa verde · ⏱ 10 min, during the natural release
- In a small bowl, soak the **~1 oz torn crustless ciabatta** in the **2 tbsp red wine vinegar** for **5 minutes**. This is the classic Italian trick: the bread gives the sauce body, so it spreads and stays put on the sandwich instead of running out.
- Pulse these in the mini food processor on **Chop** until very finely chopped:
  - **1 packed cup parsley**
  - **2 tbsp tarragon**
  - **1 tbsp capers**, rinsed
  - **1 small shallot**, roughly chopped
  - **1 garlic clove**, roughly chopped
- Add the rest and pulse, scraping down twice, until it's thick and spreadable like pesto:
  - **The soaked bread** and any vinegar left in the bowl
  - **Zest of 1 lemon** + **1 tbsp lemon juice**
  - **⅓ cup olive oil**
  - **Pinch of red pepper flakes** and salt
- ⚠️ **Use short pulses, and don't let it run.** A long spin heats and bruises the herbs, which turns them dark and bitter.
- Taste it. It should be bright and a little sharp, since its job is to cut through the rich pork. Add a splash of vinegar if it tastes flat, or **1 tbsp cold water** if it's too stiff to spread.

### 7️⃣ Pull, defat, and reduce · ⏱ 25 min
- Move the pork to a big bowl or sheet pan with tongs, and shred it with two forks. Pick out and discard any soft lumps of fat as you go. ⚠️ **This is the step that decides whether it tastes rich or greasy**, so be picky.
- Pour the pot liquid into a fat separator or a tall jar and wait **10 minutes**. Spoon or pour off the clear fat layer on top until you've removed nearly all of it.
- Return the defatted liquid to the pot and simmer on **Sauté on high** for **10–15 minutes**, until reduced by about half. This concentrates it into a glossy, savory jus.
- Toss the pork with **about 1 cup of the jus**, the **reserved 1 tsp cracked fennel**, the **zest of 1 lemon**, and **1 tbsp lemon juice**. Adding these raw at the end puts back the brightness that pressure cooking takes out. Taste, and add salt only now, since the jus concentrates as it reduces.
- Save the leftover jus in a jar for reheating.

### 8️⃣ Build a sandwich · ⏱ 5 min
- Split a **ciabatta roll** and toast it cut-side down in a skillet over medium heat for **1–2 minutes**, until golden. The toasted surface keeps the salsa from soaking into the bread.
- Pull the roll out. Add **~4 oz pulled pork** to the same skillet with a small spoonful of **jus** and warm it for **1–2 minutes**.
- Pile the pork into a mound, lay **2 slices provolone** over the top, and cover the skillet for **about 1 minute** until the cheese melts.
- Slide the cheesy pork onto the bottom half of the roll, and spread **1–2 tbsp salsa verde** on top of the cheese.
- Add a **handful of arugula** and close it up.

## Tips & Notes
- **Why shoulder.** Loin is lean, so it only stays tender in a narrow temperature window, and cold thick slices eat chewy. Shoulder is the opposite: long moist heat melts its collagen and most of its fat into the meat. Serve it warm and shredded, because cold shoulder fat goes waxy.
- **What ruins this: skipping the defatting.** Shoulder gives off a lot of fat, and it all ends up in the pot. If you have the time, refrigerate the liquid overnight and lift the solid fat off in one piece. That's the cleanest way to do it.
- **What else ruins it: rushing the release.** Give it the full 20 minutes.
- **Make it ahead.** The pork is better the next day. Store it with its jus so it stays moist, and always reheat it with a spoonful of jus.
- **How long things keep:** cooked pork lasts **4–5 days** in the fridge or **3 months** in the freezer. Freeze it with some jus. Salsa verde lasts about **5 days**, and a thin layer of oil on top keeps it greener.
- **Don't build sandwiches ahead.** Keep the parts separate and assemble each one fresh, or the bread goes soggy.
- **No Instant Pot?** Use the 10-inch Dutch oven. Sear and build the braise the same way, cover, and bake at **300°F for 3–3½ hours**, until the chunks shred easily.
- **Where's the anchovy?** Classic Italian salsa verde often has one. It's left out here, and the capers bring a similar briny punch.
- **Want more fennel?** Swap the arugula for thinly shaved fresh fennel bulb tossed with lemon and salt. It doubles down on the anise.
- **Broccoli rabe upgrade:** sauté chopped broccoli rabe with garlic, chili flakes, and olive oil, then pile it on instead of arugula. Its bitterness is a classic match for rich pork, and Philly's famous roast pork sandwich uses it with sharp provolone.
- **No tarragon or basil?** All-parsley salsa verde is still great, just a little less sweet and herbal.
- **The history:** *porchetta* means "little pig." It comes from central Italy, where a whole pig is deboned, stuffed with herbs, rolled, and roasted for hours. The town of Ariccia, outside Rome, is famous for it and holds an official EU protected status for its version. In Rome it's sold sliced to order from trucks and stalls, stuffed into a plain roll, and eaten standing up. Wild fennel is the signature seasoning, which is why fennel seed does the heavy lifting here.
