# 🥔 Crispy Pan-Seared Gnocchi with Brown Butter, Meatballs & Red Sauce

*One large skillet, one saucepan, and the oven, ~30 minutes, serves 2. Frozen gnocchi seared straight from the bag, finished in garlicky brown butter with fennel and bee balm, and piled over saucy meatballs.*

## Shopping List

**From the yard**
- Dried bee balm / wild bergamot (½ tsp crumbled)
  - No bee balm on hand? Dried oregano is the easy swap and works great

**Produce**
- Garlic (3 cloves)
- Lemon (1). You only need the zest
- Fresh basil (a small bunch, ~8 leaves)

**Dairy**
- Unsalted butter (3 tbsp)
- Shredded parmesan (½ cup)

**Frozen**
- **Gnocchi (one 16 oz bag)**
  - Shelf-stable vacuum-packed gnocchi also sears well, if that's what the store has
  - Skip refrigerated fresh gnocchi. It's too soft to hold a crust
- **Meatballs (12).** Any style works, and Italian-seasoned beef/pork is the default. Most bags have well over 12

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Jarred red sauce (one standard 24 oz jar)
- Fennel seed. Check yours first: if it doesn't smell like licorice when you crush one, buy a fresh jar
- Red pepper flakes

*You should have at home: neutral oil (canola, grapeseed, light olive), kosher salt*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*Meatballs & sauce*
- 12 frozen meatballs
- 24 oz jarred red sauce

*Gnocchi*
- 16 oz frozen gnocchi (keep frozen until it hits the pan)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- ½ tsp kosher salt

*Brown butter*
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- ½ tsp fennel seed, lightly crushed
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes
- ½ tsp dried bee balm (or oregano), crumbled

*To finish*
- Zest of 1 lemon
- 8 fresh basil leaves, torn
- ½ cup shredded parmesan

---

### 1️⃣ Start the meatballs · ⏱ ~30 min, mostly hands-off
- Heat the oven to **350°F** and bake the **12 frozen meatballs** for about **20 minutes**, or whatever the bag says. Package directions win, since they're calibrated to that particular meatball.
- Start this before anything else. Preheat plus bake is the long pole, and everything else fits inside it.
- Meanwhile, warm the **24 oz red sauce** in a saucepan over **low** heat.
- When the meatballs come out, slide them into the sauce to hold until you plate. Baking browns the outsides, and they don't water down the sauce the way simmering from frozen would.

### 2️⃣ Prep the aromatics · ⏱ 5 min
- While the meatballs bake, get the brown-butter mix and the finishers ready:
  - Slice the **3 garlic cloves** thin
  - Lightly crush the **½ tsp fennel seed** under the flat of a knife
  - Crumble the **½ tsp dried bee balm** between your palms. Crushing it wakes up the oils, which is where the flavor lives
  - Zest the **1 lemon**
  - Tear the **8 basil leaves**
- Set it all within arm's reach of the stove. Once the gnocchi hit the pan, things move fast and there's no time to go hunting for the zester.

### 3️⃣ Sear the gnocchi · ⏱ 4–5 min
- Time this so the meatballs have about 10 minutes left in the oven.
- Heat the **2 tbsp oil** in a large skillet over **medium-high** until it shimmers.
- Add the **16 oz gnocchi** straight from frozen in a single layer, with space between them. If they won't fit, sear in two batches and hold the first on a plate. They stay crisp for several minutes.
- Season with the **½ tsp kosher salt**, then **don't touch them.** Moving them early tears off the crust that's trying to form.
- ⚠️ **Crowding is what ruins this.** Gnocchi packed together trap steam, and you get pale, gummy dumplings instead of crisp ones. Pale and soft after 5 minutes means the pan was too full.

### 4️⃣ Brown the second side · ⏱ 3–4 min
- Toss or flip, then leave them alone again so the other side blisters.
- You want deep golden in patches, not evenly tan. While the outsides crisp, the insides steam themselves soft and chewy.

### 5️⃣ Brown the butter and bloom the aromatics · ⏱ 2 min
- Drop the heat to **medium** and add the **3 tbsp butter**.
- Once it foams, add everything from the aromatics pile:
  - **3 sliced garlic cloves**
  - **½ tsp crushed fennel seed**
  - **¼ tsp red pepper flakes**
  - **½ tsp crumbled bee balm**
- Swirl the pan constantly. You want the milk solids in the butter going nutty brown and the garlic just turning pale gold. Dried herbs and spices need fat and heat to release their oils, which is why they go in here and not at the end.
- Pull the pan off the burner the moment the butter smells like toasted hazelnuts.
- ⚠️ **Butter goes from brown to burnt in seconds.** If the bits go black and it smells sharp instead of nutty, the bitterness won't cook out. Wipe the pan and redo the butter.

### 6️⃣ Finish off the heat · ⏱ 1 min
- Still off the burner, add the **lemon zest** and half the basil (**4 leaves**) and toss.
- Basil and zest are both delicate. Direct heat turns basil black and cooks the brightness out of the zest, so they go in last.

### 7️⃣ Plate in layers
- Spoon the saucy meatballs into bowls first, then pile the gnocchi on top rather than stirring everything together.
- Shower with the **½ cup parmesan** and the last **4 basil leaves**.
- Keeping the gnocchi on top means they stay crisp until the last few bites instead of going soft the second they touch sauce.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: steamed gnocchi.** Crowding, a pan that isn't hot yet, or fiddling with them too early all lead to the same soft, pale result. A big pan and patience fix all three.
- **Cast iron or stainless sears best.** Nonstick works but gives a paler crust.
- **Don't salt beyond the gnocchi.** Jarred sauce and parmesan bring plenty.
- **Want it fully tossed?** Stir about half the sauce into the gnocchi off the heat right before serving and eat immediately. It won't stay crisp, so it's a commit-now decision.
- **No oven?** Simmer the meatballs covered in the sauce for 20–25 minutes instead. Start them first either way. They'll be softer and the sauce a little thinner.
- **Bee balm vs. bergamot:** the dried herb is bee balm (*Monarda*), a mint-family plant that tastes like oregano with a citrusy edge. It's not the bergamot orange in Earl Grey, though the crushed leaves smell like it, which is where the name came from. The lemon zest plays up that citrus side.
- **The name:** *gnocchi* likely comes from *nocchio*, a knot in wood, or *nocca*, knuckle. Little lumpy dumplings, named for little lumps. Potato gnocchi are the newcomers, since potatoes didn't reach Italy until the 1500s. Older versions were made from bread, flour, or semolina.
