# 🥔 Crispy Pan-Seared Gnocchi with Brown Butter, Meatballs & Red Sauce
*Frozen gnocchi seared straight from the bag, finished in garlicky brown butter, piled over saucy meatballs — 2-3 servings, ~25 minutes*

---

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Garlic (3 cloves)
- Lemon (1) — you only need the zest
- Fresh basil (small bunch, ~8 leaves)

**Dairy**
- Unsalted butter
- Shredded parmesan (1/2 cup)

**Frozen**
- Gnocchi (16 oz / one bag) — *shelf-stable vacuum-packed gnocchi also sears well; skip refrigerated fresh gnocchi, it's too soft*
- Meatballs (12) — any style; Italian-seasoned beef/pork is the default

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Jarred red sauce (24 oz — one standard jar)
- Neutral oil (canola, grapeseed, light olive)
- Fennel seed
- Red pepper flakes
- Dried bergamot — *hard to find; dried oregano is the easy swap and works great*

*You should have at home: kosher salt*

---

## Recipe (2-3 servings)

**Ingredients**
- 16 oz frozen gnocchi (keep frozen)
- 12 frozen meatballs
- 24 oz jarred red sauce
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1/2 tsp fennel seed, lightly crushed
- 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes
- 1/2 tsp dried bergamot (or oregano), crumbled
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- Zest of 1 lemon
- 8 fresh basil leaves, torn
- 1/2 cup shredded parmesan

**Steps**

1. **Start the meatballs (5 min hands-on, 20 min simmering).** Pour the red sauce into a saucepan and add the meatballs straight from frozen. Bring to a bare simmer, cover, and let them heat through while you do everything else. Frozen meatballs need real time to come up to temperature in the center, so this is the long pole — start it first.

2. **Prep the aromatics (5 min).** While the sauce heats: slice the garlic thin, lightly crush the fennel seed, zest the lemon, and tear the basil. Set it all within arm's reach. Once the gnocchi hit the pan things move fast and there's no time to go hunting for the zester.

3. **Sear the gnocchi (4-5 min).** Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Add the gnocchi straight from frozen in a single layer — if they won't fit without crowding, do two batches. Season with the kosher salt and then **do not touch them.** Moving them early tears off the crust that's trying to form.

4. **Flip and brown the second side (3-4 min).** Toss or flip and let the other side blister. You want deep golden in patches, not uniformly tan. The insides are steaming themselves soft and chewy while this happens.

5. **Brown the butter and bloom the aromatics (2 min).** Drop the heat to medium and add the butter. Once it foams, add the garlic, fennel seed, red pepper flakes, and bergamot. Swirl the pan constantly — you want the milk solids going nutty brown and the garlic just turning pale gold. Dried herbs need fat and heat to release their oils, which is why they go in here and not at the end. Pull the pan the moment the butter smells like toasted hazelnuts; it goes from brown to burnt in seconds.

6. **Finish off the heat (1 min).** Off the burner, add the lemon zest and half the basil and toss. Basil and zest are both volatile — direct heat turns the basil black and cooks the brightness out of the zest, so they go in last.

7. **Plate in layers.** Spoon the saucy meatballs into bowls first, then pile the gnocchi on top rather than stirring everything together. Shower with the parmesan and the remaining basil. Keeping the gnocchi on top means they stay crisp until the last few bites instead of going soft the moment they hit the sauce.

---

## Tips & Notes
- **Pan size matters more than anything else here.** A crowded skillet steams instead of sears and you end up exactly where you were trying not to be. If your largest pan is 10 inches, sear in two batches and hold the first on a plate — they stay crisp for several minutes.
- **Don't salt beyond the gnocchi.** Jarred sauce and parmesan bring plenty.
- **Burnt butter is the main failure mode.** If the milk solids go black and it smells sharp instead of nutty, wipe the pan and start the butter step over — the bitterness won't cook out.
- **Want it fully tossed?** Stir in about half the sauce off the heat right before serving and eat immediately. It won't stay crisp, so this is a commit-now decision.
- **Cast iron or stainless sears best.** Nonstick works but gives you a paler crust.
- **On bergamot:** the dried herb is bee balm (*Monarda*), a mint-family plant that tastes like oregano with a citrusy edge — not the bergamot orange in Earl Grey. Oregano plus the lemon zest already in the dish gets you most of the way there.
