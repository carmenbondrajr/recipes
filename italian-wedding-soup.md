# 🥣 Italian Wedding Soup

*One big pot plus a small one for the pasta, ~1 hour, makes about 5 bowls. A single 1 lb batch of tiny parmesan meatballs simmered in chicken broth with escarole and **acini di pepe**, the little peppercorn-shaped pasta.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- 1 yellow onion
- 2 carrots
- 2 celery stalks
- 1 garlic head (5 cloves total)
- **Greens: 1 small head escarole, or one 5 oz bag baby spinach**
  - Escarole is mildly bitter and holds its shape. Spinach is mild and melts into the broth
  - Can't decide? Grab both and do half and half
- Fresh parsley (1 small bunch)
- 1 lemon

**Meat — 1 lb**
- **1 lb meatloaf mix** (beef/pork/veal), if your store has it. The pork and veal make the most tender meatballs
  - No meatloaf mix: **1 lb ground pork** is the next best, or **1 lb 80/20 ground beef** for a simpler, slightly leaner meatball
  - **Pivot: 1 lb ground chicken or turkey.** Lighter, more broth-forward soup. Grab **dark meat/thigh** if it's there, and plan on adding 1–1½ tbsp olive oil to the mix (see Tips)

**Dairy**
- Grated parmesan — **1 cup** (½ cup for the meatballs, ½ cup for the table)
- 1 egg

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Plain breadcrumbs (½ cup)
- Acini di pepe (¾ cup dry)
- Chicken broth — **8 cups / 64 oz**. That's exactly two 32 oz cartons

*You should have at home: olive oil, milk, kosher salt, black pepper, bay leaves*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The meatballs (~45)*
- 1 lb ground meat
- ½ cup breadcrumbs
- 6 tbsp milk (⅓ cup plus a splash), more if needed
- ½ cup grated parmesan
- 1 egg
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- Black pepper

*The soup*
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, 2 carrots, 2 celery stalks, all finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 8 cups chicken broth
- 2 bay leaves
- ¾ cup acini di pepe
- Escarole (1 small head, chopped) or about 4 oz baby spinach
- ½–1 lemon, kosher salt, black pepper
- ½ cup parmesan for the table

---

### 1️⃣ Soak the breadcrumbs · ⏱ 10 min
- Heat the oven to **400°F** and line a sheet pan with parchment.
- Stir the **½ cup breadcrumbs** into the **6 tbsp milk** and let them sit the full 10 minutes. This is a *panade*, and it's the whole difference between tender meatballs and little rubber pellets: the starch traps moisture that would otherwise squeeze out as the meat cooks.
- Go by texture, not the measurement. You want a loose, spreadable paste like wet oatmeal. If it still holds its shape or looks crumbly, add milk a splash at a time until it slumps.
- ⚠️ **An under-hydrated panade is the quiet failure.** Dry crumbs can't hold water they never absorbed, so the meatballs come out dense. Erring wet is safe, since the meat soaks up the extra.

### 2️⃣ Mix and roll the meatballs · ⏱ 15 min
- Add everything to the panade:
  - **1 lb ground meat**
  - **½ cup parmesan**
  - **1 egg**
  - **2 cloves minced garlic**
  - **2 tbsp parsley**
  - **1 tsp kosher salt**
  - A few grinds of black pepper
- Mix with your hands only until it comes together. Overworking compacts the protein and toughens the meatballs.
- ⚠️ **Don't skimp on the salt.** The broth gets seasoned at the end, but the meatballs are seasoned once and never again. Under-salted meatballs in a well-seasoned broth taste bland, and there's no fixing it later.
- On the lined sheet pan, press the mix into a ¾"-thick rectangle and cut a **6 × 8 grid**. Roll each square into a ball, about 1" across. Every ball comes out the same size, and it's much faster than scooping. Keep your hands wet with cold water so the meat doesn't stick.
- Keep them small. These have to fit on a spoon next to the pasta, and a tablespoon scoop makes them too big.

### 3️⃣ Bake the meatballs · ⏱ 12–15 min
- Spread the meatballs out on the sheet pan and bake at **400°F for 12–15 minutes**, until lightly browned. Baking browns them all at once with no batches, and it firms them up so they hold together in the simmer.
- Start the vegetables while they bake.

### 4️⃣ Sweat the vegetables · ⏱ 8 min
- Heat **2 tbsp olive oil** in a big pot over **medium**.
- Add the **diced onion, carrots, and celery** with a pinch of salt and cook until soft and the onion is translucent.
- Add the **3 cloves minced garlic** for 1 minute more.
- No browning needed. This soup wants a clean, light broth, not a deep fond.

### 5️⃣ Simmer the meatballs · ⏱ 10 min
- Add the **8 cups broth** and **2 bay leaves** and bring it to a gentle simmer.
- Slide in the baked meatballs and keep it at a lazy simmer for 10 minutes so they soak up some broth and give some flavor back.
- ⚠️ **No hard boil.** A rolling boil knocks the meatballs apart.
- Put a small pot of salted water on to boil for the pasta.

### 6️⃣ Cook the acini di pepe on the side · ⏱ 8–9 min
- Boil the **¾ cup acini di pepe** in the small pot until tender, then drain.
- It cooks separately because the tiny pasta drinks broth like a sponge and keeps going even after it's done. Cooked in the soup pot, it turns the soup thick and stewy fast.

### 7️⃣ Wilt the greens · ⏱ 2 min
- Add the **chopped escarole** (or **about 4 oz spinach**, most of the bag) to the soup pot. It'll look like way too much for a second, then collapse into almost nothing.
- Cook just until wilted.

### 8️⃣ Finish and serve
- Fish out the bay leaves.
- Squeeze in the **juice of ½ lemon**, then taste. Add more lemon and salt until the broth tastes bright and seasoned, not flat.
- Spoon some acini into each bowl and ladle the soup over it. **½ cup parmesan** goes on the table.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: bland or dense meatballs.** Both get decided before anything hits the heat. Salt them properly (1 tsp per lb), and give the panade its full soak.
- **Shredded parm instead of grated?** Fine. Pack the measuring cup a little extra (shreds are fluffier) and rough-chop them so they spread through the meat instead of forming cheesy pockets.
- **Half-and-half swaps 1:1 for the milk.** Heavy cream works too, but it's thick and soaks in slowly. Thin it with a tablespoon of water and give it a few extra minutes.
- **Cream buys richness, not tenderness.** Tenderness comes from starch holding water, so you can't fix a dry meatball by adding fat.
- **Chicken or turkey meatballs:** lean meat dries out fast, so use dark meat/thigh if you can, add **1–1½ tbsp olive oil** to the mix, keep the panade, and pull them from the oven a minute or two early.
- **A parmesan rind** dropped in with the broth is the best free upgrade here: savory depth, no new flavor direction. Fish it out with the bay leaves.
- **Bay leaves, not rosemary.** Bay adds background warmth and stays quiet. Rosemary is piney and assertive and will bully this broth.
- **Egg ribbons, if you ever want them:** whisk **1 egg** with **¼ cup parmesan**. With the soup at a *bare* simmer, drizzle it in a thin stream while stirring slowly in one direction. It sets on contact into silky strands, like *stracciatella*. Ladle out a bowl first if you want to test it.
- **Buttered bread for dipping** is not optional, apparently.
- **The name:** there's no wedding. "Italian wedding soup" is a fumbled translation of *minestra maritata*, "married soup." The marriage is **the greens and the meat**: bitter greens cutting through rich pork. That's why escarole is traditional. Its bitterness is doing a job.
