# 🥣 Italian Wedding Soup

Makes about 6-8 servings. Uses **acini di pepe** — the tiny peppercorn-shaped pasta.

**The meatballs are always a double batch.** You make ~90, use half in tonight's soup, and freeze
the other half raw. Next time this is a 25-minute weeknight soup.

- 2 eggs

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Plain breadcrumbs (1 cup)

- Chicken broth — **10 cups / 80 oz**. That's three 32 oz cartons (you'll have ~2 cups left), or one 48 oz + one 32 oz

## Recipe

**Meatballs** — makes ~90, half go in the soup
- 2 lbs ground meat (1 lb beef + 1 lb pork)
- 1 cup breadcrumbs + ¾ cup milk (more if needed — see step 1)
- 1 cup grated parmesan
- 2 eggs
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- ¼ cup chopped parsley
- **2 tsp kosher salt** — don't shortchange this, see step 2
- Black pepper

**Soup**
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, 2 carrots, 2 celery stalks — all finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic
- 10 cups chicken broth
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 cup acini di pepe
- Escarole or spinach — about 80% of the bag/head, eyeballed
- Lemon, kosher salt, black pepper, parmesan for the table

### 1. Soak the Breadcrumbs — 10 min
Stir the breadcrumbs into the milk and let them sit. This is a *panade*, and it's the whole difference between tender meatballs and little rubber pellets — the starch traps moisture that would otherwise squeeze out as the meat cooks.

**Go by texture, not the measurement.** You want a loose, spreadable paste — wet oatmeal, not damp crumbs. If it still holds its shape or looks crumbly, add milk a splash at a time until it slumps. Erring wet is safe; the meat absorbs the excess. Erring dry means the panade can't do its job.

Give it the full 10 minutes — dried breadcrumbs hydrate slower than they look like they should.

**Half-and-half or heavy cream work too** — see Tips.

### 2. Mix & Roll the Meatballs — ~20 min
Combine meat, soaked breadcrumbs, parmesan, eggs, minced garlic, parsley, **2 tsp salt**, and pepper. **Mix with your hands only until it comes together** — overworking compacts the protein and toughens them.

**Salt is the one thing to get right.** Everything else in this soup gets seasoned at the end, but the meatballs are seasoned once and never again — under-salt them and you get bland meatballs floating in a well-seasoned broth, which you can't fix later. 2 tsp for 2 lbs.

Roll into **~1" balls** — about 2 teaspoons of meat each. Bigger balls are a different soup; these need to fit on a spoon alongside the pasta.

**Fastest way to portion them:** press the mix into a ¾"-thick rectangle on a sheet pan, cut a grid, and roll each square. Every ball comes out the same size and it beats scooping. Keep your hands wet with cold water so the meat doesn't stick.

**Split the batch here.** Freeze half raw on a sheet pan, then bag them once solid. Bake the other half at 400°F for 12-15 min, or brown them in the soup pot and set aside.

### 3. Sweat the Vegetables — ~8 min
Olive oil in a big pot over medium. Onion, carrot, celery until soft and the onion is translucent. Add remaining garlic, 1 min more. No browning needed — this soup wants a clean, light broth, not a fond.

### 4. Simmer the Meatballs — 10 min
Add broth and bay leaves, bring to a gentle simmer, drop in the meatballs. Keep it at a lazy simmer; a hard boil knocks them apart.

### 5. Pasta — 8-9 min
Add the acini di pepe directly to the pot **only if you're eating the whole batch tonight.** Otherwise cook it separately (see Tips). Stir once or twice so it doesn't clump on the bottom.

### 6. Greens — 2 min
Dump in **about 80% of the bag** (or most of the head, chopped) — eyeball it, this isn't precise. It'll look like way too much for a second and then collapse into almost nothing. Cook just until wilted.

### 7. Finish
Fish out the bay leaves. Squeeze in lemon juice, taste, adjust salt. Parmesan at the table.

## Tips & Notes
- **Salt the meatballs properly** — 2 tsp per 2 lbs. It's the only seasoning they ever get
- **Acini di pepe drinks broth like a sponge.** Cooking it in the pot is fine for a same-night batch, but for leftovers boil it separately and spoon it into each bowl — otherwise day two is a casserole
- **Roll them small** — ~1", about 2 tsp of meat, or the spoon math doesn't work. A tablespoon scoop makes them too big
- **Shredded parm instead of grated?** Fine — pack the cup a little extra (shreds are fluffier) and rough-chop or pulse them so they disperse instead of forming cheesy pockets
- **Half-and-half swaps 1:1 for the milk.** Heavy cream works too, but it's thick and soaks in slower and unevenly — thin it with a couple tbsp of water and give it a few extra minutes
- **Under-hydrated panade is the quiet failure.** Dry crumbs can't hold water they never absorbed, so the meatballs come out denser. Let it sit the full 10 min and go by texture — loose paste, not damp crumbs
- **Cream buys richness, not tenderness.** The tenderness comes from starch holding water, so you can't fix a dry meatball by adding fat
- **Don't overmix the meat** — squish just until combined. Easy to overdo at 2 lbs
- **Gentle simmer only** once the meatballs are in
- **Bay leaves, not rosemary.** Bay adds background warmth and won't take over. Rosemary is piney and assertive and will bully this broth — if you use it, one whole sprig pulled after 10 min, never left in overnight
- **A parmesan rind** in the broth is the best free upgrade here — savory depth, no new flavor direction
- **Escarole vs. spinach:** escarole is a chicory — mildly bitter, sturdy ribs, holds its shape. Spinach is mild and melts into the broth. Both good, different soups
- **Try half and half:** one head of escarole plus a handful of spinach. Good way to find out which direction you actually like
- **Egg ribbons, if you ever want them:** whisk 2 eggs with ½ cup parmesan. With the soup at a *bare* simmer — not boiling — drizzle it in a thin stream while stirring slowly in one direction. It sets on contact into silky strands (stracciatella). Low risk: ladle out a bowl first, then try it on the rest of the pot
- **Chicken or turkey meatballs work** — very common in the American version, makes a lighter, more broth-forward soup. Lean meat dries out fast, so: use ground **dark meat/thigh** if you can find it, add **2-3 tbsp olive oil** to the mix, keep the panade, and pull them a touch early — lean goes from done to chalky quickly
- **Frozen meatballs go straight into the broth** — no thawing. Add a few extra minutes at a lazy simmer
- **Buttered bread for dipping** is not optional, apparently
- **Leftovers:** 4-5 days. Freezes well without pasta

## Where the Name Comes From
There's no wedding. "Italian wedding soup" is a fumbled translation of *minestra maritata* — "married soup." The marriage is **the greens and the meat**: bitter greens cutting through rich pork. That's why escarole is traditional — the bitterness is doing a job.
