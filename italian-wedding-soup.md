# 🥣 Italian Wedding Soup

Makes about 6-8 servings. Uses **acini di pepe** — the tiny peppercorn-shaped pasta.

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- 1 yellow onion
- 2 carrots
- 2 celery stalks
  - *Shortcut: pre-diced mirepoix works fine here*
- Escarole (1 small head) or baby spinach (6 oz)
- Fresh parsley
- 1 lemon

**Meat**
- **1 lb total** ground meat — beef/pork blend, or all beef
  - Easiest: a 1 lb package of "meatloaf mix" (beef/pork/veal), if your store has it
  - Otherwise 1 lb ground beef is fine
  - *Or buy 1 lb of each and make a double batch — see freezing tip below*
- **Pivot option: 1 lb ground chicken or turkey.** Common in the American version, lighter soup. Grab **dark meat/thigh** if available, and add 1-2 tbsp olive oil to the mix — see tips

**Dairy**
- Grated parmesan (1 cup total — ½ for meatballs, ½ for finishing)
- 1 egg

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Plain breadcrumbs (½ cup)
- Acini di pepe (¾ cup dry)
- Chicken broth — **10 cups / 80 oz**. That's three 32 oz cartons (you'll have ~2 cups left), or one 48 oz + one 32 oz

*You should have at home: olive oil, garlic, milk, kosher salt, black pepper*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb ground beef/pork mix
- ½ cup breadcrumbs + ¼ cup milk
- ½ cup grated parmesan
- 1 egg
- 5 cloves garlic total (2 for meatballs, 3 for the pot)
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, 2 carrots, 2 celery stalks — all finely diced
- 10 cups chicken broth
- ¾ cup acini di pepe
- 6 oz escarole or spinach, chopped
- Lemon, kosher salt, black pepper

### 1. Soak the Breadcrumbs — 5 min
Stir the breadcrumbs into the milk and let them sit until pasty. This is a *panade*, and it's the whole difference between tender meatballs and little rubber pellets — the starch traps moisture that would otherwise squeeze out as the meat cooks.

**Half-and-half or heavy cream work too** — see Tips.

### 2. Mix & Roll the Meatballs — ~15 min
Combine meat, soaked breadcrumbs, parmesan, egg, 2 cloves minced garlic, parsley, 1 tsp salt, and pepper. **Mix with your hands only until it comes together** — overworking compacts the protein and toughens them.

Roll into **¾" balls**, marble-sized. Bigger balls are a different soup; these need to fit on a spoon alongside the pasta. You'll get 40-50 of them, which sounds like a lot and isn't.

Bake at 400°F for 12-15 min, or brown them in the soup pot and set aside.

### 3. Sweat the Vegetables — ~8 min
Olive oil in a big pot over medium. Onion, carrot, celery until soft and the onion is translucent. Add remaining garlic, 1 min more. No browning needed — this soup wants a clean, light broth, not a fond.

### 4. Simmer the Meatballs — 10 min
Add broth, bring to a gentle simmer, drop in the meatballs. Keep it at a lazy simmer; a hard boil knocks them apart.

### 5. Pasta — 8-9 min
Add the acini di pepe directly to the pot **only if you're eating the whole batch tonight.** Otherwise cook it separately (see Tips). Stir once or twice so it doesn't clump on the bottom.

### 6. Greens — 2 min
Stir in the escarole or spinach and cook just until wilted.

### 7. Finish
Squeeze in lemon juice, taste, adjust salt. Parmesan at the table.

## Tips & Notes
- **Acini di pepe drinks broth like a sponge.** Cooking it in the pot is fine for a same-night batch, but for leftovers boil it separately and spoon it into each bowl — otherwise day two is a casserole
- **Roll them small** — ¾" or the spoon math doesn't work
- **Half-and-half swaps 1:1 for the milk.** Heavy cream works too, but it's thick and soaks in unevenly — thin it (3 tbsp cream + 1 tbsp water) or let it sit an extra few minutes
- **Cream buys richness, not tenderness.** The tenderness comes from starch holding water, so you can't fix a dry meatball by adding fat
- **Don't overmix the meat** — squish just until combined
- **Gentle simmer only** once the meatballs are in
- **Escarole vs. spinach:** escarole is a chicory — mildly bitter, sturdy ribs, holds its shape. Spinach is mild and melts into the broth. Both good, different soups
- **Try half and half:** one head of escarole plus a handful of spinach. Good way to find out which direction you actually like
- **Egg ribbons, if you ever want them:** whisk 2 eggs with ½ cup parmesan. With the soup at a *bare* simmer — not boiling — drizzle it in a thin stream while stirring slowly in one direction. It sets on contact into silky strands (stracciatella). Low risk: ladle out a bowl first, then try it on the rest of the pot
- **Chicken or turkey meatballs work** — very common in the American version, makes a lighter, more broth-forward soup. Lean meat dries out fast, so: use ground **dark meat/thigh** if you can find it, add **1-2 tbsp olive oil** to the mix, keep the panade, and pull them a touch early — lean goes from done to chalky quickly
- **Meatballs freeze great raw** — roll a double batch, freeze on a sheet pan, bag them. Next time this is a 25-minute soup
- **Leftovers:** 4-5 days. Freezes well without pasta

## Where the Name Comes From
There's no wedding. "Italian wedding soup" is a fumbled translation of *minestra maritata* — "married soup." The marriage is **the greens and the meat**: bitter greens cutting through rich pork. That's why escarole is traditional — the bitterness is doing a job.
