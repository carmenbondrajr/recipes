# 🥣 Italian Wedding Soup

*One big pot, no oven, ~1½ hours, makes about 5 bowls. A single 1 lb batch of tiny parmesan chicken meatballs poached raw in a pancetta-enriched chicken broth with escarole and **acini di pepe**, the little peppercorn-shaped pasta.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- 1 yellow onion
- 4 carrots (or about 24 baby carrots)
- 4 celery stalks
- 1 garlic head (5 cloves total)
- **Greens: 1 small head escarole, or one 5 oz bag baby spinach**
  - Escarole is mildly bitter and holds its shape. Spinach is mild and melts into the broth
  - Can't decide? Grab both and do half and half
- Fresh parsley (1 small bunch)
- 1 lemon

**Meat**
- **1 lb ground chicken.** Makes a light meatball that lets the broth lead
  - Grab **dark meat/thigh** if it's there, and skip anything labeled extra lean or breast only, which cooks up dry
  - Ground turkey swaps 1:1
  - **Pivot: 1 lb meatloaf mix** (beef/pork/veal), **ground pork**, or **80/20 ground beef** for a richer, more traditional meatball (see Tips)
- **4 oz diced pancetta.** Usually a 4 oz tub of pre-diced cubes, near the deli or the specialty cheese
  - Only sliced pancetta? Get 4 oz and chop it up
  - **Pivot: 4 slices thick-cut bacon**, diced. It works the same way but adds smoke, which pulls the soup in a different direction

**Dairy**
- Grated parmesan — **1 cup** (½ cup for the meatballs, ½ cup for the table)
- 1 egg

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Plain breadcrumbs (½ cup)
- Acini di pepe — **¾ cup dry**, about a quarter of a 16 oz box
- **Low-sodium** chicken broth — **8 cups / 64 oz**. That's exactly two 32 oz cartons
  - Low-sodium matters here. Pancetta and parmesan both bring a lot of salt, and the broth reduces as the acini drinks it, so regular broth can tip the soup over

*You should have at home: olive oil, milk, kosher salt, black pepper, bay leaves, ice*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The meatballs (~70 small ones)*
- 1 lb ground chicken
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- ½ cup breadcrumbs
- Milk, enough to make a loose paste (roughly ⅔ cup, depending on the crumbs)
- ½ cup grated parmesan
- 1 egg
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- Black pepper

*The soup*
- 4 oz diced pancetta
- Olive oil, only if the pot looks dry
- 1 onion, finely chopped in the food processor
- 4 carrots and 4 celery stalks, hand-chopped into a ¼-inch dice
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 8 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 2 bay leaves
- ¾ cup acini di pepe
- Escarole (1 small head, chopped) or about 4 oz baby spinach
- ½–1 lemon, kosher salt, black pepper
- ½ cup parmesan for the table

---

### 1️⃣ Soak the breadcrumbs · ⏱ 10 min
- Put the **½ cup breadcrumbs** in the big mixing bowl and pour in milk while stirring, until every crumb is soaked and it's a loose, spreadable paste like wet oatmeal. This is a *panade*, and it's the whole difference between tender meatballs and little rubber pellets: the starch traps moisture that would otherwise squeeze out as the meat cooks.
- Let it sit the full 10 minutes, then check again. The crumbs keep drinking as they soak, so a paste that looked right at first often stiffens up. If it holds its shape or looks crumbly, stir in more milk a splash at a time until it slumps.
- ⚠️ **An under-hydrated panade is the quiet failure.** Dry crumbs can't hold water they never absorbed, so the meatballs come out dense. Stop adding milk as soon as it slumps, though. Ground chicken is already wet and soft, and extra milk makes the mix harder to shape.
- While it soaks, pulse **5 garlic cloves** in the mini food processor on **Chop**, scraping down once, until minced. Scoop a little over half (**3 cloves' worth**) into a small bowl for the soup. Add parsley leaves (enough for **2 tbsp chopped**) to what's left and pulse a few more times for the meatballs.

### 2️⃣ Mix the meatballs, then chop while they chill · ⏱ 5 min + ~30 min chill
- Add everything to the panade:
  - **1 lb ground chicken**
  - **1 tbsp olive oil**
  - **½ cup parmesan**
  - **1 egg**
  - **2 cloves minced garlic + 2 tbsp parsley** (the mix from step 1)
  - **1½ tsp kosher salt**
  - A few grinds of black pepper
- The olive oil stands in for the fat that chicken doesn't have. Lean meat dries out fast, and this keeps the meatballs tender.
- Mix with your hands only until it comes together. Overworking compacts the protein and toughens the meatballs.
- ⚠️ **Don't skimp on the salt.** The broth gets seasoned at the end, but the meatballs are seasoned once and never again. Under-salted meatballs in a well-seasoned broth taste bland, and there's no fixing it later. Poaching makes it worse: some salt leaches out into the broth, and there's no browned crust to carry flavor, so these need more than a baked meatball would.
- Cover the bowl and put it in the fridge while you chop and do all of step 3, **about 30 minutes**. These get scooped raw straight into hot broth, so the mix needs to be properly cold. Cold mix holds its shape when it hits the pot. Soft, room-temperature chicken slumps and smears.
- While it chills, cut the **1 onion** into 1-inch chunks and pulse it on **Chop** until finely chopped. Stop while you can still see separate little pieces. It gets its own bowl, because it goes in the pot first.
- Chop the **4 carrots** and **4 celery stalks** by hand into a small dice, about ¼ inch. They can share a bowl.
- Use a knife for these two, not the food processor. The processor minces them so fine that they vanish into the broth, and this soup needs pieces that show up on the spoon. The onion is meant to melt away, so the processor is fine for it.

### 3️⃣ Render the pancetta and sweat the vegetables · ⏱ 22 min
- Put the **4 oz diced pancetta** in the big pot while it's still cold, then turn the heat to **medium**. Cook 6–8 minutes, stirring now and then, until the fat has melted out and the cubes are golden. Starting cold gives the fat time to melt out before the outside browns.
- This is where the broth gets its richness and savory depth. Boxed broth has almost no fat, chicken meatballs barely add any, and poached meatballs never brown, so the pancetta does the work that browning would have done. Without it the soup tastes thin.
- Leave the pancetta and all of its fat in the pot. You want about 2 tbsp of fat. If it looks dry, add a splash of olive oil.
- ⚠️ **Golden, not dark.** If the bits on the bottom of the pot are turning dark brown, turn the heat down. Burnt bits make the whole broth bitter.
- Add the **chopped onion** alone with a pinch of salt and cook about 5 minutes, until it's translucent and smells sweet instead of sharp. Processor onion gives off more harsh, eye-stinging fumes and more water than knife-cut, so it needs this head start to cook them off.
- Go by the smell, not the clock. Onion that still smells sharp here will still taste raw in the finished soup.
- Add the **diced carrots and celery** and cook 8–10 minutes more, until the carrots have started to soften. They finish getting tender in the simmer.
- Add the **3 cloves minced garlic** for 1 minute more.
- Scrape the bottom of the pot as the vegetables give off their water. The golden bits the pancetta left behind dissolve into it, and that's flavor for the broth.

### 4️⃣ Scoop the meatballs straight into the pot · ⏱ ~35 min
- Add the **8 cups broth** and **2 bay leaves** and bring it to a gentle simmer.
- Pull the meatball bowl from the fridge and drop in **one test meatball** first. Let it simmer **4–5 minutes**, then fish it out and cut it open. Only taste it once it's white all the way through, since this is raw chicken. If it's bland, work another pinch of salt into the bowl now. This is your last chance to season them.
- Set a bowl of **ice water** next to the pot, one a little wider than the meatball bowl. You'll dunk the scoop in it, and when you need to set the mix down, rest the meatball bowl on top of the ice water so it stays cold.
- Portion the mix with the **#100 scoop** (2 teaspoons), releasing each one right at the surface of the broth so it slips in instead of splashing. You'll get about 70, each around 1" across, the size of a grape.
- Fill the scoop **level or a little shy, never heaped**. Scrape it flat against the side of the bowl so each one comes out a neat, round ball. Round ones cook up smooth, and ragged ones shed little bits into the broth while they simmer.
- Dunk the scoop in the ice water every few scoops. A wet scoop releases cleanly, and a dry one gums up within three or four meatballs.
- ⚠️ **They should look too small.** Anything bigger won't fit on a spoon next to the pasta, and you end up cutting every meatball in half as you eat. A heaped scoop is already too much.
- **Take your time.** Meatballs already in the pot are fine sitting at a gentle simmer while you scoop the rest. They won't go tough or fall apart, so slow down and make each scoop a nice round one. It usually takes 10–15 minutes.
- Keep the broth at a gentle simmer the whole time. If it goes still, pause until it comes back. Meatballs sitting in sub-simmer broth go soft instead of setting.
- ⚠️ **Don't stir while you scoop, or for 2–3 minutes after the last one goes in.** They set from the outside in, and a spoon knocks them apart before the outside has firmed up. If one lands on another, leave it. They slide apart once they've set.
- After the last one is in, keep it at a lazy simmer for **8–10 minutes**, until one of the last ones you dropped in is white all the way through when you halve it. The first ones will have had longer, and that's fine. Gentle simmering in broth is forgiving, and the panade and olive oil keep them tender.
- 🌡️ **165°F** in the center if you want to check one with the thermometer.
- The broth turns a little cloudy here, and that's the point. Juices, fat, and a bit of panade starch from the raw meatballs stay in the pot and give the broth body, and the acini adds the rest in step 5. Don't bother skimming the foam. It's just protein from the meat, and it stirs back in.
- ⚠️ **No hard boil.** A rolling boil knocks the meatballs apart and turns them tough, especially the first ones, which spend the longest in the pot.
- While it simmers, prep the **1 small head escarole** (spinach needs nothing, skip ahead):
  - Trim off the root end so the leaves fall apart, and toss any ragged outer leaves. Keep the pale ribs, which are the mildest, most tender part.
  - Stack the leaves and cut them into bite-size pieces about 1 inch across, like Caesar salad. Long ribbons drape off the spoon and drag the pasta with them.
  - Wash *after* chopping, and don't skip it. Escarole grows low and holds sandy soil in the base of its leaves, and grit in the broth can't be fixed later. Swish the pieces in a big bowl of cold water, let it sit a minute so the dirt sinks, then lift the greens out with your hands. Pouring through a colander dumps the grit right back on top.
  - No need to dry it, since it's going straight into soup.

### 5️⃣ Cook the acini di pepe in the pot · ⏱ 8–10 min
- Stir the **¾ cup acini di pepe** straight into the simmering soup and cook until tender. Start tasting at 8 minutes. A gentle simmer cooks pasta a little slower than the box time, which assumes a rolling boil.
- The pasta is the thickener. It sheds starch into the broth as it cooks, which turns a thin broth into one with some body.
- Stir gently every minute or so, scraping the bottom. The tiny balls sink and stick.
- The pasta also drinks a cup or more of the broth. If the soup ends up thicker than you like, stir in a splash of water.
- Keep it at the same gentle simmer. A hard boil still breaks up the meatballs.

### 6️⃣ Wilt the greens · ⏱ 2 min
- Add the **chopped escarole** (or **about 4 oz spinach**, most of the bag) to the soup pot. It'll look like way too much for a second, then collapse into almost nothing.
- Cook just until wilted.

### 7️⃣ Finish and serve
- Fish out the bay leaves.
- Squeeze in the **juice of ½ lemon**, then taste before adding any salt, since the pancetta has already seasoned the broth. Add more lemon and salt until it tastes bright and seasoned, not flat.
- Ladle into bowls. **½ cup parmesan** goes on the table.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: bland, dense, or broken meatballs.** All three get decided before anything hits the heat. Salt them properly (1½ tsp per lb), give the panade its full soak, and get the mix properly cold before it meets the broth.
- **Why raw, straight into the pot:** it's the old-school nonna way. No oven, no tray, and poaching makes a softer, more tender meatball than baking. Everything the meatballs give off stays in the soup, which is what gives this broth its body. Baking adds a little browned flavor, but the pancetta covers that side.
- **Shredded parm instead of grated?** Fine. Pack the measuring cup a little extra (shreds are fluffier) and rough-chop them so they spread through the meat instead of forming cheesy pockets.
- **Half-and-half swaps 1:1 for the milk.** Heavy cream works too, but it's thick and soaks in slowly. Thin it with a tablespoon of water and give it a few extra minutes.
- **Cream buys richness, not tenderness.** Tenderness comes from starch holding water, so you can't fix a dry meatball by adding fat.
- **Pork or beef meatballs instead:** use **1 lb meatloaf mix**, ground pork, or 80/20 ground beef. Leave out the 1 tbsp olive oil, since the meat brings its own fat. They're firmer than chicken, so the fridge chill can be shorter, and they need a minute or two longer in the pot.
- **No #100 scoop handy?** Use two wet teaspoons: scoop a level spoonful with one and push it off into the broth with the other. Don't reach for a regular small cookie scoop, which is 1 tbsp and makes them too big.
- **Using the Instant Pot instead of the Dutch oven:** no pressure, just Sauté. The full batch is about 4½ qt (~4.3 L), which fits under the inner pot's 5 L max line with room to stir. Heaped greens can poke above it for a minute before they wilt, and that's fine.
  - Use Sauté on **Normal** for the pancetta and vegetables. **More** runs hot enough to scorch the pancetta fond.
  - Drop to **Less** once the broth is in, for steps 4–5. Normal can push a full pot into a hard boil that breaks up the meatballs.
  - ⚠️ Sauté shuts itself off after about 30 minutes. Restart it right before step 4 so the meatballs never sit in cooling broth, and watch for it cutting out again during the acini.
  - The thin steel bottom sticks more than enamel, so scrape it every minute once the acini goes in.
  - When the soup is done, cancel Sauté and switch off Keep Warm. The pot stays hot and keeps softening the pasta.
- **Leftovers set up thick.** The acini keeps drinking broth in the fridge, so the next day it's closer to a stew. Add a splash of water or broth when you reheat, then re-check the salt and lemon.
- **Cooking mostly for leftovers?** Boil the acini in a separate small pot of salted water, drain it, and spoon it into each bowl instead. The broth comes out thinner, but the pasta stops swelling in the fridge. Store the two separately.
- **A parmesan rind** dropped in with the broth is the best free upgrade here: savory depth, no new flavor direction. Fish it out with the bay leaves.
- **Bay leaves, not rosemary.** Bay adds background warmth and stays quiet. Rosemary is piney and assertive and will bully this broth.
- **Egg ribbons, if you ever want them:** whisk **1 egg** with **¼ cup parmesan**. With the soup at a *bare* simmer, drizzle it in a thin stream while stirring slowly in one direction. It sets on contact into silky strands, like *stracciatella*. Ladle out a bowl first if you want to test it.
- **Buttered bread for dipping** is not optional, apparently.
- **The name:** there's no wedding. "Italian wedding soup" is a fumbled translation of *minestra maritata*, "married soup." The marriage is **the greens and the meat**: bitter greens cutting through rich pork. That's why escarole is traditional. Its bitterness is doing a job. With chicken meatballs, the pancetta is the pork half of the marriage.
