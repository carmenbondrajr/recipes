# 🥓 Tortellini alla Panna con Pancetta e Piselli
*Cheese tortellini in a Parmesan cream sauce with crispy pancetta and peas — serves 2, about 25 minutes*

---

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Garlic (1 clove)

**Meat**
- Pancetta, diced (~4oz / 100g)

**Dairy / Refrigerated**
- Refrigerated cheese tortellini — **one 9–10 oz package** (Buitoni is 9 oz, Rana is 10 oz; either works). Cheese is the classic; prosciutto-filled tortellini doubles down on the pork if that's what's there
- Heavy cream — **one half-pint (1 cup) carton**; you'll use ¾ cup
- Parmigiano-Reggiano, **a small wedge (~4 oz)** — not the pre-grated tub. Look for the dotted rind stamp; domestic "Parmesan" wedge is fine if the real stuff is pricey
- Unsalted butter

**Frozen**
- Frozen peas

*You should have at home: salt, black pepper, a microplane or fine grater*

---

## Recipe (2 servings)

**Ingredients**
- 1 package (9–10 oz) refrigerated cheese tortellini
- 100g (4oz) diced pancetta
- ¾ cup heavy cream
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 60g (2oz) Parmigiano-Reggiano, freshly grated (about 1½ cups fluffy microplaned), plus more for serving
- 1 garlic clove, smashed
- Salt and black pepper

**Steps**

1. **Put the water on & grate the cheese** *(5 min)*. Bring a big pot of salted water to a boil. While it heats, microplane the **60g / 2oz Parmigiano-Reggiano**. Grate it now, not mid-sauce — freshly grated parm is fluffy and fine, so it melts into the cream instantly. Pre-grated tubs are coated in anti-caking powder (cellulose) that keeps it from melting and makes the sauce gritty.

2. **Crisp the pancetta** *(6–8 min)*. Start the **100g / 4oz pancetta** in a cold, dry skillet — no oil. Bring to medium heat so the fat renders out slowly before the meat browns; a hot pan seizes the outside and leaves it chewy. Add the **smashed garlic clove** for the last minute, then fish it out. Scoop the pancetta onto a paper towel, leaving the fat in the pan. **Save a small handful of pancetta aside for topping** — whatever goes back into the cream softens, so this is what keeps the dish actually crispy.

3. **Build the cream sauce** *(4 min)*. Turn heat to medium-low. Add the **1 tbsp butter** to the pancetta fat, then pour in the **¾ cup heavy cream**. Bring to a gentle simmer (small lazy bubbles, not a rolling boil) and let it reduce for 3–4 min until it coats the back of a spoon. Reducing concentrates the cream so it clings instead of pooling at the bottom of the bowl. Add the **1 cup frozen peas** for the last 2 minutes. If it reduces past "coats a spoon" into paste territory, splash in pasta water to loosen it — you can steal a ladle from the pot anytime.

4. **Cook the tortellini** *(2–7 min, per package)*. While the cream reduces, drop the **tortellini** into the boiling water and cook **1 minute less than the package says** — they finish in the sauce. Keep it at a steady boil, not a violent one; hard rolling water bursts the seams. **Scoop out a mug of pasta water** before draining.

5. **Bring it together** *(1–2 min)*. Add the drained tortellini and the pancetta (minus your reserved handful) to the cream. Toss gently over low heat for about a minute with a splash of pasta water. The starch in the water helps the cream and cheese bind into one glossy sauce.

6. **Finish with the Parmesan** *(1 min)*. **Take the pan off the heat**, then add the **60g / 2oz grated parm** in two handfuls, tossing between each. This is the moment that can go wrong: over direct heat, parm proteins tighten into stringy clumps and the sauce turns greasy. Off heat, it melts smooth. Add plenty of black pepper and taste. **It will likely taste like it needs salt — reach for more parm instead of the salt shaker.** An extra handful seasons it *and* thickens the sauce, so it fixes a too-loose sauce and a flat taste in one move. Too thick instead? Loosen with pasta water a tablespoon at a time.

7. **Serve right away.** Top with the reserved crispy pancetta, more grated parm, and cracked pepper. Tortellini keep absorbing the sauce as they sit, so it'll look saucier in the pan than it will be 5 minutes later — that's normal, and why it goes straight to the table.

## Tips & Notes
- **Sauce broke or went greasy?** Heat was too high when the cheese went in. Off heat, whisk in a splash of pasta water or cream — it usually comes back together
- **Sauce too thin?** More parm at the end (or simmer 1 more minute before adding the tortellini). **Too thick?** More pasta water
- **Use unsalted butter — tested and confirmed.** Pancetta and parm carry all the salt this dish needs, and since the finish is "add parm to taste," salted butter would box you in before you get there
- **Leftovers soak up everything.** Reheat on low with a splash of milk or cream to bring the sauce back
- **Bacon works** instead of pancetta — smokier, and you may want to pour off some fat since bacon renders more
- **Frozen tortellini works too** — no need to thaw; just add 2–3 min to the cook time
- A **squeeze of lemon** or some **lemon zest** at the end cuts the richness nicely
- A handful of **baby spinach** wilted in with the peas adds some green
- **A pinch of nutmeg** in the cream is a classic Emilia-Romagna touch — cream + nutmeg is the base of their besciamella
- **The history:** tortellini come from Emilia-Romagna, and Bologna and Modena still argue over who invented them. Legend says an innkeeper in Castelfranco Emilia shaped them after peeking at Venus's navel through a keyhole. Traditionally they're served *in brodo* (in broth) — *tortellini panna, prosciutto e piselli* was the rich, cream-sauced version that swept Italian trattorias in the 1980s, which is basically what this is with pancetta swapped in
