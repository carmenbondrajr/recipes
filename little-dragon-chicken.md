# 🐉 Little Dragon Chicken
### Creamy Tarragon-Dijon Chicken with Crusty Bread

*One large skillet, ~40 minutes, serves 2 generously or 3. A French bistro classic (*poulet à l'estragon*): thin chicken cutlets seared golden, then finished in a creamy pan sauce of shallots, Dijon and lots of fresh tarragon, with plenty of bread for swiping up the sauce. Tarragon goes in twice. The stems simmer in the sauce for depth, and the leaves get stirred in at the end for a bright licorice hit. Stove heat is given as numbers on the dial, same as [Chickpea Chops](chickpea-chops.md).*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- **Fresh tarragon (1 pack, ~¾ oz).** Look for perky, bright green leaves with no black spots. You'll use about 3 tbsp chopped leaves
- Shallots (2 medium)
- Garlic (2 cloves, from a head)
- Lemon (1)

**Meat**
- **Boneless skinless chicken breasts, one standard pack (~1.2–1.4 lb)**, usually 2 or 3 breasts
  - Or grab **thin-sliced cutlets** and skip the slicing

**Dairy**
- **Heavy cream (½ cup).** The smallest carton is ½ pint (1 cup), so you'll use half
- Butter (2 tbsp)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Low-sodium chicken broth (1¼ cups).** One 14.5 oz can or a small carton. Low-sodium matters here, because it reduces down a lot
- Dijon mustard (1 tbsp). Check yours first
- **Crusty bread:** a baguette or a round loaf. Or see Tips for mashed potatoes instead

*You should have at home: all-purpose flour, olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*Chicken*
- 1.2–1.4 lb boneless skinless chicken breasts (2 or 3)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- Black pepper
- ¼ cup all-purpose flour, for dredging
- 2 tbsp olive oil (1 per batch)

*Sauce*
- 1 tbsp butter
- 2 medium shallots, halved and thinly sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, smashed
- 1¼ cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 2 tsp lemon juice, squeezed from half the lemon
- Tarragon stems, from the bunch
- ½ cup heavy cream
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard

*Finish*
- ~3 tbsp fresh tarragon leaves, roughly chopped
- 1 tbsp cold butter
- ½ lemon, plus wedges for serving
- Crusty bread

---

### 1️⃣ Prep · ⏱ 10 min
Sort everything by *when it hits the pan*, so each step is one dump:
- **The chicken:**
  - Lay each breast flat, hold it down with your palm, and slice it **horizontally** into **2 thin cutlets**, about **½ inch** thick. If the thick end is still chunky, press it down with the heel of your hand.
  - Pat them dry and season **both sides** with **1 tsp salt** and pepper.
  - Put the **¼ cup flour** on a plate next to the stove.
  - Even thickness is what keeps breast meat juicy. A cutlet that's thick at one end is dry at the other by the time it's cooked through.
- **Bowl 1, the aromatics:** **2 shallots**, halved through the root and thinly sliced, plus **2 garlic cloves**, smashed with the flat of your knife.
- **The tarragon:** strip the leaves off the stems and give them a rough chop, about **3 tbsp**. Keep the stems, since they go into the sauce.
- **By the stove:**
  - **1¼ cups broth** in your measuring cup. This is the only liquid to measure ahead, and everything else happens during step 3
  - The **lemon**, cut in half
  - The **tarragon stems**, the **cream**, the **Dijon** and a fork
- **Bowl 2, the finish:** **3 tbsp tarragon leaves** and **1 tbsp cold butter**.
- Set the empty skillet on **6** for 3–4 minutes while you finish prepping, so it's evenly hot when the chicken goes in.

### 2️⃣ Dredge and sear the chicken · ⏱ 10–12 min
- Press each cutlet into the **flour**, flip it, and shake off the extra. You want a thin dusting, not a coat. The flour browns fast, leaves tasty browned bits in the pan for the sauce, and later helps thicken it.
- Add **1 tbsp olive oil** and wait for it to shimmer.
- Lay in as many cutlets as fit **without touching**, usually 2 or 3.
- Leave them alone for **3 minutes**, until they're golden and release from the pan on their own. Flip and cook **2 minutes** more, then move them to a plate.
- Repeat with the **second 1 tbsp oil** and the rest of the cutlets.
- They don't need to be cooked through yet. They'll finish in the sauce.
- ⚠️ **Don't crowd the pan.** Cutlets that touch trap steam between them and turn pale and rubbery instead of golden.
- ⚠️ **Watch the browned bits.** They should be golden-brown. If they're going black by the second batch, turn the dial down to **5**, because burnt bits make a bitter sauce.

### 3️⃣ Build the sauce · ⏱ 10–15 min
- Turn the dial to **5** and add the **1 tbsp butter**.
- Add the **shallots and garlic** with a pinch of salt. Stir for **2–3 minutes**, until the shallots are soft and turning translucent.
- Pour **½ cup of the broth** into the pan, stopping when the measuring cup drops to the **¾ cup line**.
- Squeeze in **2 tsp lemon juice** from one lemon half, which is a good firm squeeze, catching the seeds with your fingers. Scrape up all the browned bits from the bottom of the pan and let it bubble until it's almost gone, about **2 minutes**. That concentrates the browned bits into the base of the sauce, and the lemon brings the brightness wine usually would.
- Pour in the **remaining ¾ cup broth** and drop in the **tarragon stems**. Let it bubble until it's reduced by about half, anywhere from **3 to 8 minutes** depending on your pan and burner. Go by the look, not the clock, and don't add the cream until it gets there. The chicken is happy to wait.
- While that bubbles, pour the **½ cup cream** into the empty measuring cup, no rinsing needed. Add the **1 tbsp Dijon** and whisk with a fork, so it blends smoothly instead of leaving mustard blobs in the sauce.
- Once the broth has reduced, turn the dial down to **4** and stir in the **cream-Dijon**. Simmer for **2 minutes**, until the sauce coats the back of a spoon.
- ⚠️ **Don't reduce it too far.** It thickens more once the chicken goes back in and as it cools on the plate. If it gets gloopy, or you see oil separating at the edges, stir in a splash of broth or water.

### 4️⃣ Finish the chicken in the sauce · ⏱ 3–4 min
- Fish out the **tarragon stems**. By now they've given the sauce all their flavor. Leave the garlic in, because it's soft and great to eat.
- Nestle the **cutlets** back into the pan, along with any juices from the plate, and spoon sauce over the tops.
- Simmer gently on **4** for **2–3 minutes**, until the chicken is cooked through.
- 🌡️ **Pull at 160°F** in the thickest cutlet. It coasts up to 165°F off the heat.

### 5️⃣ Finish and serve · ⏱ 2 min
- Turn off the burner. Stir the **3 tbsp tarragon leaves** and the **1 tbsp cold butter** into the sauce, and squeeze in the **unsqueezed lemon half**.
- Taste the sauce and add salt if it needs it.
- Tarragon's licorice flavor fades with heat. Cooked too long, it goes flat and grassy. Stirred in at the very end, it stays bright. The cold butter melts slowly into the sauce and gives it a glossy, rich finish.
- Serve the cutlets with lots of sauce, the **crusty bread** for swiping, and lemon wedges on the side.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: dry, rubbery chicken.** Usually that's from uneven cutlets or too long in the sauce. Slice evenly, use the thermometer, and remember the chicken only needs a few minutes in the sauce.
- **What else ruins it: a gloopy or greasy sauce.** That's over-reduced cream. It's easy to fix, though: a splash of broth or water brings it right back.
- **Want mashed potatoes instead of bread?** Cut **1½ lb Yukon Golds** into rough 2-inch chunks, skin on, and boil them in salted water for about **15 minutes**, until a fork slides in. Drain, then mash right in the pot with **3 tbsp butter**, **¼ cup warm milk** or cream, and salt. Yukons are buttery enough that skin-on and a little lumpy is a good thing. Start them before you sear the chicken.
- **Lean into the licorice:** add a tiny splash (about **1 tsp**) of **pastis or Pernod** with the deglazing broth. It amplifies the tarragon without tasting boozy.
- **No fresh tarragon?** Use **1 tsp dried**, added with the broth so it has time to soften. It's more muted and hay-like than fresh, but still good. Finish with fresh parsley for some green.
- **Have dry vermouth at the bar, or wine already open?** Use **½ cup** of it instead of the first pour of broth and the lemon juice in step 3. It adds a nice herbal note, and the alcohol cooks off as it bubbles down.
- **Out of lemons?** **1 tsp white wine vinegar** works in place of the lemon juice in step 3. If the finished sauce seems flat, it needs more acid or salt.
- **What "tarragon" means:** it comes through the Arabic *tarkhūn* and French *estragon*, both traced back to the Greek *drakōn*, "dragon." The Latin name is *Artemisia dracunculus*, "little dragon," probably for its coiled, snakelike roots. Hence the 🐉.
- **Tarragon's famous job** is in **béarnaise sauce**, named for Béarn, a region of southwest France. It's one of the four *fines herbes* of French cooking, along with parsley, chives and chervil. Chervil has the same gentle anise note.
