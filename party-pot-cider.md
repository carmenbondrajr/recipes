# 🍎 Party Pot Cider
### Stovetop Mulled Cider

*One big pot on the stove all day, serves 6 with refills. Apple cider kept just under a simmer with whole spices and orange. The house smells like Halloween, and only the host can pull this one off.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Oranges (2): 1 for the pot, 1 for slicing into mugs
- Fresh ginger (a thumb-sized knob)

**Refrigerated / Juice aisle**
- **Apple cider, 1½ gallons total:** one 1-gallon jug plus one ½-gallon jug for topping off
  - Get the cloudy, unfiltered cider, usually in the refrigerated section. Clear apple juice tastes thin and sugary once it's heated
  - If there's an orchard or farm stand nearby, buy it there. It's the best upgrade you can make

**Spices** (whole only, no ground)
- Cinnamon sticks: 4 for the pot, plus extras for mugs if you want them
- Whole cloves (1 tsp)
- Star anise (3 pods)
  - Optional. It adds a soft licorice note, but you won't miss it if you can't find it
- Whole allspice berries (1 tsp)
  - Optional. If the store doesn't have them, skip it
- **Where to look:** first check the **Hispanic/international aisle** for cheap bagged spices (Badia, El Guapo). Look for *canela* (cinnamon), *clavo* (cloves), *anís estrella* (star anise), and *pimienta gorda* (allspice)
  - In the fall, there are often **mulling spice packets** near the cider or in the produce section. One packet can replace all four spices
  - Asian markets sell star anise cheap, and **bulk bins** let you buy just a spoonful

**Liquor (optional, for spiking)**
- Bourbon, spiced rum, or apple brandy. Pick one, a 750 ml bottle is plenty
  - Apple brandy (Laird's Applejack, or Calvados if you want to be fancy) is the most "apple-y" choice

*You should have at home: maple syrup or brown sugar (only if the cider needs it), a ladle*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The pot*
- 1 gallon apple cider (the ½ gallon waits in the fridge for topping off)
- 1 orange
- 1 tsp whole cloves
- 4 cinnamon sticks
- 3 star anise pods
- 1 tsp whole allspice berries (optional)
- 4–5 coin-sized slices of fresh ginger

*For serving*
- 1 orange, sliced into half-moons
- Extra cinnamon sticks
- Maple syrup or brown sugar, to taste
- Bourbon, spiced rum, or apple brandy (optional)

---

### 1️⃣ Stud the orange · ⏱ 3 min
- Cut **1 orange** in half and push the **1 tsp whole cloves** into the peel, pointy end first, like little nails.
- Studded cloves stay put, so nobody gets a mouthful of loose cloves at the bottom of their mug, and you can pull them all out at once later.

### 2️⃣ Toast the spices · ⏱ 1–2 min
- Set your biggest pot over **medium** heat with nothing in it. You need at least 6 quarts so it doesn't slosh when you ladle.
- Add the dry spices:
  - **4 cinnamon sticks**
  - **3 star anise pods**
  - **1 tsp allspice berries**
- Shake the pot every so often for **about 1 minute**, until you can smell them. Heat wakes up the oils inside whole spices, so the cider gets more flavor and the house smells better from the start.
- ⚠️ **Watch them closely.** Spices go from toasty to burnt in seconds, and burnt spices make the whole pot bitter. If you see smoke or they start to darken, go straight to the next step.
- Whole spices only. Ground spices never dissolve, so they turn the cider muddy and leave grit in every mug.

### 3️⃣ Build the pot · ⏱ 2 min
- Pour in the **1 gallon cider** slowly. It'll sizzle and steam when it hits the hot pot, and that's normal.
- Add the studded orange halves and the **4–5 slices fresh ginger**. The cloves and ginger skip the toasting because the cloves are already in the orange and fresh ginger would just scorch.

### 4️⃣ Warm it up · ⏱ 20–30 min
- Set the burner to **medium** until the cider is steaming and tiny bubbles form at the edges, then turn it down to **low**.
- ⚠️ **Don't let it boil.** Boiling pulls bitter tannins out of the cinnamon and cloves and harsh flavors out of the orange pith. The fresh apple flavor cooks off too, and the cider ends up tasting like stewed apples.
- What you want is steaming with no bubbles breaking the surface. If you see bubbles, turn it down.

### 5️⃣ Taste and sweeten · ⏱ 1 min
- After about **30 minutes**, ladle out a little and taste it.
- Good cider is usually sweet enough already. If yours tastes a little sour once it's spiced, stir in **maple syrup or brown sugar, 1 tbsp at a time**.

### 6️⃣ Pull the orange and cloves · ⏱ around the 1½-hour mark
- After about **1½ hours**, fish out the studded orange halves.
- ⚠️ **Over an all-day party, the orange is what goes wrong.** The white pith keeps releasing bitterness the longer it sits in hot liquid, and by hour four it's taking over. The cloves come out with it, which is good, because cloves overpower everything else if they stay in all day.
- Leave the cinnamon, star anise, and ginger in. They mellow over time instead of turning harsh.

### 7️⃣ Keep it going all day
- Keep the burner on the **lowest setting** and put the lid on halfway. The cider stays warm and the steam keeps the house smelling great.
- The cider slowly cooks down and gets stronger. When it's about two-thirds gone, top it off with the **½ gallon cider** you saved and let it warm back up for 10–15 minutes.
- Leave the ladle in the pot and set out mugs, the **sliced orange**, and **extra cinnamon sticks** nearby so people can serve themselves.

### 8️⃣ Spike by the mug (optional)
- Pour **1–1½ oz bourbon, spiced rum, or apple brandy** into a mug first, then ladle the hot cider over it.
- ⚠️ **Never add liquor to the pot.** Alcohol cooks off over the day, and this way anyone who isn't drinking can still have a mug. Put the bottle and a shot glass next to the stove.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: boiling, and leaving the orange in all day.** Both make it bitter. Keep the cider just below a simmer and pull the orange around 1½ hours.
- **Cider vs. juice:** in the US, "apple cider" means fresh, unfiltered, unfermented apple juice, and that's what you want here. Clear apple juice is filtered and pasteurized hard, so it tastes flat and candy-sweet once it's heated. In the UK and most of Europe, "cider" means the alcoholic kind.
- **Want it spicier?** Add a pinch of black peppercorns with the other spices, or an extra slice or two of ginger.
- **Cranberry twist:** swap 2–3 cups of the cider for unsweetened cranberry juice. It turns deep red, which looks great for Halloween, and adds a nice tartness.
- **The name:** this comes from **wassail**, from the Old English *wæs hāl*, "be well." In English cider country, people went wassailing in the orchards in the dead of winter. They sang to the apple trees, poured hot spiced cider on the roots, and hung cider-soaked toast in the branches to bless the next year's harvest (and to scare off evil spirits, which fits the occasion).
- **Applejack and America:** Laird & Company in New Jersey holds **License No. 1**, the first commercial distillery license issued in the US (1780). The family has been making apple brandy since the colonial era, and George Washington supposedly asked them for their recipe.
