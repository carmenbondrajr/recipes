# 🍆 Bruschette alla Parmigiana
### Eggplant Parmesan on Crispy Garlic Toast

*Makes 8 big toasts, enough for a snack spread or dinner for 2 with some left over. About 1 hour, with the sauce, eggplant, and bread overlapping so nothing sits around waiting. All of the crunch of a good bruschetta, all of the goo of eggplant parm. Reworked from [Cooking with Manuela's version](https://www.cookingwithmanuela.com/recipe/bruschette-alla-parmigiana-eggplant-parmesan-bruschetta/).*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Eggplant (1 medium, about 1 lb). Look for one that's firm, glossy, and feels heavy for its size
- Fresh basil (1 small package, about 10 leaves)
- Garlic (1 head)

**Dairy**
- **Fresh mozzarella (8 oz ball).** Pick the kind sold in a ball or log, not tiny pearls. Low-moisture mozzarella also works if you want the crispest toast and don't mind less milky softness
- Parmigiano Reggiano (a small wedge, about 1 oz / ½ cup finely grated)

**Bakery**
- **Rustic Italian or country loaf (8 slices).** Choose a sturdy loaf with a tight crumb. Ciabatta works if you cut it on a diagonal for bigger slices, but sauce can leak through its big holes

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Crushed tomatoes (one 14–15 oz can). Passata works too
- Dried oregano (1 tsp)

**Gear worth checking**
- 2 sheet pans and foil
- Small saucepan

*You should have at home: extra-virgin olive oil (about ½ cup), kosher salt, black pepper, red pepper flakes*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The eggplant*
- 1 medium eggplant (about 1 lb)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 3 tbsp olive oil

*The sauce*
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 2 garlic cloves, smashed and peeled
- Pinch of red pepper flakes
- 1 can (14–15 oz) crushed tomatoes
- ½ tsp dried oregano
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- Black pepper

*The toast and toppings*
- 8 slices rustic bread, ¾ inch thick
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1 garlic clove, halved
- 8 oz fresh mozzarella, cut into 16 slices
- ½ cup finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano (about 1 oz)
- ½ tsp dried oregano
- About 10 fresh basil leaves

---

### 1️⃣ Prep and salt · ⏱ 10 min, plus 15 min rest
- Set racks in the upper-middle and lower-middle of the oven and preheat to **425°F**.
- Line **2 sheet pans** with foil and brush one with a little olive oil for the eggplant. Foil lets the eggplant touch the hot pan directly, so it browns better than it would on parchment.
- Cut the **eggplant** into **½-inch rounds**, which should give you about 16. Thicker rounds stay creamy instead of drying out.
- Lay the rounds on a clean kitchen towel and sprinkle both sides with **1 tsp kosher salt**. Let them sit for **15 minutes** while the oven heats, which seasons the eggplant all the way through without adding time to the recipe.
- Slice the **8 oz mozzarella** into **16 slices** and lay them on a second towel to drain. Wet cheese is the main cause of soggy toast, so give it the full time.
- Slice the **bread** ¾ inch thick if it didn't come sliced.

### 2️⃣ Simmer the sauce · ⏱ 15 min, during the salt rest
- In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, warm **1 tbsp olive oil** with:
  - **2 smashed garlic cloves**
  - **Pinch of red pepper flakes**
- Cook for about **2 minutes**, until the garlic smells great and is just starting to turn pale gold. Add the tomatoes as soon as it gets there.
- Stir in:
  - **1 can (14–15 oz) crushed tomatoes**
  - **½ tsp dried oregano**
  - **¼ tsp kosher salt**
  - **A few grinds of black pepper**
- Simmer gently for **15 minutes**, stirring now and then, until a spoon dragged across the bottom leaves a trail that fills in slowly. This sauce needs to be thick, because any extra water ends up in the bread.
- Mash the softened garlic into the sauce with your spoon. Taste, then turn off the heat and leave the sauce on the burner.
- ⚠️ **Don't let the garlic brown before the tomatoes go in.** Dark garlic tastes bitter, and you'd taste it in every bite.

### 3️⃣ Roast the eggplant · ⏱ 25–30 min
- Pat the salted **eggplant** dry with the towel, pressing gently to soak up the moisture on the surface.
- Arrange the rounds in one layer on the oiled sheet pan and brush the tops with the **3 tbsp olive oil**, getting more on anything that looks dry.
- Roast on the **upper-middle rack for 15 minutes**, then flip each round.
- Roast another **10–15 minutes**, until both sides are deep golden and the centers are completely soft.
- ⚠️ **Don't pull them early.** Undercooked eggplant is spongy and tastes raw. It should be collapsing-soft when poked with a fork, with real browning on the outside.

### 4️⃣ Toast the bread · ⏱ 10–12 min, during the eggplant's second half
- When you flip the eggplant, brush both sides of the **8 bread slices** with **3 tbsp olive oil** and lay them on the second foil-lined pan.
- Bake on the **lower-middle rack for 5–6 minutes**, flip, then bake **5–6 minutes more**, until golden and crisp on both sides. Toasting the bread all the way now gives it a dry, crunchy layer that can stand up to the sauce.
- Rub one side of each warm toast with the cut side of the **halved garlic clove**. The crunchy surface works like a grater, spreading a little raw garlic flavor across the toast.

### 5️⃣ Build the toasts · ⏱ 5 min
- Leave the toasts on their pan, garlic side up, and top each one in this order:
  - **1 tbsp sauce**, spread thin
  - **2 eggplant rounds**, overlapped to fit
  - **1 tsp grated Parmigiano**
  - **1 tbsp sauce**
  - **2 mozzarella slices**
  - A pinch of the remaining **½ tsp dried oregano**
- Finish with the rest of the **Parmigiano** over the top.
- ⚠️ **Go easy on the sauce.** Two tablespoons per toast is plenty. More sauce will soak through and soften the bottoms. Any left over is great for dipping.

### 6️⃣ Melt and finish · ⏱ 6–8 min
- Bake on the **upper-middle rack at 425°F for 6–8 minutes**, until the mozzarella is melted and bubbling and has a few golden spots. A short, hot bake melts the cheese before the bread has time to soften.
- Tear the **basil** over the top after they come out, since baked basil turns black and loses its fresh flavor.
- Serve right away, while the bottoms are still crunchy.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: water.** Wet mozzarella, thin sauce, and undercooked eggplant all end up in the bread. If you handle those three things, the toast stays crisp.
- **Snack-mode batching:** the roasted eggplant and sauce keep covered in the fridge for up to 3 days. Toast a slice or two and build them whenever you want one. Bake at 425°F for about 8 minutes, since the toppings start out cold.
- **Assembled toasts don't keep.** They go soggy in the fridge, so only build what you're eating.
- **Only have one sheet pan?** Toast the bread in a large skillet over medium heat, 2–3 minutes per side, while the eggplant finishes.
- **Darker cheese:** broil for the last 60 seconds and don't walk away, because it can go from golden to burnt quickly.
- **Bee balm instead of oregano:** your dried bee balm tastes a lot like oregano, so use it on top. It's a nice way to bring in the yard.
- **Sausage-y sauce:** add **¼ tsp crushed fennel seeds** with the garlic.
- **The names:** *bruschetta* comes from *bruscare*, to toast or char, and it's pronounced **broo-SKET-tah**. *Bruschette* is the plural. Despite the name, *parmigiana* may not come from Parma. Sicily also claims the dish, and one theory traces it to *parmiciana*, a word for the overlapping slats of a shutter, a lot like layered eggplant.
