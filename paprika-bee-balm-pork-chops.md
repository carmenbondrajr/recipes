# 🌿 Paprika & Bee Balm Pork Chops with Crispy Chickpeas & Green Beans

*One 10-inch Dutch oven, ~40 minutes, serves 2. Boneless chops in a sweet-paprika and bee balm rub, basted in thyme-garlic butter, with chickpeas and green beans crisped in the same pot, soaking up everything the chops left behind. The herbs come from the yard.*

## Shopping List

**From the yard**
- Fresh thyme (~6 sprigs)
- Fresh rosemary (1 sprig)
- Dried bee balm / wild bergamot (~1 heaping tsp crumbled), from roughly 6–10 dried leaves or 2 dried flower heads. *Leaves, flowers, or both. Flowers are milder and a little sweeter, and half and half works well.*

**Produce**
- Green beans (½ lb / 8 oz) — *a handful-sized bag or loose from the bin. Look for ones that snap instead of bending*
- Lemon (1)
- Garlic (1 head)

**Meat**
- **Boneless pork loin chops, ~1¼ lb total** (2 or 3 chops). *Thicker is better. Boneless chops have no bone to slow the cooking, so thin ones (under 1 inch) go from juicy to dry in about a minute. If the only ones you can find are thin, buy them anyway and start checking the temperature early.*

**Dairy**
- Butter (you'll use 3 tbsp)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Chickpeas (one 15 oz can)
- **Sweet paprika** — *check yours first. Paprika fades fast. If it's dull brown instead of bright brick red, or doesn't smell like much, buy a fresh jar. It's the main flavor of the crust.*

*You should have at home: neutral oil (vegetable/canola), kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, brown sugar*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The rub*
- 2¼ tsp sweet paprika
- 1 heaping tsp crumbled dried bee balm
- 1 heaping tsp kosher salt
- Heaping ½ tsp garlic powder
- Heaping ½ tsp brown sugar
- Heaping ¼ tsp black pepper

*Everything else*
- Boneless pork loin chops, ~1¼ lb total (2 or 3 chops)
- 2–3 tbsp neutral oil, split into 1 tbsp (sear) + a splash (second sear round) + 1 tbsp (chickpeas)
- 3 tbsp butter, split into 2 tbsp + 1 tbsp
- 3 garlic cloves, smashed and peeled
- ~6 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 sprig fresh rosemary
- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 8 oz green beans, stem ends trimmed
- ½ lemon, plus wedges for serving

**Steps**

1. **Make the rub and season the chops** (~5 min, then rest 45 min to overnight if you can). Crumble the **heaping 1 tsp dried bee balm** between your palms as it goes into a small bowl. Crushing it wakes up the oils. Mix it with the **2¼ tsp paprika, heaping 1 tsp salt, heaping ½ tsp garlic powder, heaping ½ tsp brown sugar, and heaping ¼ tsp pepper**. Pat the **chops** dry and press the rub into every side, edges included. Ideally leave them uncovered in the fridge for 45 minutes or longer, so the salt seasons all the way through and the surface dries out for a better crust. **No time? Rub them last, right before they go in the pot.** Avoid the in-between (5–30 min), when the salt has pulled moisture to the surface and it won't sear well.

2. **Preheat the pot and prep the rest** (~5 min). Set the empty Dutch oven over **medium** heat while you prep. Heavy pots heat unevenly if you rush them. Drain and rinse the **chickpeas**, then spread them on a towel and pat them **really** dry. Any water left on them steams instead of frying, and they never crisp. Trim the **8 oz green beans**, smash the **3 garlic cloves**, and set the **thyme sprigs**, **rosemary**, **butter**, and tongs next to the stove. Tongs beat a spatula in a deep pot, and the butter baste happens fast.

3. **Sear, in rounds if needed** (~6–12 min). Turn the heat to **medium-high** and add **1 tbsp oil**. When it shimmers, lay in as many chops as fit **without touching**. Usually that's 2 in a 10-inch Dutch oven, because the flat bottom is smaller than the rim. Crowded chops steam instead of searing. Leave them alone for **3 minutes**, until the bottom is a deep brick red and they release on their own. Flip and sear **2 minutes**, then move them to a plate. Add a splash more oil and sear any remaining chops the same way. **Watch the color.** Paprika and brown sugar scorch faster than a plain sear. Deep red-brown is perfect. Dark brown going black, with a bitter smell, means lift the pot off the burner for a moment. A Dutch oven holds heat so well that turning the burner down alone takes minutes to work.

4. **Baste with thyme butter** (~2–4 min). **Lift the pot off the burner for 30 seconds**, then set it back on **medium-low**. Return all the chops, snug together this time, since crowding only matters during the sear. Add **2 tbsp butter**, the **smashed garlic**, and the **thyme sprigs**, tucked in around the chops. When the butter foams, tilt the pot toward you so it pools, and spoon it over the chops again and again. The hot butter cooks the top gently and pulls the thyme and garlic flavor right into the crust. **Pull the chops at 140°F** in the thickest part. They'll coast to 145°F while resting. Check as soon as the baste starts, because thinner chops can be done at around 6 minutes of total cooking.

5. **Rest, and save that butter** (5 min). Move the chops to a plate and pour the basting butter over them, garlic and thyme included. Tent loosely with foil. Leave the browned bits in the pot. That's what seasons the chickpeas and beans.

6. **Crisp the chickpeas** (~6 min). Put the pot back on **medium-high** and add the **last 1 tbsp butter** and **1 tbsp oil**. The oil keeps the butter from burning during the longer cook. Add the **chickpeas** with a pinch of salt, and tuck the **rosemary sprig** in among them. Let them fry, stirring now and then, until golden with crispy edges. Scrape up the paprika fond as you go so they pick up the rub's flavor. The tall sides catch most of the popping and spitting. Once the rosemary is crisp and dark green, about 2–3 minutes, pull it out onto a plate. The oil already has its flavor, and the needles go bitter if they stay in the whole time. **Keep the lid off from here on.** The tall walls already trap steam, and steam undoes crispy.

7. **Add the green beans and blister** (~7–8 min). Push the chickpeas to the sides and pile the **8 oz green beans** in the middle. **Leave them alone for 3 minutes**, until they have dark blistered spots. Toss everything together and repeat once or twice. The pot's walls trap enough steam to cook them through, so they shouldn't need any water. Finish with a pinch of salt and a squeeze from the **½ lemon**.

8. **Plate it.** Chops with their resting butter spooned over the top, chickpeas and beans alongside, lemon wedges on the side. Crumble the crispy rosemary needles over the chickpeas (toss the stem) and strip a few fresh thyme leaves over everything.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: scorched paprika.** Paprika is ground dried peppers with natural sugars, and it goes from sweet to bitter fast over high heat. That's why this sears at medium-high instead of the usual screaming-hot and the baste happens at medium-low. In a Dutch oven, scorching is mostly a problem in the second sear round, because the fond from the first round is already sitting in a hot pot. If the crust looks black instead of red-brown, the heat was too high.
- **What else ruins it: dry chops.** That comes from thin boneless chops or skipping the thermometer. Pull at 140°F. Slightly pink in the middle is correct and safe.
- **Using a 12-inch skillet instead?** Two or three chops fit in one round. Sear and baste in a single pass, and turning the burner down works on its own without lifting the pan. Chickpeas and beans blister better in the wider pan. If the beans are still too crunchy, add 2 tbsp water and cover for 1 minute.
- **One herb per job.** Bee balm goes in the rub, thyme in the butter baste, and rosemary with the chickpeas. Thyme shares bee balm's aromatic compounds, so they blend into one flavor. Rosemary is piney and loud and would fight the bee balm on the pork, but rosemary and chickpeas are an old Italian pairing (think *pasta e ceci*).
- **Bee balm strength varies a lot** by plant, season, and how it was dried. Taste a pinch first. If it's sharp enough to numb your tongue a little, use a level teaspoon. Dried flowers are milder and slightly sweeter than the leaves. When you crumble a flower head, pick out any bits of stem. If the plant still has fresh flowers, scatter a few petals over the plate at the end.
- **Want a little smoke?** Swap **½ tsp** of the sweet paprika for smoked. That's enough to hint at a grill without taking over the herbs.
- **No thyme sprigs?** A couple of fresh bee balm leaves work in the baste too, if the plant is still going. Use fewer than you would thyme.
- **Chickpeas not crisping?** They're either still wet or crowded. Dry them better next time, and give them the full 6 minutes before the beans go in. Even soft, they'll still taste great.
- **Asparagus in spring:** same blistering method, about 5 minutes. Thin spears are best.
- **Why bee balm and thyme go together:** bee balm's oregano-like bite comes from **thymol and carvacrol**, the same aromatic compounds behind thyme and oregano. This dish is basically one flavor family in three forms: fresh, dried, and in the crust. Wild bergamot (*Monarda fistulosa*) is the most oregano-like bee balm, and in parts of the Southwest it's even called *oregano de la sierra*.
- **The names:** *Monarda* honors Nicolás Monardes, a 16th-century Spanish physician who wrote one of the first European books about New World plants without ever leaving Spain. People called it "bergamot" because the crushed leaves reminded them of bergamot orange. And "bee balm" because bees and hummingbirds absolutely swarm it.
