# 🍅 Sheet Pan Sunday
### Parmesan Panko Chicken with Burst Tomatoes, Veggies & Crispy Gnocchi

*Two half-sheet pans, one trip into the oven, ~50 minutes, serves 3–4 (dinner for 2 plus lunches). Thin chicken cutlets with a crunchy parm-panko crust go on one pan, with gnocchi crisping in the gaps around them. The other pan holds cherry tomatoes that burst into a garlicky sauce, plus zucchini, pepper and onion. At the end the crispy gnocchi gets tossed into the tomato pan to soak up the juices. Everything roasts at **450°F** on pans that preheat with the oven, so the bottoms start sizzling the second they land.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Cherry or grape tomatoes (1 pint / 10 oz, ~2 cups)
- Zucchini (1 medium)
- Bell pepper (1). Red, orange or yellow, which are sweeter than green
- Red onion (1 small)
- Lemon (1)
- Fresh basil (1 small pack, ~½ cup leaves)

**Meat**
- **Boneless skinless chicken breasts, ~1½ lb** (2 large)
  - Or grab **thin-sliced cutlets** (~1½ lb, about 4) and skip the slicing. Aim for about ½ inch thick either way

**Dairy**
- **Parmesan, grated (¾ cup).** A wedge you grate yourself, or the tub of fresh-grated. Not the shelf-stable shaker can, which doesn't brown or melt the same

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Shelf-stable potato gnocchi (1 lb pack).** Near the dry pasta, in a vacuum-sealed pouch. Skip the frozen kind, which steams instead of crisping
- Panko breadcrumbs (¾ cup)
- Minced garlic (jar)
- Mayonnaise (3 tbsp) and Dijon mustard (1 tsp). Check yours first

*You should have at home: olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, Italian seasoning, paprika*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*Chicken*
- 1½ lb boneless skinless chicken breast (2 large), or 4 thin cutlets
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- Black pepper

*Crust*
- ¾ cup panko
- ½ cup grated Parmesan
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- ½ tsp paprika
- Zest of 1 lemon (save the lemon itself)
- 3 tbsp mayonnaise
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp minced garlic

*Veggies*
- 1 pint cherry tomatoes (~2 cups), left whole
- 1 medium zucchini
- 1 bell pepper
- 1 small red onion
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tsp minced garlic
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp Italian seasoning
- Black pepper

*Gnocchi & finish*
- 1 lb shelf-stable potato gnocchi
- 2 tbsp olive oil, divided
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ cup grated Parmesan
- ~½ cup fresh basil leaves
- The zested lemon

---

### 1️⃣ Heat the oven and pans · ⏱ ~20 min, while you prep
- Put **both empty sheet pans** in the oven, side by side on the middle rack, and set it to **450°F**.
- Pans that heat up with the oven sear whatever lands on them, like a skillet. That's what gets the gnocchi and the bottoms of the cutlets crisp and the veggies charred instead of steamed.
- ⚠️ **Give it the full 20 minutes, even after it beeps.** Ovens beep when the air is hot, not when the walls and pans are, and an oven that runs cold needs that extra time even more.

### 2️⃣ Prep · ⏱ 15 min
Sort everything into bowls by which pan it goes on, so loading the hot pans is fast:
- **Bowl 1, the veggies.** Cut them all to finish together with the chicken, then toss:
  - **1 pint cherry tomatoes**, left whole so they burst in the oven
  - **1 zucchini**, halved lengthwise and cut into **¾-inch half-moons**. Thinner pieces go mushy before the tomatoes are done
  - **1 bell pepper**, cut into **1-inch squares**
  - **1 red onion**, cut into **½-inch wedges** through the root so they hold together
  - **2 tbsp olive oil**, **2 tsp minced garlic**, **½ tsp salt**, **½ tsp Italian seasoning**, and pepper. Toss until everything's glossy. Mixed into the oil and tucked between the veggies, the garlic doesn't burn the way it does sitting bare on a 450°F pan.
- **Bowl 2, the gnocchi.** Break up the **1 lb gnocchi** with your fingers, since they stick together in the pack. Toss with **1 tbsp olive oil** and **¼ tsp salt**.
- **Bowl 3, the crumbs.** Rub these together with your fingers until it looks like damp sand:
  - **¾ cup panko**
  - **½ cup Parmesan**
  - **2 tbsp olive oil**
  - **1 tsp garlic powder**, **1 tsp Italian seasoning**, **½ tsp paprika**
  - **Zest of the lemon**
  - The oil is what toasts the panko. Dry crumbs stay pale and sandy in the oven no matter how long they go.
- **Bowl 4, the glue.** Stir together **3 tbsp mayo**, **1 tsp Dijon**, and **1 tsp minced garlic**.
- **The chicken, last,** so raw chicken never touches the veggie board:
  - Lay each breast flat, hold it down with your palm, and slice it **horizontally** into **2 thin cutlets**, about **½ inch** thick. If the thick end is still chunky, press it down with the heel of your hand.
  - Pat them dry and season **both sides** with **1 tsp salt** and pepper.
  - Even thickness matters more than anything else here. A cutlet that's thick at one end and thin at the other is dry at one end and raw at the other.

### 3️⃣ Crust the cutlets · ⏱ 5 min
- Lay the cutlets on the cutting board. Spread the **mayo glue** thinly over the **tops only**.
- Pile the **crumbs** on top and press them down firmly with your palm so they stick.
- The mayo is glue, fat, and seasoning all at once. It holds the crust on, keeps the breast meat juicy, and helps the crumbs brown. You won't taste mayo.
- The crust only goes on top, so you never flip the cutlets and knock it off. The bottoms sear on the hot pan instead.

### 4️⃣ Load the hot pans · ⏱ 3 min
- With oven mitts, pull **both pans** out onto the stovetop and shut the oven door so it keeps its heat.
- **Pan 1:** drizzle **1 tbsp olive oil** and tilt to spread it. Lay the **cutlets** on crust-side up, with space between them, then scatter the **gnocchi** into the gaps in one layer. They should sizzle as they land.
- **Pan 2:** dump the **veggie bowl** on and spread it out in one layer.
- Slide both pans back in, side by side.
- ⚠️ **One layer, nothing piled up.** Crowded veggies trap their own steam and come out soft and pale instead of charred. If it doesn't all fit, leave some zucchini off rather than piling it.
- ⚠️ **Set hot pans on the stovetop, not the counter.** A 450°F pan can scorch a countertop.

### 5️⃣ Roast · ⏱ 18–22 min
- At **10 minutes**, open the oven, flip the gnocchi with a spatula, give the veggies a quick shake, and rotate both pans: swap left and right, and turn each one 180°. Every oven has hot spots, and this evens them out.
- Start checking the chicken at **16 minutes**.
- It's done when the crust is deep golden, the tomatoes have burst and slumped, and the gnocchi is golden and crisp on the bottom.
- 🌡️ **Pull at 160°F** in the thickest cutlet. It coasts up to 165°F while it rests.

### 6️⃣ Finish the side · ⏱ 3 min
- Let the cutlets rest on their pan while you do this. Resting keeps the juices in the chicken instead of on the cutting board.
- On the veggie pan, smash about **half the tomatoes** with a fork. Their juice mixes with the garlic oil into a loose, chunky sauce.
- Scrape the **gnocchi** over onto the veggie pan. Add the **¼ cup Parmesan**, tear in the **basil**, and squeeze in **half the lemon**. Toss everything together.
- The gnocchi soaks up the tomato juice while staying a little crisp. That's the whole point of the side.

### 7️⃣ Serve
- Cut the other **half lemon** into wedges.
- Plate a cutlet next to a big scoop of the tomato gnocchi, with a lemon wedge to squeeze over the chicken right before you eat.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: a soggy, pale crust.** The usual causes are dry crumbs with no oil, a thick layer of mayo, or a pan that went in cold. If the chicken is at 160°F but the crust still looks pale, **broil for 1–2 minutes** and watch it the whole time, because panko goes from golden to burnt fast.
- **What else ruins it: dry chicken.** Usually that's uneven cutlets. Slice evenly, press the thick end down, and use a thermometer.
- **Get an oven thermometer.** They're ~$8, and since your oven runs cold, it tells you how far off it really is. If it's way off, set the dial higher to make up the difference.
- **Prep ahead:** cut the veggies and mix the crumbs in the morning, and keep them in the fridge. Crust the chicken right before it goes in, though. The crumbs go soft if they sit on the mayo for hours.
- **Leftovers:** re-crisp the cutlets at **375°F for 8–10 minutes** in the oven or air fryer. The microwave turns the crust soggy. The gnocchi side reheats fine either way.
- **Other starches:** crusty bread to mop up the tomato juice, or orzo cooked on the stove and tossed with the tomatoes instead of gnocchi.
- **Want it more Parm-ish?** Put a slice of fresh mozzarella on each cutlet for the last 5 minutes.
- **What "panko" means:** it's Japanese. *Pan* means bread (borrowed from the Portuguese *pão*, from traders in the 1500s), and *ko* means flour or crumb. It's made from crustless bread that's baked and shredded into big, airy flakes instead of ground fine, and that's why it stays crunchier than regular breadcrumbs.
