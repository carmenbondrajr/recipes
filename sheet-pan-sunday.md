# 🍅 Sheet Pan Sunday
### Parmesan Panko Chicken with Burst Tomato & Veggie Orzo

*Two half-sheet pans, one trip into the oven, plus a pot of toasted orzo on the stove. ~50 minutes, serves 3–4 (dinner for 2 plus lunches). Thin chicken cutlets with a crunchy parm-panko crust go on one pan with plenty of space around them. The other pan holds cherry tomatoes that burst into a garlicky sauce, plus zucchini, pepper and shallots. The orzo gets toasted golden, then simmered in broth until it's creamy, and at the end the whole veggie pan, juices and all, gets scraped into the pot. Everything roasts at **450°F** on pans that preheat with the oven, so the bottoms start sizzling the second they land. Stove heat is given as numbers on the dial, same as [Chickpea Chops](chickpea-chops.md).*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Cherry or grape tomatoes (1 pint / 10 oz, ~2 cups)
- Zucchini (1 medium)
- Bell pepper (1). Red, orange or yellow, which are sweeter than green
- Shallots (3 medium). Firm, with dry papery skins. Or skip them entirely and grab a second bell pepper
- Lemon (1)
- Fresh basil (1 small pack, ~½ cup leaves)
- Garlic (1 head). You'll use about 6 cloves

**Meat**
- **Boneless skinless chicken breasts, ~1½ lb** (2 large)
  - Or grab **thin-sliced cutlets** (~1½ lb, about 4) and skip the slicing. Aim for about ½ inch thick either way

**Dairy**
- **Parmesan, grated (¾ cup).** A wedge you grate yourself, or the tub of fresh-grated. Not the shelf-stable shaker can, which doesn't brown or melt the same
- **Sour cream, full-fat (3 tbsp).** Light sour cream is watery and can make the crust soggy

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Orzo (1½ cups dry / ~9 oz).** A 1 lb box is about 2⅔ cups, so you'll use a little over half
- **Low-sodium chicken broth (one 32 oz carton).** You'll use about 3½ cups. Low-sodium matters here, because the broth cooks down into the orzo and the parm brings plenty of salt
- Panko breadcrumbs (¾ cup)
- Dijon mustard (1 tsp). Check yours first

*You should have at home: olive oil, kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, Italian seasoning, paprika*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*Chicken*
- 1½ lb boneless skinless chicken breast (2 large), or 4 thin cutlets
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- Black pepper
- 1 tbsp olive oil, for the pan

*Crust*
- ¾ cup panko
- ½ cup grated Parmesan
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- ½ tsp paprika
- Zest of 1 lemon (save the lemon itself)
- 3 tbsp full-fat sour cream
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 clove garlic, finely grated or minced

*Veggies*
- 1 pint cherry tomatoes (~2 cups), left whole
- 1 medium zucchini
- 1 bell pepper
- 3 medium shallots
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 5 cloves garlic, smashed
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp Italian seasoning
- Black pepper

*Orzo & finish*
- 1½ cups dry orzo (~9 oz)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 3 cups low-sodium chicken broth, plus about ½ cup more for loosening
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ cup grated Parmesan
- ~½ cup fresh basil leaves
- The zested lemon

---

### 1️⃣ Heat the oven and pans · ⏱ ~20 min, while you prep
- Put **both empty sheet pans** in the oven, side by side on the middle rack, and set it to **450°F**.
- Pans that heat up with the oven sear whatever lands on them, like a skillet. That's what gets the bottoms of the cutlets crisp and the veggies charred instead of steamed.
- ⚠️ **Give it the full 20 minutes, even after it beeps.** Ovens beep when the air is hot, not when the walls and pans are, and an oven that runs cold needs that extra time even more.

### 2️⃣ Prep · ⏱ 15 min
Sort everything into bowls by which pan it goes on, so loading the hot pans is fast:
- **Bowl 1, the veggies.** Cut them all to finish together with the chicken, then toss:
  - **1 pint cherry tomatoes**, left whole so they burst in the oven
  - **1 zucchini**, halved lengthwise and cut into **¾-inch half-moons**. Thinner pieces go mushy before the tomatoes are done
  - **1 bell pepper**, cut into **1-inch squares**
  - **3 shallots**, peeled and halved lengthwise through the root so the layers hold together. Quarter any big ones. Roasted, they go soft and jammy and lose the sharp raw-onion bite completely
  - **5 garlic cloves**, peeled and smashed with the flat of your knife so they crack open but stay in one piece. Whole cloves roast soft, sweet and jammy at 450°F, where minced garlic would scorch
  - **2 tbsp olive oil**, **½ tsp salt**, **½ tsp Italian seasoning**, and pepper. Toss until everything's glossy, and tuck the garlic between the veggies when you spread them out so it's not sitting bare on the hot pan.
- **Bowl 2, the crumbs.** Rub these together with your fingers until it looks like damp sand:
  - **¾ cup panko**
  - **½ cup Parmesan**
  - **2 tbsp olive oil**
  - **1 tsp garlic powder**, **1 tsp Italian seasoning**, **½ tsp paprika**
  - **Zest of the lemon**
  - The oil is what toasts the panko. Dry crumbs stay pale and sandy in the oven no matter how long they go.
- **Bowl 3, the glue.** Stir together **3 tbsp sour cream**, **1 tsp Dijon**, and **1 clove garlic**, grated on a microplane or minced fine. Chunks would make lumps under the crust.
- **For the orzo:** measure out the **1½ cups orzo** and **3 cups broth** and set them by the stove, with the Dutch oven ready on a burner. Once the pans are in, things move fast.
- **The chicken, last,** so raw chicken never touches the veggie board:
  - Lay each breast flat, hold it down with your palm, and slice it **horizontally** into **2 thin cutlets**, about **½ inch** thick. If the thick end is still chunky, press it down with the heel of your hand.
  - Pat them dry and season **both sides** with **1 tsp salt** and pepper.
  - Even thickness matters more than anything else here. A cutlet that's thick at one end and thin at the other is dry at one end and raw at the other.

### 3️⃣ Crust the cutlets · ⏱ 5 min
- Lay the cutlets on the cutting board. Spread the **sour cream glue** in a thin layer over the **tops only**, just enough to look shiny.
- Pile the **crumbs** on top and press them down firmly with your palm so they stick.
- The sour cream holds the crust on and keeps the breast meat juicy. It also adds a little tang that goes with the lemon and parm.
- ⚠️ **Thin layer only.** Sour cream is wetter than most crumb glues. Too much of it steams the panko from underneath and leaves the crust soft.
- The crust only goes on top, so you never flip the cutlets and knock it off. The bottoms sear on the hot pan instead.

### 4️⃣ Load the hot pans · ⏱ 3 min
- With oven mitts, pull **both pans** out onto the stovetop and shut the oven door so it keeps its heat.
- **Pan 1:** drizzle **1 tbsp olive oil** and tilt to spread it. Lay the **cutlets** on crust-side up, spaced out across the whole pan. Room around each one lets hot air hit every edge, which matters in an oven that runs cold.
- **Pan 2:** dump the **veggie bowl** on and spread it out in one layer.
- Slide both pans back in, side by side, and set a timer for **8 minutes**.
- ⚠️ **One layer, nothing piled up.** Crowded veggies trap their own steam and come out soft and pale instead of charred.
- ⚠️ **Set hot pans on the stovetop, not the counter.** A 450°F pan can scorch a countertop.

### 5️⃣ Roast the pans, toast the orzo · ⏱ ~25 min
- At **8 minutes**, give the veggies a quick shake and rotate both pans: swap left and right, and turn each one 180°. Every oven has hot spots, and this evens them out.
- Right after that, set the Dutch oven on **5** with **1 tbsp olive oil** and the **1½ cups orzo**. Stir often for **3–4 minutes**, until most of the grains are golden and it smells nutty, like toast.
- Toasting browns the outside of the pasta, which gives it a deep, nutty flavor that boiled orzo doesn't have. It also helps the grains hold their shape instead of going mushy.
- ⚠️ **Keep it moving.** Small pasta goes from golden to burnt in under a minute. If it's browning unevenly, turn the dial down a notch.
- Pour in the **3 cups broth** and **½ tsp salt**. Stand back, because it'll sputter. Bring it to a boil, then turn it down to a steady simmer, uncovered.
- Simmer for **~10 minutes**, stirring every couple of minutes, until most of the broth is absorbed and the orzo is just tender with a little bite.
- Meanwhile, start checking the chicken at **16 minutes**. It's done when the crust is deep golden and the tomatoes have burst and slumped. Pull both pans when it's ready, even if the orzo isn't. The chicken happily rests while the orzo finishes.
- 🌡️ **Pull the chicken at 160°F** in the thickest cutlet. It coasts up to 165°F while it rests.
- ⚠️ **Stop the orzo while it's still a little soupy.** It keeps absorbing liquid off the heat, and the tomato juices are coming. If it goes dry before it's tender, add a splash more broth.

### 6️⃣ Finish the orzo · ⏱ 3 min
- Turn off the burner. Scrape the **whole veggie pan** into the orzo pot, including every bit of the garlicky oil and tomato juice. That's the sauce.
- Smash about **half the tomatoes** and all the **roasted garlic cloves** against the side of the pot with a spoon. The garlic is soft enough to melt right into the sauce.
- Add the **¼ cup Parmesan**, tear in the **basil**, and squeeze in **half the lemon**. Stir in more **broth**, a splash at a time, until it's creamy and loose.
- Because nothing gets drained, the starch from the orzo stays in the pot. That's what pulls the oil, tomato juice and parm together into a creamy, almost risotto-like sauce.

### 7️⃣ Serve
- Cut the other **half lemon** into wedges.
- Plate a cutlet next to a big scoop of the tomato orzo, with a lemon wedge to squeeze over the chicken right before you eat.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: a soggy, pale crust.** The usual causes are dry crumbs with no oil, a thick layer of sour cream, or a pan that went in cold. If the chicken is at 160°F but the crust still looks pale, **broil for 1–2 minutes** and watch it the whole time, because panko goes from golden to burnt fast.
- **What else ruins it: dry chicken.** Usually that's uneven cutlets. Slice evenly, press the thick end down, and use a thermometer.
- **And: gluey orzo.** That comes from cooking it until it's dry in the pot. Pull it while it's still loose and let the veggie juices and residual heat finish it.
- **Get an oven thermometer.** They're ~$8, and since your oven runs cold, it tells you how far off it really is. If it's way off, set the dial higher to make up the difference.
- **Prep ahead:** cut the veggies and mix the crumbs in the morning, and keep them in the fridge. Crust the chicken right before it goes in, though. The crumbs go soft if they sit on the sour cream for hours.
- **Leftovers:** re-crisp the cutlets at **375°F for 8–10 minutes** in the oven or air fryer. The microwave turns the crust soggy. The orzo soaks up more liquid in the fridge, so add a splash of broth or water before reheating it.
- **Want it more Parm-ish?** Put a slice of fresh mozzarella on each cutlet for the last 5 minutes.
- **What "orzo" means:** it's Italian for *barley*, because that's what the little grains look like. In Italy it's often called *risoni*, "big rice."
- **What "panko" means:** it's Japanese. *Pan* means bread (borrowed from the Portuguese *pão*, from traders in the 1500s), and *ko* means flour or crumb. It's made from crustless bread that's baked and shredded into big, airy flakes instead of ground fine, and that's why it stays crunchier than regular breadcrumbs.
