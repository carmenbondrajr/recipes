# 🧁 Bignè
### Italian Cream Puffs with Vanilla Whipped Cream

*Makes about 16 small puffs. One saucepan, one sheet pan, one wooden spoon, no mixer. About 1¾ hours start to finish, but only 30 minutes of that is hands-on. Written for a first-time baker, so every step says what it should look like.*

## Shopping List

**Dairy**
- Unsalted butter (4 tbsp / ½ stick / 57 g)
- Large eggs (3). The recipe uses 2, and the third is backup in case the dough needs loosening
- **Heavy whipping cream (1 cup / one 8 oz carton).** Half-and-half and light cream won't whip, so check the label

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- All-purpose flour (½ cup / 65 g)
- Powdered sugar (2 tbsp for the cream, plus more for dusting)
- Vanilla extract (1 tsp)
- Parchment paper

**Gear worth checking**
- **Kitchen scale.** About $15 and worth it, because flour measured by the cup can vary a lot
- Small saucepan, wooden spoon, mixing bowl, whisk, sheet pan
- Zip-top bag for piping. Gallon or quart both work

*You should have at home: kosher salt, granulated sugar*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The puffs*
- ½ cup (113 g) water
- 4 tbsp (57 g) unsalted butter, cut into small pieces
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- ½ cup (65 g) all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs, plus 1 backup

*The filling*
- 1 cup cold heavy whipping cream
- 2 tbsp powdered sugar, plus more for dusting
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

---

### 1️⃣ Set up · ⏱ 10 min
- Set the **3 eggs** in a bowl of warm tap water. Room-temperature eggs blend into the dough much more easily.
- Move a rack to the middle of the oven and preheat to **400°F**. The puffs need a fully hot oven the moment they go in, because that first blast of heat is what inflates them.
- Line a sheet pan with parchment.
- Put a mixing bowl and whisk in the fridge for the cream later. Cold cream in a cold bowl whips much faster, which matters when your arm is the mixer.
- Weigh out the **65 g flour** and keep it right next to the stove, because it all goes in at once.

### 2️⃣ Make the dough · ⏱ 5 min
- In a small saucepan, combine:
  - **½ cup (113 g) water**
  - **4 tbsp (57 g) butter, in small pieces**
  - **1 tsp granulated sugar**
  - **¼ tsp kosher salt**
- Heat over medium until the butter has melted and the water is at a full, rolling boil. Small butter pieces melt before much water can boil away, which keeps the ratio right.
- Take the pot off the heat and dump in all of the **65 g flour** at once.
- Stir hard with a wooden spoon. It will look like a lumpy disaster for about 20 seconds, then pull together into a smooth ball.

### 3️⃣ Dry the dough · ⏱ 2 min
- Return the pot to medium-low and keep stirring and smearing the dough around the pot for **2 minutes**. Drying it out now leaves room for the eggs, and more egg means more puff.
- It's ready when it looks glossy, smells a little like toasted pie crust, and a thin film has formed on the bottom of the pot. Nonstick pots won't form the film, so go by the 2 minutes.
- Scrape the dough into a mixing bowl and spread it up the sides. Let it cool for **5 minutes**, until it feels warm but no longer hot, so it doesn't scramble the eggs.

### 4️⃣ Beat in the eggs · ⏱ 5 min
- Crack **1 egg** into the dough and beat hard with the wooden spoon. The dough will break apart into slippery clumps and look ruined. That's normal, so keep beating for about a minute until it comes back together.
- Beat the **second egg** in a small cup with a fork, then add half and beat until smooth.
- Now test it: lift the spoon and watch the dough fall. You want it to slide off slowly and leave a **V-shaped ribbon** hanging from the spoon.
- Add the rest of the second egg a spoonful at a time, testing after each one, and stop as soon as you get the V. You may not need all of it.
- If it's still too stiff after both eggs, beat the **backup egg** in a cup and add it 1 tsp at a time.
- ⚠️ **Don't go past the V.** Too little egg gives you small, dense puffs, but too much gives you dough that spreads out flat. It should hold its shape when piped, like very thick frosting.

### 5️⃣ Pipe the puffs · ⏱ 5 min
- Scrape the dough into a zip-top bag, push it toward one corner, and snip off about **¾ inch** of the corner.
- Pipe mounds about **1¼ inches wide** and about 1 inch tall, spacing them **2 inches apart** because they'll roughly double. Hold the bag still and straight up, squeeze, stop squeezing, then flick away.
- Dip a fingertip in water and gently press down any pointy tips. Peaks burn before the rest of the puff is done, and smoother tops rise more evenly.

### 6️⃣ Bake · ⏱ 32–38 min
- Bake at **400°F for 20 minutes**. Then, **without opening the door**, lower the oven to **350°F** and bake another **12–18 minutes**.
- ⚠️ **Keep the door closed for the first 30 minutes.** The puffs are being held up by steam until their shells set, and a rush of cool air can collapse them. Use the oven light instead.
- They're ready when they're a **deep golden brown all over**, including inside the cracks and around the bottom edge.
- ⚠️ **Pale means underbaked.** Pull one puff out and leave it on the counter for 2 minutes. If it stays firm and feels light and hollow, they're done. If it softens or dents, bake the rest for 5 more minutes.

### 7️⃣ Let the steam out · ⏱ 10 min, plus 20 min to cool
- Poke a small slit in the side of each puff with a paring knife, so the steam inside can escape instead of turning the shell soggy.
- Turn the oven off, return the pan, and prop the door open with a wooden spoon for **10 minutes**. This dries the shells so they stay crisp.
- Cool on the counter for about **20 minutes**, until completely cool. Warm puffs will melt the cream.

### 8️⃣ Whip the cream · ⏱ 3–4 min
- In your chilled bowl, combine:
  - **1 cup cold heavy whipping cream**
  - **2 tbsp powdered sugar**
  - **1 tsp vanilla extract**
- Tilt the bowl slightly and whisk quickly in big circles, lifting the whisk through the cream to pull in air. It'll look like nothing is happening for the first minute or two, then it thickens fast.
- Stop when lifting the whisk leaves a peak that holds its shape and just curls over at the tip.
- ⚠️ **Stop as soon as you hit that peak.** Keep going and it turns grainy on the way to becoming butter. If it just starts looking rough, gently stir in **1–2 tbsp more cold cream** to smooth it out.

### 9️⃣ Fill and serve · ⏱ 5 min
- Slice off the top third of each puff with a serrated knife. Cutting them open is easier than injecting them, and it shows off the cream.
- Spoon or pipe about **2 tbsp cream** into each one and set the top back on at a jaunty angle.
- Dust with **powdered sugar** through a small strainer and eat them within a couple of hours, while the shells still have some crispness.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: underbaking.** Almost every collapsed cream puff came out of the oven too soon. When in doubt, give them 5 more minutes. A darker puff still tastes great.
- **Second most common problem: runny dough.** If it spreads instead of holding a mound, too much egg went in. Bake it anyway and call them rustic.
- **Only filling a few?** Fill just what you'll eat, since cream softens the shells within hours. Empty puffs keep airtight at room temperature for 1 day or in the freezer for 1 month. Re-crisp them at **300°F for 5 minutes**, then cool before filling.
- **Coffee cream:** dissolve **1 tsp instant espresso** in the vanilla before adding it to the cream.
- **Chocolate tops:** microwave **2 oz chopped chocolate** with **2 tbsp heavy cream** in 15-second bursts, stirring until smooth, then dip the lids.
- **Browner, softer puffs:** swap half the water for whole milk. The milk sugars brown faster, so start checking at the low end of the bake time.
- **Round two filling:** once the shells feel easy, try classic pastry cream, the custard filling you'd see in an Italian bakery case.
- **The names:** *bignè* comes from the French *beignet*, or fritter. The dough is *pâte à choux*, or "cabbage paste," because the puffs look like tiny lumpy cabbages. Legend credits the dough to an Italian chef who followed Catherine de' Medici to France in the 1500s, which would make these a round trip.
- **Roman cousin:** *bignè di San Giuseppe* are made from nearly the same dough, but they're traditionally fried and filled with custard for St. Joseph's Day on March 19.
