# 🍪 Pasticcini alle Mandorle
### Chewy Italian Almond Cookies with Lemon

*Makes about 20 cookies. One bowl, one sheet pan, no mixer. About 1¼ hours start to finish, with 20 minutes of actual work. Crackly outside and marzipan-chewy inside, and the easiest bake in this folder. Reworked from [Cooking with Manuela's version](https://www.cookingwithmanuela.com/recipe/italian-almond-cookies-pasticcini-alle-mandorle/).*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Lemon (1 large), for zest

**Dairy**
- Large eggs (3). The recipe uses 2 whites, and the third is backup in case the dough is dry

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Blanched almond flour (2½ cups / 250 g).** Buy a 16 oz bag, since an 8 oz bag comes up just short. Look for "blanched" and "fine," because almond meal has the skins in it and bakes up crumbly
- Whole almonds (about ¼ cup / 20 almonds), for the tops. Skin-on or blanched both work, or use halved candied cherries instead
- Powdered sugar (½ cup / 60 g), for rolling
- Honey (1 tbsp)
- Vanilla extract (1 tsp)
- Almond extract (¼ tsp). A small bottle lasts forever, and it's what makes these taste like a bakery cookie
- Parchment paper

**Gear worth checking**
- **Kitchen scale.** Almond flour packs very unevenly in a cup, so weighing it is the surest way to get the dough right

*You should have at home: granulated sugar, kosher salt*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**
- 2½ cups (250 g) blanched almond flour
- 1 cup (200 g) granulated sugar
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- Zest of 1 large lemon
- 2 large egg whites, plus 1 backup
- 1 tbsp honey
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ¼ tsp almond extract
- ½ cup (60 g) powdered sugar, for rolling
- About 20 whole almonds

---

### 1️⃣ Set up · ⏱ 5 min
- Line a sheet pan with parchment.
- Separate **2 eggs**, passing each yolk back and forth between the shell halves while the white falls into a small bowl. A little yolk in the whites won't hurt anything here, so don't stress over it.
- Beat the **2 egg whites** with a fork for about 20 seconds, until loose and a bit frothy. Loose whites spread evenly through the dough, while gloppy ones leave wet patches.
- Put the **½ cup powdered sugar** in a shallow bowl.

### 2️⃣ Make the dough · ⏱ 5 min
- In a large bowl, stir together:
  - **2½ cups (250 g) blanched almond flour**
  - **1 cup (200 g) granulated sugar**
  - **¼ tsp kosher salt**
  - **Zest of 1 large lemon**
- Rub the zest into the dry mix with your fingertips for a few seconds. The sugar grinds against the zest and releases its oils, so the whole batch tastes more like lemon.
- Stir **1 tbsp honey**, **1 tsp vanilla extract**, and **¼ tsp almond extract** into the egg whites, then pour it all into the bowl.
- Stir with a sturdy spoon until no dry patches remain. It'll seem too dry at first, so finish by squeezing it together with your hand.

### 3️⃣ Check the dough · ⏱ 1 min
- Roll a small piece into a ball. It should feel like soft, slightly tacky Play-Doh and hold its shape without cracking.
- **Too dry and crumbly?** Beat the **backup egg white** with a fork and add it **1 tsp at a time**, mixing well after each. Egg size and almond flour brands vary, so some batches need it and some don't.
- **Too wet to hold a ball?** Let it sit for **10 minutes**, since almond flour keeps absorbing moisture. If it's still too soft, mix in **1–2 tbsp almond flour**.
- ⚠️ **Don't let it get loose.** Extra moisture is what makes these spread into flat, sad discs, so add liquid slowly.

### 4️⃣ Roll and top · ⏱ 10 min
- Dampen your hands so the dough doesn't stick, rewetting them whenever it starts to cling.
- Roll the dough into balls about **1¼ inches wide**, a heaping tablespoon or **25 g each**. Keeping them the same size means they all finish at the same time.
- Drop each ball into the **powdered sugar** and roll until completely coated. The coating is what cracks into that snowy pattern as the cookie expands.
- Set them on the sheet pan, about **1½ inches apart**, and press **1 whole almond** gently into each top.

### 5️⃣ Rest while the oven heats · ⏱ 30 min
- Leave the cookies uncovered on the counter for **30 minutes**. The surface dries a little, which helps form a crisp shell with a chewy center and encourages the crackle.
- Move a rack to the middle of the oven and preheat to **325°F** during the rest. A moderate oven lets the insides set gently before the outsides get too brown.

### 6️⃣ Bake · ⏱ 16–20 min
- Bake at **325°F for 16–20 minutes**, checking at 16.
- They're ready when the tops are cracked and still pale, the edges and bottoms are lightly golden, and they feel just set but soft when gently pressed.
- ⚠️ **Don't wait for the tops to brown.** By then the centers have gone from chewy to crunchy. They'll look a little underdone and firm up as they cool.

### 7️⃣ Cool on the pan · ⏱ 20 min
- Leave them on the pan for at least **20 minutes** before moving them. They're fragile when hot, and the warm pan finishes setting the centers.
- They're great the same day, and even chewier the next.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: overbaking.** An extra 3–4 minutes can make the difference between marzipan-chewy and jawbreaker. Trust the pale tops.
- **What else ruins it: wet dough.** If the batch does spread, it'll still taste great. Next time, hold back more of the egg white.
- **Storing them:** keep airtight at room temperature for up to 1 week, or freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw on the counter.
- **Leftover yolks:** save them in a small covered container in the fridge for up to 2 days. They're perfect for custard for a round of [bignè](bigne.md).
- **Orange version:** swap the lemon for orange zest, which plays nicely with the almond.
- **Pistachio tops:** press chopped pistachios onto the dough in place of a whole almond.
- **The history:** these belong to the family of Sicilian almond sweets known as *paste di mandorla*. Sicily's almond tradition is often traced to Arab rule on the island, and Palermo's convents later became famous for shaping almond paste into uncannily realistic little fruits.
