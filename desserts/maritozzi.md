# 🍞 Maritozzi
### Roman Sweet Buns Stuffed with Whipped Cream

*Makes 6 big buns. No mixer, and a slow overnight rise replaces most of the kneading. Day one is about 1½ hours, with 15 minutes of actual work. Day two is about 4 hours, with 30 minutes of actual work. Written for a first-time baker, so every step says what it should look like.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Orange (1), for zest

**Dairy**
- Whole milk (½ cup + 1 tbsp)
- Large egg (1)
- Unsalted butter (4 tbsp / ½ stick / 57 g)
- **Heavy whipping cream (1½ cups).** Buy a 16 oz carton, since the 8 oz size is too small. Half-and-half and light cream won't whip

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- **Bread flour (2½ cups / 300 g).** It makes the buns stretchier and taller. All-purpose works too if you don't want to buy a whole bag, and the buns will be a little softer and flatter
- Instant yeast (1½ tsp). One ¼ oz packet is enough. If the label says "active dry," it'll still work: stir it into the warm milk and wait 5 minutes until foamy
- Honey (1 tbsp)
- Powdered sugar (3 tbsp for the cream, plus more for dusting)
- Vanilla extract (2 tsp)
- Parchment paper and plastic wrap

**Gear worth checking**
- **Kitchen scale.** Especially useful here, because bread dough is sensitive to how much flour goes in
- Big mixing bowl, whisk, sheet pan, instant-read thermometer

*You should have at home: kosher salt, granulated sugar, neutral oil*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The dough*
- ½ cup + 1 tbsp (137 g) whole milk
- ¼ cup (50 g) granulated sugar
- 1½ tsp (5 g) instant yeast
- 1 large egg
- 1 tbsp honey
- Zest of 1 orange
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 2½ cups (300 g) bread flour
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 4 tbsp (57 g) unsalted butter, very soft

*The glaze*
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar
- 2 tbsp water

*The filling*
- 1½ cups cold heavy whipping cream
- 3 tbsp powdered sugar, plus more for dusting
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

---

## Day One

### 1️⃣ Mix the dough · ⏱ 5 min, plus 20 min rest
- Leave the **4 tbsp butter** out on the counter. It needs to be soft enough to squish easily.
- Microwave the **½ cup + 1 tbsp milk** for about 30 seconds, until barely warm. 🌡️ Aim for around **100°F**, because much hotter than 110°F starts to harm the yeast.
- In a big bowl, whisk together:
  - **The warm milk**
  - **¼ cup (50 g) granulated sugar**
  - **1½ tsp instant yeast**
  - **1 large egg**
  - **1 tbsp honey**
  - **Zest of 1 orange**
  - **1 tsp vanilla extract**
- Add **2½ cups (300 g) bread flour** and **1 tsp kosher salt**, then stir with a spoon or your hand until no dry flour is left. It'll look rough and shaggy.
- Cover the bowl and rest it for **20 minutes**. The flour soaks up the liquid during this break and starts building gluten on its own, which is free kneading.

### 2️⃣ Work in the butter · ⏱ 5 min, plus 10 min rest
- Add the **4 tbsp soft butter** in small pieces and squish it into the dough with your hand.
- The dough will turn greasy, slippery, and shredded, and it will seem like it can't possibly come together. Keep squeezing and folding for about **5 minutes** until the butter disappears. It does come back.
- ⚠️ **Don't add more flour, even if it's sticky.** Extra flour is what turns soft buns into dense ones. Rub a little oil on your hands instead.
- Cover and rest for **10 minutes**.

### 3️⃣ Fold three times · ⏱ 1 hour, mostly waiting
- With lightly oiled hands, grab one edge of the dough, stretch it up until it resists, and fold it over the middle. Turn the bowl a quarter turn and repeat, going all the way around 4 times. Each of these rounds takes about 30 seconds.
- Cover and wait **20 minutes**, then repeat. Do **3 rounds total**, with 20 minutes between them. Each round builds strength with almost no effort, standing in for the kneading a mixer would do.
- By the last round the dough should feel noticeably smoother and stretchier, and hold together in a soft ball.

### 4️⃣ Chill overnight · ⏱ 8–18 hours
- Cover the bowl tightly and put it in the fridge. The slow, cold rise builds a much better flavor, and it firms up the butter, which makes sticky dough far easier to shape.
- It should grow noticeably by morning, though it doesn't need to fully double.

---

## Day Two

### 5️⃣ Shape the buns · ⏱ 10 min
- Line a sheet pan with parchment.
- Turn the cold dough onto a very lightly floured counter and divide it into **6 equal pieces**, about **100 g each**.
- For each piece, pull the edges into the center to make a rough ball, then flip it seam-side down. Cup your hand around it and roll it in small circles on the counter until the top is smooth and tight.
- Rest the balls for **5 minutes** so the dough relaxes, then roll each one back and forth under your palms into a **4-inch oval** with slightly tapered ends.
- Space them **3 inches apart** on the pan, seam-side down.

### 6️⃣ Let them rise · ⏱ 1½–2½ hours
- Cover loosely with lightly oiled plastic wrap and leave them somewhere around 70–75°F. Cold dough needs a while to wake up, so go by how it looks rather than the clock.
- If your kitchen is chilly, put the pan in the turned-off oven with just the oven light on.
- They're ready when they look puffy and nearly doubled. Poke one gently with a floured finger: if the dent fills back slowly and doesn't disappear entirely, they're ready.
- ⚠️ **If the dent springs right back, keep waiting.** Underproofed buns bake up dense and can split open. If the dent never fills back and the dough sags, bake them right away.
- ⚠️ **Take the pan out before you preheat** if it's been rising in the oven. It's easy to forget, and plastic wrap melts.

### 7️⃣ Bake and glaze · ⏱ 18–22 min
- Preheat the oven to **350°F** with a rack in the middle. Give it the full preheat, so the buns get a strong burst of rise as soon as they go in.
- Bake for **18–22 minutes**, until deep golden on top.
- 🌡️ **Pull at 190°F** in the center of a bun. Enriched doughs brown quickly, so color alone can fool you.
- While they bake, stir **2 tbsp granulated sugar** and **2 tbsp water** in a mug and microwave for about 30 seconds until the sugar dissolves.
- Brush the glaze over the buns as soon as they come out of the oven. It keeps the crust soft and gives them that bakery shine.

### 8️⃣ Cool completely · ⏱ at least 1 hour
- Move the buns to a rack or clean towel and let them cool completely.
- ⚠️ **Don't rush this.** Warm bread melts whipped cream, and a warm bun is also gummy inside when you cut it.

### 9️⃣ Whip, stuff, and devour · ⏱ 10 min
- Chill a mixing bowl and whisk for **10 minutes**, then combine in the bowl:
  - **1½ cups cold heavy whipping cream**
  - **3 tbsp powdered sugar**
  - **1 tsp vanilla extract**
- Whisk quickly in big circles for about **5 minutes**, until lifting the whisk leaves a peak that stands up almost straight. This filling needs to be firmer than a dollop, because it has to stay put inside the bun.
- ⚠️ **Stop the moment it holds firm.** If it starts looking rough and grainy, gently stir in **1–2 tbsp more cold cream** to smooth it out.
- Cut a deep slit along the top of each bun with a serrated knife, stopping before you get through the bottom so it opens like a hinge.
- Gently pry each bun open and pile in about **½ cup cream**. Then drag a butter knife across the opening to smooth it level with the bun, which is the signature Roman look.
- Dust with **powdered sugar** and eat them the same day.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: rushing.** Cold, under-risen dough or a still-warm bun causes most of the problems here. All the fixes are just more waiting.
- **Same-day version:** after the folds, let the dough rise at room temperature for **1–1½ hours**, until doubled, then shape and continue. Sticky dough will be harder to handle, and the flavor is a little milder. Plan on about 5 hours total.
- **Don't want all 6 at once?** Keep unfilled buns airtight for 1 day, or freeze them for up to 1 month. Thaw at room temperature and fill just before eating.
- **A little flavor twist:** add the zest of a lemon alongside the orange, or fold ¼ cup raisins into the dough during the final round of folds. Raisins and candied peel show up in the older Lenten versions of these buns.
- **Dough rising slowly?** Check the yeast's expiration date, and move it somewhere warmer. Sweet, buttery doughs always rise more slowly than plain bread.
- **The name:** *maritozzo* comes from *marito*, or husband. The story goes that on the first Friday of March, Roman suitors gave these to their sweethearts, sometimes with a ring or small gift tucked inside. In Rome they're a breakfast food, usually with a cappuccino, which is a very good way to start a day.
