# 🍲 Pasta Fazool
### Instant Pot Pasta Fagioli

*One Instant Pot, ~1 hour including prep and pressure time, makes a big pot (roughly 6 generous servings): eat half this week, freeze half for a future dinner. Ham and pepperoni render into an orange, smoky base, three kinds of beans make it hearty, and the pasta gets cooked separately so the soup stays soup.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- 1 yellow onion
- Baby carrots (2 cups, about half a 1 lb bag)
- Celery (6 stalks, about one bunch)
  - *Shortcut: a tub of pre-diced mirepoix instead of chopping onion, carrot, and celery*

**Meat / Deli**
- Ham steak (14 oz)
- Pepperoni (5 oz stick or bag)

**Dairy**
- Grated parmesan or pecorino (½ cup, plus more for serving)
  - *Topping swap: full-fat sour cream (one 8 oz tub) and a bunch of flat-leaf parsley instead. See Tips*

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Petite diced tomatoes (one 28 oz can)
- Chicken broth (one 32 oz carton)
- Chickpeas (one 15 oz can)
- Kidney beans (one 15 oz can)
- Cannellini beans (one 15 oz can)
- Ditalini pasta (one 1 lb box). Any tiny tube or shell works if they're out

*You should have at home: olive oil, garlic powder, dried basil, bay leaves, sea salt grinder, black pepper, hot sauce, two quart freezer containers (deli quarts work)*

## Recipe

**Ingredients**

*The base*
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 14 oz ham steak, cubed
- 5 oz pepperoni, cut into coins then quartered
- 1 yellow onion, diced

*The pot*
- 2 cups baby carrots, cut into coins
- 6 celery stalks, diced
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp dried basil
- ~10 cranks of the sea salt grinder, plus more to taste
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 dried bay leaves
- 1 carton (32 oz) chicken broth
- 1 can (28 oz) petite diced tomatoes, undrained
- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (15 oz) kidney beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (15 oz) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed

*To finish*
- ½ cup grated parmesan or pecorino
- Hot sauce, to taste
- Ditalini, cooked separately, only what you're eating that day

### 1️⃣ Prep everything into stage bowls · ⏱ ~20 min
- Do all the chopping before the pot turns on. Once Sauté starts, the fond can scorch in the time it takes to dice an onion. Group things by **when they hit the pot**, not by what they are, so each step is one dump.
- **Bowl 1, the meat:**
  - **14 oz ham steak**, cubed
  - **5 oz pepperoni**, cut into coins then quartered
- **Bowl 2, the onion:** the **1 yellow onion**, diced. It gets its own bowl because it goes in a step after the meat.
- **Bowl 3, the veg and spices:**
  - **2 cups baby carrots**, cut into coins
  - **6 celery stalks**, diced
  - **1 tsp garlic powder**, **1 tsp dried basil**, **~10 cranks of sea salt**, **½ tsp black pepper**, and **2 bay leaves**, sprinkled right on top. They all go in together, so there's nothing to measure later.
- **Broth:** the **32 oz carton of chicken broth**, opened and ready to pour. Keep it next to the pot, because it's also your emergency splash if the bottom starts going dark.
- **Colander:** open all three cans of beans (**chickpeas, kidney, cannellini**) and drain and rinse them together. They go in at the same moment, so one colander is all you need.
- **The tomatoes:** open the **28 oz can** and leave it on the counter. Don't drain it.
- **Using the mirepoix shortcut?** Dump the whole tub into Bowl 2 in place of the onion and skip the carrots and celery in Bowl 3, so Bowl 3 is just the spices. Giving the carrot and celery a few extra minutes of sauté does no harm.

### 2️⃣ Render the ham & pepperoni · ⏱ ~7 min
- Set the Instant Pot to Sauté on medium-high with the **2 tbsp olive oil**.
- Add the **14 oz cubed ham** and **5 oz quartered pepperoni** and cook until the fat renders and the pepperoni edges curl. The oil goes orange, and that orange oil is the flavor base of the whole soup.
- Stir every couple of minutes, and watch the **pot bottom**, not the meat. A light brown crust on the bottom is the goal.
- ⚠️ **Don't let the bottom scorch.** Overcooked meat isn't the risk here, black fond is: it turns the whole soup bitter. If it starts going dark, splash in some of the broth right away and scrape it up.

### 3️⃣ Soften the onion · ⏱ ~5 min
- Add the **diced yellow onion** and cook until translucent.
- Scrape the bottom clean as you go. The onion's moisture lifts the fond off the bottom and into the soup, where its flavor belongs.

### 4️⃣ Load the pot · ⏱ ~5 min
- Turn off Sauté.
- Add the vegetables and seasonings:
  - **2 cups carrot coins**
  - **6 diced celery stalks**
  - **1 tsp garlic powder**
  - **1 tsp dried basil**
  - **~10 cranks of sea salt**
  - **½ tsp black pepper**
  - **2 bay leaves**
- Pour in the **whole 32 oz carton of chicken broth**, the **28 oz can of tomatoes**, and all three cans of beans (**chickpeas, kidney, cannellini**, drained and rinsed), then stir everything together.
- ⚠️ **Check the fill line.** Soups shouldn't go past the ⅔ mark. If it's close, hold back a cup of broth and stir it in after cooking.

### 5️⃣ Pressure cook & cook the pasta · ⏱ 10 min + ~15 min to pressure and release
- Seal and cook on **High Pressure for 10 minutes**. The carrots set the timer, and the canned beans are already cooked, so they're just along for the ride.
- Fill the kettle, bring it to a boil, and set it to keep warm. The IP takes ~10 min to come up to pressure, so the water is ready long before you need it, and it gets the pasta pot boiling in a minute instead of ten.
- When the IP display reads **10 min** (it's at pressure and the countdown has started), put a pot on the stove over high, pour in the hot water, and salt it.
- When the IP reads **5 min**, add the **ditalini**, about **¼ cup dry per bowl**. The last 5 min of pressure plus the 5 min natural release gives the pasta ~10 min, so it finishes right when the soup does.
- When the IP beeps, let it release naturally for **5 minutes**, then vent the rest.
- ⚠️ **Don't quick-release right away.** A thick soup will sputter out through the valve.
- Drain the pasta, but don't rinse it. The soup comes out of the IP brothy, and the starch on the ditalini gives it body. Put the pasta back in its pot and toss it with a drizzle of **olive oil** so it doesn't stick together while it waits.
- Cook only what you're eating today. Pasta stored in the soup keeps drinking broth and goes gluey overnight.

### 6️⃣ Finish & serve
- Fish out the **2 bay leaves**. ⚠️ They stay stiff and sharp-edged even after cooking, and nobody wants one in a spoonful.
- Taste the soup and adjust the salt.
- Spoon pasta into each bowl and ladle the soup over it.
- Pass the **½ cup grated cheese** and the **hot sauce** at the table.

### 7️⃣ Freeze half · ⏱ ~5 min
- Right after dinner, ladle **half the soup (about 2 qt)** into **two quart containers**, with no pasta. Splitting it while it's still warm also means the half that stays in the pot cools faster.
- Leave **about 1 inch of headspace** in each container. Soup expands as it freezes, and a full container cracks or pops its lid.
- Let them cool on the counter with the lids off for up to an hour, then lid them, label them with the date, and put them in the freezer.
- ⚠️ **Don't leave them out longer than 2 hours.** A big mass of soup cools slowly, and the middle sits in the danger zone. If you're running late, the fridge first and the freezer tomorrow is fine.
- The other half goes in the fridge, in the inner pot or a container. Plan to eat it within **3–4 days**.

## Tips & Notes
- **Never cook the pasta in the soup, and never store it in the soup.** It swells and goes gluey, and in a pressure cooker it turns to mush.
- **All three cans of beans go in for the pressure cook.** Canned beans hold up fine for 10 minutes. No mush.
- **Fridge leftovers:** good for **3–4 days** from when you cooked it, and better on day two. You can reheat it more than once, as long as it gets back to a simmer every time, but the clock doesn't reset. Reheat in the inner pot on **Sauté → Less** with a **½–1 cup splash of broth or water**, stirring every few minutes, until it simmers (🌡️ 165°F). Then switch to Keep Warm to hold it. ⚠️ Keep Warm can't heat cold soup on its own. It's too gentle and leaves the soup in the danger zone for hours.
- **Reheating the fazool ice cube:** best within **3 months**.
  - Move it to the fridge **the night before**. A quart takes about a day to thaw, and half-thawed is fine.
  - Run the container under hot water for 30 seconds so the block slides out, and drop it into the inner pot with **½ cup water**.
  - Set **Sauté → Less**, cover it with any lid that fits loosely (not the pressure lid), and break up the block with a spoon as it softens. Once it's liquid, turn it up to Normal and bring it to a simmer (🌡️ 165°F). About 15 min if it was thawed, 30+ min if it's still frozen solid.
  - While it heats, cook fresh ditalini with the kettle trick from step 5, **¼ cup dry per bowl**.
  - ⚠️ **Don't pressure cook it to thaw it faster.** The carrots and beans get a second full cook and go mushy.
- **No Instant Pot? Use the 10-inch Dutch oven.** It holds about 5½ qt to the brim, so trim the batch to **4 celery stalks** and **3 cups broth** (hold back 1 cup from the carton). That leaves it about ¾ full, and the saved cup thins the leftovers. The steps change like this:
  - Render the meat on **medium**. A Dutch oven holds heat, so if the fond goes dark, splash in broth rather than waiting for the burner to cool.
  - After the onion, sweat the vegetables and spices for ~4 min so the dried spices toast in the orange fat.
  - Add the broth, tomatoes, and beans and stir everything together.
  - Bring it to a boil, then simmer at a **bare bubble with the lid ajar for ~20 min**, until the carrots are tender. Scrape the bottom every 5 min so settled beans don't scorch, and stop by 30 min before the beans split.
  - Use the same kettle trick for the pasta and drop it in for the last ~10 min of the simmer.
- **Topping swap: sour cream & parsley.** Instead of the parm, put **~2 tbsp full-fat sour cream** on each bowl and scatter a pinch of roughly chopped **flat-leaf parsley** over it. The tang cuts through the smoky ham and pepperoni, and the parsley adds something fresh. Pick one topping, not both, because the sour cream's tang drowns out the parm. Use full-fat: light sour cream can split into curds in hot soup. Set it out while the soup cooks so a cold dollop doesn't cool your bowl down.
- **Want it creamier?** Mash a ladle of the beans against the side of the pot after cooking and stir them back in. The starch thickens the broth.
- **The name:** *pasta e fagioli* just means "pasta and beans." In Neapolitan dialect, *fagioli* is *fasule*, which Italian-American kitchens turned into "fazool." Dean Martin made it famous in "That's Amore": *"When the stars make you drool just like pasta e fazool."*
