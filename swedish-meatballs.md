# 🇸🇪 Swedish Meatballs with Mashed Potatoes
*The IKEA plate at home: tender allspice meatballs in a silky cream gravy over buttery mash, with lingonberry on the side. 4 servings (~24 meatballs), ~1 hour*

---

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Yukon Gold potatoes (3 lb, usually one bag)
- Yellow onion (1 small)

**Meat**
- Ground beef, 85/15 (1½ lb). *Store-time pivot: 1 lb beef + ½ lb ground pork is the traditional Swedish blend and a bit more tender. All-beef is great too.*

**Dairy**
- Unsalted butter (9 tbsp total, a little over one stick)
- Whole milk (1¼ cup, so one quart is plenty)
- Heavy cream (½ cup, one half-pint)
- Eggs (1)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Low-sodium beef broth (2 cups, half of a 32 oz carton). *Get low-sodium because the soy sauce and pan drippings add plenty of salt.*
- Panko breadcrumbs (½ cup)
- Soy sauce
- Dijon mustard
- Worcestershire sauce
- Ground allspice
- Ground nutmeg
- Lingonberry jam. *Look near the jams or in the international aisle, or grab it at IKEA's Swedish Food Market or World Market. **Swap:** whole-berry cranberry sauce, which is in season right now and has the same tart-sweet job.*

*You should have at home: kosher salt, black pepper, all-purpose flour, neutral oil*

---

## Recipe (4 servings)

**Ingredients**

*Meatballs*
- 1½ lb ground beef (or 1 lb beef + ½ lb pork)
- ½ cup panko
- ½ cup whole milk
- 1 egg
- ½ small yellow onion, grated
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ½ tsp ground allspice
- ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
- 2 tbsp butter + 1 tbsp neutral oil, for searing

*Gravy*
- 3 tbsp butter
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 2 cups low-sodium beef broth
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- ½ tsp Worcestershire sauce
- ½ cup heavy cream
- Black pepper to taste

*Mashed potatoes*
- 3 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into 2-inch chunks
- 4 tbsp butter
- ¾ cup whole milk, warmed
- 1½ tsp kosher salt + more for the water

*To serve*
- Lingonberry jam (or whole-berry cranberry sauce)

**Steps**

1. **Make the panade (5 min).** In a big bowl, stir the **½ cup panko** into the **½ cup milk** and let it sit while you grate the onion. The soaked crumbs sit between the meat proteins and keep them from squeezing together tightly as they cook. That's what makes Swedish meatballs soft and bouncy, not dense.

2. **Mix the meatballs (5 min).** Grate **½ small onion** straight into the bowl on the small holes of a box grater. Grating makes a pulp that melts into the meat, so you don't get crunchy onion bits. Add the **1 egg**, **1 tsp salt**, **½ tsp pepper**, **½ tsp allspice**, and **¼ tsp nutmeg** and stir to combine, then add the **1½ lb beef**. Mix with your hands **only until it just comes together.** Overmixing is the #1 way to get tough, rubbery meatballs. The mixture will feel wet and sticky, and that's correct.

3. **Roll and chill (10 min).** Wet your hands and roll 1¼-inch balls (about a heaping tablespoon each, ~24 total) onto a plate or sheet pan. Pop them in the fridge while you start the potatoes. Chilled meatballs hold their shape much better in the pan.

4. **Start the potatoes (25 min, mostly hands-off).** Put the **3 lb potato chunks** in a pot, cover with cold water by an inch, and salt it well. Bring to a boil, then simmer until a fork slides through with no resistance, about 15-20 min. Starting in cold water cooks them evenly. Dropping them into boiling water gives you mushy outsides around raw middles.

5. **Sear the meatballs (10 min).** While the potatoes cook, heat **2 tbsp butter + 1 tbsp oil** in a large skillet over medium-high. Sear the meatballs in two batches, turning them, until browned on most sides, 4-5 min per batch. They don't need to be cooked through yet because they finish in the gravy. Set them aside on a plate and **don't clean the pan.** The brown bits stuck to it are the flavor base of the gravy.

6. **Make the roux (2-3 min).** Lower the heat to medium. Melt the **3 tbsp butter** in the same skillet, then whisk in the **3 tbsp flour**. Keep whisking for 2 minutes until it smells toasty and turns peanut-butter tan. Cooking the flour gets rid of the raw pasty taste and adds the nutty depth that makes this gravy taste like gravy, not white sauce.

7. **Build the gravy (5 min).** Slowly pour in the **2 cups broth** while whisking constantly, scraping up the brown bits as you go. Adding it gradually is how you avoid lumps. If some show up anyway, keep whisking and they'll smooth out as it simmers. Whisk in the **1 tbsp soy sauce**, **1 tsp Dijon**, and **½ tsp Worcestershire** and simmer until it coats a spoon, 3-4 min. It's an unusual trio for Swedish food, but it adds the savory depth you'd get from a long-simmered stock.

8. **Add the cream and finish the meatballs (8-10 min).** Stir in the **½ cup heavy cream**, return the meatballs and any juices from the plate, and simmer gently, turning them now and then, until cooked through (160°F in the center, or no pink when you cut one open). Keep it at a lazy bubble, not a hard boil. Taste and add pepper. Hold off on salt until you've tasted, because it's usually already there.

9. **Mash the potatoes (5 min).** Drain the potatoes well and let them steam in the colander for a minute or two so the extra water evaporates. Return them to the warm pot and mash with the **4 tbsp butter** first, then add the **¾ cup warm milk** and **1½ tsp salt**. Butter goes in first so the fat coats the starch before the milk does, which gives you a creamier, less gluey mash. Use a masher or ricer, **never a blender or food processor.** Those burst the starch cells and turn the potatoes into glue.

10. **Plate it IKEA-style.** Put a big scoop of mash on the plate, add meatballs, pour gravy over everything, and add a spoonful of **lingonberry jam** on the side. Get a little of everything in each bite. The tart jam against the rich gravy is what makes this dish.

## Tips & Notes
- **Timing cheat sheet:** get the potatoes boiling before you start searing. If the gravy is done first, it happily holds on low. If the mash is done first, cover the pot and it stays hot for 15+ min.
- **Gravy too thick?** Splash in more broth or cream. **Too thin?** Simmer a few more minutes, since it thickens as it reduces and again as it cools.
- **Pickled cucumbers are the classic third side.** *Pressgurka* is just thinly sliced cucumber in 2 parts water, 1 part white vinegar, sugar, and salt for 20 min. The bright crunch cuts through all the richness. If you want it, add a cucumber and white vinegar to your shopping list.
- **Leftovers reheat beautifully.** Store the meatballs in their gravy, then reheat gently with a splash of broth or milk because the gravy tightens in the fridge. Reheat the mash with a little milk too.
- **Freezer-friendly:** Roll a double batch and freeze the extra meatballs raw on a sheet pan, then move them to a bag. Sear them straight from frozen, adding a couple extra minutes.
- **Fun history:** Sweden's meatballs, *köttbullar*, may not be Swedish at all. In 2018, Sweden's official Twitter account said King Charles XII brought the recipe back from the Ottoman Empire in the early 1700s, inspired by Turkish *köfte*. Food historians still debate that. IKEA started selling them in 1985 and reportedly goes through about a billion a year.
