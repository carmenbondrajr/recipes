# 🥦 Lazy Orzo
### Chicken, Broccoli & Orzo Bake

*One 9x13 pan, serves 4–6. Dry orzo goes in raw and cooks in the broth — no separate pasta pot.*

## Shopping List

**Produce**
- Broccoli (2 crowns / ~6 cups florets) — *or one 12 oz bag frozen florets, no thawing needed*
- Lemon
- Yellow onion
- Fresh parsley (optional, for finishing)

**Meat**
- Boneless skinless chicken thighs (1.5 lb) — *breasts work, but thighs won't dry out during the bake*

**Dairy**
- Heavy cream (1 cup / 8 oz)
- Sharp cheddar, block (8 oz)
- Parmesan, block (~2 oz / 1 cup grated)

**Pantry / Center Aisles**
- Orzo (1.5 cups dry / ~10 oz — a 1 lb box covers it)
- Chicken broth (3 cups / 24 oz — one 32 oz carton)
- Minced garlic (jar)

*You should have at home: olive oil, butter, salt, pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, Italian seasoning, paprika, red pepper flakes*

---

## Recipe

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch pieces
- 1.5 cups dry orzo
- 3 cups chicken broth, hot
- 1 cup heavy cream
- ~6 cups broccoli florets, cut small (bite-size — big trees won't cook through)
- ½ yellow onion, diced
- 3 tsp minced garlic
- 2 tbsp olive oil + 2 tbsp butter
- 8 oz sharp cheddar, grated (~2 cups)
- 1 cup grated parmesan
- 1 lemon (zest + juice)
- Salt, pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, Italian seasoning, paprika
- Pinch red pepper flakes

**Steps**

1. **Preheat.** 400°F. Butter a 9x13 pan.

2. **Season and sear the chicken** (~8 min). Toss the **1.5 lb chicken pieces** with **2 tbsp olive oil**, 1 tsp salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika, and Italian seasoning. Sear in a hot skillet in a single layer, 2–3 min per side — you want color, not doneness. It finishes in the oven, so pulling it at ~80% is correct. Set aside.

3. **Build the base** (~5 min). Same skillet, melt the **2 tbsp butter**, soften the **½ diced onion** 3 min, then add the **3 tsp garlic** and red pepper flakes for 30 seconds. Add the **3 cups hot broth** and **1 cup cream** and bring to a bare simmer. Season with ½ tsp salt — **taste it now.** Once the orzo is in you can't fix under-seasoned liquid, because the pasta drinks it all.

4. **Assemble** (~3 min). Scatter the **1.5 cups dry orzo** evenly across the 9x13. Pour the hot broth-cream mixture over it and stir to level — even depth means even cooking. Tuck the seared chicken and any resting juices in on top.

5. **First bake, covered.** Foil tightly, 20 minutes. The seal is what cooks the orzo — steam is doing the work, so don't peek.

6. **Add the broccoli** (~2 min). Uncover, stir the orzo up from the bottom (it sticks and cooks unevenly if you skip this), then fold the **~6 cups broccoli florets** in. Re-cover and bake 12 more minutes. Broccoli goes in late on purpose: 30+ minutes in the oven turns it olive-drab and sulfurous.

7. **Cheese and finish** (~8 min). Uncover, stir in half the cheese (**1 cup cheddar + ½ cup parm**), scatter the rest (**1 cup cheddar + ½ cup parm**) over the top. Bake uncovered 8–10 min until the top browns in spots and the edges bubble. Broil 1–2 min if you want it darker — watch it the whole time.

8. **Rest 10 minutes.** It'll look a touch loose out of the oven. It sets as it sits.

9. **Finish bright.** Zest and juice of the **1 lemon** over the whole pan, plus parsley. The dish is rich and flat without acid — this step isn't optional.

## Tips & Notes
- **What ruins this: liquid ratio.** 1.5 cups orzo to 4 cups total liquid is the number. Soupy at the end means the foil leaked steam; crunchy means it was loose. Either way, add ¼ cup hot broth or bake uncovered a few more minutes — it's forgiving if you catch it.
- **Cut the broccoli small.** Bite-size florets cook in the 12 minutes they get. Big ones come out raw in the middle while everything else is done.
- **Frozen broccoli works** — add it straight from the bag at step 6, no thawing, but shake off ice crystals so you're not adding water to the pan.
- **Grate your own cheese.** Pre-shredded is coated in anti-caking starch that turns the sauce grainy instead of glossy.
- **Rotisserie chicken shortcut:** skip steps 1–2, use ~3 cups shredded meat, and fold it in at step 6 with the broccoli so it doesn't dry out.
- **Leftovers** reheat best with a splash of broth or milk stirred in — orzo keeps drinking liquid overnight.
- **Orzo is pasta, not rice** — the name means "barley" in Italian, though it's made from semolina wheat like any other dry pasta. It's called *risoni* ("big rice") elsewhere in Italy, which is the more honest name.
